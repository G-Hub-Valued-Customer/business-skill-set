POS Gap Scorecard
=================

Purpose: Builds a POS presence scorecard from <company>'s <shelf recognition model> without opening
a single store photo, and delivers a color-coded Excel workbook: green check where POS is present,
red X for a real actionable gap, yellow where the store could not be assessed, with failure codes
in the row margin explaining every miss.

Triggers: "what POS is missing in my market"; "where do I stand in territory <territory id> with
<business unit 4> POS"; "POS gap report"; "which stores are missing <business unit 3> signage";
"who is missing <sign type 2> signage"; or the user names a <business unit> or a sign type.

Inputs:
- The <shelf recognition model> in Power BI: per-store, per-cycle recognized POS flags, photo scene
  quality flags and pre-built visibility measures.
- The <sign type 1> state guides (one PDF per state plus a national companion set) in the live
  space folder of the <sales org> planning site on <company>'s SharePoint.
- The current half's <incentive program> details document, found through enterprise search.
- The current <monthly POS kit> folder on the same planning site.
- <field initiative guides>, <trade announcements> and memos on the <sales org> communications
  site.
- The signed-in user's profile and the model's user and hierarchy tables (role resolution).

Outputs:
- One Excel workbook "POS Gap Scorecard - <scope> - <cycle>.xlsx" with Scorecard, Summary and
  Legend sheets, saved to the session's output folder.
- A five-line chat report that states completeness explicitly.

Parameters to Fill In:
- <company> - the company whose SharePoint sites and Power BI workspace hold every source.
- <sales org> - the field sales organization whose planning and communications sites hold the
  guides, kits and announcements.
- <business unit 1> to <business unit 5> - the operating units whose POS and inventory measures
  are scored (<category 1>, <category 2>, <category 3>, <category 4> and <category 5> in turn).
- <business unit> - the generic word for one operating unit.
- <our brand 1A> - the flagship <category 1> brand whose header and loyalty flip sign are scored.
- <price family A> - the <our brand 1A> family whose flip sign is the state-value measure and
  whose store type drives flip-sign scoping.
- <price family B> - the <our brand 1A> line launched in state waves whose flip sign has no
  recognition column.
- <our brand 2A> - the leading <category 2> brand named in a paused recognition measure.
- <our brand 2C>, <our brand 2D> - the two <category 2> brands a <territory role> elects between
  for targeted signage.
- <our brand 3A> - the <business unit 3> <category 3> brand whose POS is scored.
- <our brand 4A> - the <business unit 4> <category 4> brand, with a standard line and a premium
  line.
- <variant> - a product variant named in a per-measure reason string.
- <loyalty program> - the <business unit 2> loyalty program whose on-shelf availability is
  measured.
- <promotional price program> - the promotional price program on the <business unit 3> priority
  page.
- <shelf recognition model> - the Power BI model of photo recognition results; <report id> is its
  report GUID.
- <photo platform> - the vendor platform that captures and recognizes store photos; its image

- <CRM> - the field CRM where store type and fixture facts are verified.
- <fixture data source> - the system that records fixture width.

  state guide" and its SharePoint folders are the live space and prep space.

- <sign type 3> - the age-restricted scan-check signage program.
- <sign type 4> - a further signage program with its own national companion guide.
- <sign type 5> - the retired POS measure for the <our brand 4A> premium line.
- <monthly POS kit> - the monthly kit folder that outranks every other source.
- <incentive program> - the sales incentive program whose details document defines metrics,
  weights, exclusions and start dates.
- <field initiative guide> - a dated, doc-numbered initiative guide from the field sales force.

Skill Instructions
------------------

The skill turns <photo platform> image-recognition results into a rep-usable Excel scorecard that
shows exactly which POS is missing, where, and why, without ever opening a photo.

When Not to Use:

- Editing, reformatting or charting an existing workbook: the xlsx skill.
- Ranking or prioritizing stores to visit: Top Opportunity Stores.
- Contract level, merchandising footage or funding math: out of scope. This skill scores POS
  signage presence only, not contract compliance.
- Reading or interpreting store photos directly: Contract Compliance Audit (<business unit 4> and
  <business unit 3>), which fetches the <photo platform> photos through the photo bridge flow.
  This skill reads recognition results instead.

Critical Context:

The assistant never attempts to fetch a <photo platform> photo from this skill. The image links in
the <planning workbook> and in the model (<scene image link column>, <image link column>,
<merchandising space image column>) point at the <photo platform> API endpoint, which Cowork's file
tools cannot reach. The recognition results are available without the images and are the faster
source for a presence check. If the user wants the photos themselves read (contract footage,
facings, header position), that is the contract audit skill, which brings the photos into OneDrive
through the photo bridge flow; the assistant says so and still delivers the scorecard.

Workflow:

Step 1 - Resolve Scope:

1. The assistant reads the signed-in user's home state and login from the profile, then resolves
   role and span from the <shelf recognition model>'s user table, never from the directory title
   alone. The full "who is asking and what they own" procedure (alias from the login, lookup in
   the model's user table with the sales-geography fallback, the class table, the hierarchy table
   as store list, explicit scope override, row-level security and run sizing) is the Hub
   section's. Differences for this skill: the home state is read with the login because Step 2
   needs it to pick the state guide; above one market the assistant states the store count,
   proposes to run market by market, and runs the whole span only when the user says so.
2. Default scope by role: a <territory role> gets their territory; a <market support role> gets
   their whole market, the same report a <market role> gets (they work across the market and need
   to see what leadership sees before being told where to go, so they are never scoped down to
   one territory by default); a <market role> gets their market; a <section role> their section
   as one workbook per market or one tab per market; a <region role> their region the same way;
   <account role>s their chains; an <HQ role> names the scope. The assistant says which scope was
   used. An explicit scope in the request always wins ("territory <territory id>", "<market>",
   "<section id>", a chain).
3. The measure set follows the phrasing: a broad ask ("what POS is missing") takes every measure
   the <incentive program> documents score; a narrow ask ("where do I stand with
   <business unit 4> POS") takes that unit's columns only, plus trend.

Row-level security is built into the model, so results are already limited to the user's stores;
the assistant adds no person filter.

Step 2 - Read What Is Actually Being Scored (Mandatory - Never Skip):

The scorecard is never run off a hardcoded measure list. Scored POS changes every cycle and by
state; a stale list reports a retired measure as a territory-wide failure and misses a live one.
The sources below are read first, every run.

Step 2 - The <sign type 1> Live Space - the Authoritative Per-State Source:

This is the only correct <sign type 1> location; no other copy is ever read. It is the folder
"<sign type 1> live space - <monthly POS kit> planning" in the shared documents library of the
<sales org> planning site on <company>'s SharePoint. Each state guide is read by its file URL: one
PDF per state (all 50 plus the capital district), named by the two-letter state code, refreshed
every cycle. A numbered national companion set sits beside them: the national <sign type 1>
guide; the <sales org> POS guide every state guide cites for the complete POS listing; the
flavor-ban guide (geography variance that changes <business unit 4> and <business unit 2> SKU
minimums); the <sign type 4> signage guide; the <business unit 5> execution guide (merchandising
by contract level); the <business unit 1> resource guide; the <business unit 1> header
configuration guide (by store type); the <business unit 4> interim execution guide; and the
<business unit 2> look book (store-type signage).

A sibling prep space folder stages the next cycle and is unreleased; it is never read for a live
run. Stale mirrors exist (a months-old copy of one state's guide sits in a miscellaneous folder of
the <sales org> communications site); any <sign type 1> guide surfaced outside the live space is
discarded.

The assistant parses the scored-focus block on the state guide's cover page. The guide separates
what the rep is scored on (the scored-focus block) from what the rep is executing (the
current-initiative block); the measure set is built from the scored-focus block, per state, per
cycle. Scoring is never inferred from the initiative block: in one <jurisdiction> cycle the scored
focus was "<sign type 2> or <sign type 3> POS" while the initiative was narrower (<sign type 3> in
stores with age-verification scanning, <sign type 2> elsewhere); scoring the narrower rule would
create false misses.

Also read from the state guide: the <business unit 1> store type categorization (which flip signs
apply, and the "<price family A> with <price family B>" versus "without <price family B>" split),
the <business unit 1> current initiatives, the <business unit 2> store type and cluster page, and
the <business unit 3> prioritization page (a ranked list of that unit's programs for the cycle,
from a contracted-stores-only initiative down to the <promotional price program>).

Cover date: each guide's cover carries a month and a dated confidentiality line. The assistant
confirms it matches the cycle being scored and otherwise warns on the Summary sheet rather than
trusting the guide silently.

Multi-state scopes: a market can span several states. The assistant resolves the distinct
<store state column> values in scope, reads every state guide present and scores each store against its
own state's rules. One state's measure set is never applied to another's stores.

PDF parsing: the guides are linearized PDF 1.7 and the pdfplumber library fails on them with a
missing-root-object error; the pypdf library reads them, with pdfplumber kept as a fallback for
other documents.

Step 2 - The Other Three Sources:

2. The <incentive program> details document for the current half, found through enterprise search
   by title. Authoritative on metric definitions, weights, measurement level and exclusions,
   including the <business unit 1> rule that stores with a fixture narrower than
   <minimum fixture width> (per the <fixture data source>) and fewer than <minimum shelf count> shelves (per the
   <photo platform> photo) are excluded from scoring.
3. The current <monthly POS kit>, in a dated folder of the planning site. The most recent source;
   it outranks the others on anything it addresses, including mid-cycle pauses (for example a
   top-independent <our brand 2A> recognition measure paused for a cycle).
4. <field initiative guides>, <trade announcements> and memos, filed by year and <business unit>
   on the <sales org> communications site. Dated and doc-numbered; authoritative on per-state
   waves, launches and postponements (for example a <price family B> launch in a first wave of
   states including <jurisdiction>, with a second wave postponed elsewhere and the first wave
   unaffected).

Precedence when sources disagree: <monthly POS kit>, then the state <sign type 1> guide, then the
<field initiative guides>, then the <incentive program> details. Most recent and most specific
wins.

From these the assistant builds the run's measure list and states it to the user before querying:
which measures are in scope this cycle for this state, and which model column backs each one.

Retire what the guides no longer score: any measure absent from the current <sign type 1> guide
and <incentive program> deck is dropped, not shown as failing. A model column with no counterpart
in the current guides is labeled RETIRED and excluded, never reported as a gap. Known example: the
<sign type 5> column is retired; the <our brand 4A> premium line is now scored on dual-offer
signage (single-can and roll pricing POS). A run that reports <sign type 5> as missing has read a
stale list and skipped this step.

If a state guide cannot be found in the live space, the assistant says so plainly, names the
measure list it fell back to, and flags the whole run "measure set unconfirmed". It never silently
proceeds on assumption.

Step 3 - Query the <shelf recognition model>:

The assistant invokes the Power BI skill, then finds the artifact. The artifact discovery tool does
not index this model, so the assistant uses enterprise search restricted to Power BI sources with
the model's name. The result is a workspace-app URL that the report-id resolver rejects as not
found; the assistant passes the <report id> taken from that URL straight to the report metadata,
schema and query tools, which works.

Core tables: <recognized POS fact table> (per-store, per-cycle POS flags plus store attributes,
about 150 columns); <photo scene fact table> (per-visit photo scenes plus image-quality flags);
<POS visibility measure table> (several hundred pre-built measures, including eligibility and
"missing" counts).

Column map for the current half's scored measures (legend: measure: backing column; eligibility):

- <business unit 1> state-value flip sign: the <price family A> flip sign flag; the
  <price family A> target store flag equals 1.
- <business unit 1> <our brand 1A> header: the <our brand 1A> header flag; fixture stores, with the
  <business unit 1> any-header flag as fallback.
- <business unit 1> <price family B> flip sign: no recognition column exists because the
  <photo platform> cannot see it, so the cell is grey "N/S - not recognizable by <photo platform>; verify
  in <CRM> or the <fixture data source>"; store type "<price family A> with <price family B>",
  live in first-wave states including <jurisdiction>.
- <business unit 1> loyalty flip sign: the loyalty flip sign flag; the <business unit 1> loyalty
  election flag; grey state-wide where loyalty programs are unlawful.
- <business unit 2> POS by store type: the <business unit 2> any-sign flag and flip sign brand POS
  flag; store type from <CRM>.
- <business unit 3> any current <our brand 3A> POS: the <business unit 3> any-POS flag; the
  <business unit 3> eligible-stores measure.
- <business unit 4> <our brand 4A> standard and premium lines: the <business unit 4> any-POS flag;
  all stores; <required sign count> signs, with a lower count in the top <business unit 4>
  contract tier.
- Age-restricted access prevention, <sign type 2> or <sign type 3>: the combined "<sign type 3> or
  <sign type 2> signage" flag; all stores; one combined measure, never two.

The assistant never invents a column mapping. A scored measure with no confirmed recognition
column is grey "N/S - no recognition column mapped", the report says so, and the user is asked. A
mismapped column silently scores the wrong sign.

Scope columns: <territory column>, <market column>, <section column>, <region column>,
<store id column>, <store name column>, <store address column>, <store city column>, <store state column>,
<chain store flag>, <chain name column>, <latest visit date column>, <cycle column>,
<visit count column>. The model carries two territory columns whose row counts differ slightly; the assistant
filters on <territory column>, never the alternate. It pulls the latest cycle by default (the
model's latest-cycle measures) and captures the model's last refresh timestamp for the workbook
footer.

Step 3b - Completeness Architecture (Identical for <territory role> and <market role>):

A <market role> must be able to start this, walk away and come back to a report that is 100%
complete. It need not be fast; it must be perfect. A <territory role> runs one territory (a
hundred-plus stores); a <market role> or <market support role> runs a whole market (around a dozen
territories, well over a thousand stores). Same steps, same checks, same report, just longer. The
market-scope path is never shortened, sampled or simplified, and a <market support role> never
gets a reduced version of it.

1. Build the territory worklist first. The assistant queries the distinct count of stores per
   territory across the scope and persists it as a manifest (territory to expected store count)
   in the session's working scorecard folder. Every later check reconciles against it; a run that
   cannot state its expected total up front cannot verify itself.
2. One territory is one unit of work. A market is never queried in one shot: the Power BI query
   tool caps at 1,000 rows and truncates silently, dropping whole territories without error. For
   a market run the assistant dispatches territories to parallel background subagents, at most 3
   to 4 in flight. Each subagent is stateless and receives the territory id, the Step 2 measure
   list, the model id, the cycle and its own output path; each writes one JSON file per territory
   to the working scorecard folder and returns a row count.
3. The empty-result rule: wait before reacting. An empty or short result is far more often a cold
   model, a throttle or a slow render than a true zero. On any empty or below-expected result the
   assistant waits 30 seconds and re-runs the identical query; if the second attempt matches the
   first, it waits 30 seconds and tries once more (three attempts total) before concluding the
   result is genuinely empty. Only after three consistent attempts may a territory be recorded as
   verified empty, a label it carries into the final report. A first empty response is never
   treated as fact, and a query that has not been retried is never "fixed": rewriting DAX against
   a throttle produces a wrong query that returns wrong data confidently.
4. Per-territory acceptance gate. A territory is complete only when its returned store count
   equals its manifest count. On a shortfall: retry it under the 30-second rule, then split it
   into two halves by store number and retry each. Each territory is recorded as complete,
   partial (n of m) or verified empty, never an unqualified success.
5. Checkpoint after every territory. Results land on disk as each territory finishes; an
   interrupted run resumes by reading the working scorecard folder and skipping territories
   already complete. A market's results are never held only in context.
6. Reconciliation gate, the hard stop before the workbook. The Excel file is not built until every
   territory in the manifest has a terminal status, the sum of retrieved stores equals the
   manifest total, and every measure column has at least one non-null value across the scope (an
   all-null column means a broken mapping, not a territory-wide failure: stop and re-map rather
   than ship a wall of grey). If any check still fails after retries, the workbook is produced
   anyway but stamped INCOMPLETE on the Summary sheet with the exact territories and counts
   missing. A silently short report is the one failure mode this skill must never have.
7. Post-build verification. After saving, the assistant reopens the workbook, confirms the data
   row count equals the manifest total and the sheet has the expected column count, reports both
   numbers to the user, and only then calls the run finished.

Timeouts: slow is acceptable, incomplete is not. A territory is never abandoned for taking time;
it is retried under the 30-second rule. The store set is never reduced, measures never dropped and
cycle depth never lowered to finish faster.

Step 4 - Classify Every Cell:

A blank check mark is not one thing. Exactly one code is assigned per store and measure (legend:
code: cell appearance; meaning; basis):

- PASS: green check mark; POS recognized present; flag = 1.
- MISS: red X; eligible, photo usable, POS absent, the work list; flag = 0, eligible, no quality
  issue.
- IQI: yellow exclamation mark; photo unusable, a retake rather than a merchandising gap; any
  image-quality flag on the scene (bad angle, reflection, obstacle, too far, blurry, limited
  resolution, low accuracy, suspected fake, nothing to tag).
- NOPH: yellow exclamation mark; no photo captured this cycle; no scene row for the cycle.
- STALE: yellow exclamation mark; last photo predates the current cycle;
  <latest visit date column> before cycle start.
- N/S: grey dash; not scored (targeting, no election or program, legally unavailable in the
  state, fixture too small, paused, or not recognizable by the <photo platform>); eligibility
  measure = 0, <sign type 1> store-type scoping, state law, the <business unit 1> fixture
  exclusion, a <monthly POS kit> pause, or no recognition column.
- RETIRED: no cell; no longer scored this cycle, so the column is dropped entirely; absent from
  the <sign type 1> guide and <incentive program> deck.

Grey and yellow are different answers and never share a color. Grey means not scored: nothing was
expected of the store and nothing counts against it (targeted measures, missing elections and
state-law exclusions all land here). Yellow means the store should have been scored but could not
be assessed: a photo problem, someone needs to re-shoot. Collapsing the two makes a rep chase
stores that were never on the hook.

State law can make a measure permanently N/S. Loyalty programs are not lawful in every state; in a
<jurisdiction> with no loyalty, the <our brand 1A> loyalty flip sign and every loyalty-dependent
measure is grey for the entire state, not a gap and not a data problem. The <sign type 1> guide's
state pages supply these exclusions, applied before scoring. A legally unavailable measure is
never reported as a miss.

The specific grey reason is always written into the cell's reason string, because the reasons
carry different (or no) actions:

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| N/S - not targeted | Measure applies to assigned stores only |
| N/S - no program/election on file | Store not contracted or not elected in |
| N/S - not lawful in <state> | State law; permanent |
| N/S - fixture below minimums | <business unit 1> exclusion per the <incentive program> deck |
| N/S - paused this cycle | The <monthly POS kit> paused it mid-cycle |
| N/S - not recognizable by <photo platform> | Scored but no recognition column; verify in <CRM> |
| N/S - begins <cycle> | Measurement has not started yet |

A bare "N/S" tells a rep nothing; the reason is the deliverable.

Every cell carries its reason code as visible text next to the symbol (check mark PASS, X MISS,
exclamation mark IQI, dash N/E), so a rep reading a printed or filtered sheet sees why without
decoding a fill color or scrolling to the margin; the "Why" margin column then expands each code
into a sentence. A cell showing only a symbol is incomplete. (The grey code is written N/S in
general and N/E where eligibility is the cause; both are the same grey class.)

Targeted measures are the norm, not the exception. Many POS measures are scored only in a small
set of assigned stores: <our brand 2D> signage, for example, typically applies to fewer than 20
stores per rep and is a <territory role> election between <our brand 2D> and <our brand 2C> in the
discount-focus store types. Targeting is resolved from the <sign type 1> store-type pages and the
model's eligibility measures. When a measure's pass count is a small fraction of the scope, that
is targeting, not a territory-wide failure: the non-targeted stores are marked N/E, never MISS,
and the report says how many stores the measure actually applied to.

Red means "go fix this", yellow means "cannot be assessed", green means "passed". An IQI, NOPH,
N/E or STALE cell is never colored red; a rep who drives to a store because of a bad camera angle
stops trusting the report. Every cell carries both a symbol and a color, never color alone, so the
sheet stays readable for a colorblind rep.

Suggested fills: green C6EFCE with font 006100; red FFC7CE with font 9C0006; yellow FFEB9C with
font 9C6500; grey D9D9D9 with font 808080. Every status cell is centered.

Step 4b - Score Inventory / On-Shelf Availability Too:

POS visibility is only half of what the rep is scored on; the <incentive program> also carries
inventory metrics, and omitting them under-reports the rep's exposure. They form their own clearly
labeled section, reported as in stock / out of stock:

- <business unit 2> on-shelf availability: the model's measure for the percentage of
  <business unit 2> SKUs visible in the latest cycle; in the current half it covers <our brand 2D> (any
  <required SKU count> SKUs) and the <loyalty program>.
- <business unit 4> on-shelf availability: the equivalent <business unit 4> measure, covering the
  <our brand 4A> standard line and targeted <our brand 4A> premium line SKU sets.

These are percentages, not binary flags. The value is shown in the cell and shaded, but no
store-level pass or fail is asserted unless the <incentive program> deck states a store-level
threshold: payout for on-shelf availability is measured at market level, so a store percentage is
a prioritization signal, not a verdict, and the Legend says so. The cell is grey N/S where the
store has no SKU photo in the cycle.

The <incentive program> deck is also checked for measures that have not started yet; they are
marked "N/S - begins <cycle>" rather than scored. In the current half, <loyalty program> on-shelf
availability measurement begins with a later cycle; reporting it as failing before then is wrong.

Step 5 - Build the Workbook:

Built with the openpyxl library; three sheets.

Sheet 1, Scorecard, one row per store:
- Left block (freeze panes here): <store id>, store name, city, chain or independent, last visit
  date.
- One column per currently scored POS measure, each cell carrying symbol and reason code (check
  mark PASS, X MISS, exclamation mark IQI, NOPH or STALE, dash N/E).
- Right margin columns: Fails (count of MISS); Why, a readable per-measure reason string such as
  "<business unit 4> POS: MISS; <our brand 3A> <variant>: IQI (reflection); <sign type 2>: NOPH";
  and Photo Quality.
- An auto-filter across the header row so a rep can isolate MISS in one click.
- Sorted by MISS count descending, worst stores at the top.

Sheet 2, Summary: pass rate per POS measure across the scope, MISS counts, average on-shelf
availability for <business unit 2> and <business unit 4>, and the same rollup per territory for a
market run. This is the "where do I stand" answer.

Sheet 3, Legend: every code in plain English with the action it implies; the sales cycle used; the
model's last refresh timestamp; every source document the measure list came from, by exact name
and cover date (the state <sign type 1> guide or guides from the live space, the
<incentive program> details, the <monthly POS kit>, any <field initiative guide> relied on); any measure
retired or paused this cycle and why; and the indicator caveat from the Guardrails. A rep must be
able to see which guide drove the scoring without asking; for a multi-state scope every state
guide read is named.

Filename: "POS Gap Scorecard - <scope> - <cycle>.xlsx", delivered to the session's output folder.

Step 6 - Report:

Five lines in chat: scope and store count, overall pass rate, the two or three measures with the
most misses, how many stores could not be assessed and why, and the filename.

Completeness is always stated explicitly, in the form "<n> of <n> stores retrieved across 1
territory" or "<n> of <m> across 11 territories; territory <territory id> partial (<n> of <m>)".
A <market role> who walked away needs one line saying whether the report is whole. Completeness
not verified in Step 3b is never implied.

Guardrails:

- Never fetch or claim to have viewed a store photo; the images are unreachable. Every finding
  traces to a Power BI recognition result.
- Never score a measure the current guides do not score. The <sign type 1> guide and
  <incentive program> deck from Step 2 are the authority on what counts this cycle; the model's column list
  is not. A column that has outlived its initiative is a trap, not a measure.
- Never turn a targeted measure into a wall of red. Stores outside a measure's assigned set are
  grey N/S, and the applicable store count is stated.
- Never drop a scored category because it is not POS. Inventory / on-shelf availability is part of
  the same <incentive program>; it is reported, labeled as inventory, not folded into POS counts.
- Never score a measure that has not started, or one the state does not permit. Both are grey.
- Never read a <sign type 1> guide from outside the live space, and never from the prep space.
  Stale mirrors elsewhere in SharePoint silently score the wrong cycle.
- Never apply one state's measure set to another state's stores; multi-state scopes read every
  state guide in scope.
- Never accept a first empty result: three attempts, 30 seconds apart, before calling anything
  empty, and never rewrite a query that has not been retried.
- Never ship a short report silently. If the reconciliation gate fails, the workbook goes out
  stamped INCOMPLETE with the exact missing territories and counts named.
- Never shorten the <market role> or <market support role> path: same steps, checks and measures
  as a <territory role>, only longer. Slow is fine; incomplete is not.
- Never report a yellow condition as a merchandising failure, in the workbook or in chat. MISS
  counts and "could not assess" counts are separate numbers.
- Never report a scope that was not fully retrieved; if territory batching came up short of the
  control count, the gap is stated.
- State the cycle and the model refresh date on the Summary and Legend sheets and in chat. An
  undated scorecard gets argued about in a market meeting.
- Print the source caveat on the Summary sheet: the <shelf recognition model> dashboard is an
  indicator and will not match <incentive program> results exactly; use it for themes, trends and
  prioritization, never to dispute an <incentive program> score.
- Never fabricate a store, a flag or a pass rate. If a query returns nothing, say so.
- Do not evaluate or rank employees. The skill scores store conditions, never the rep who called
  on the store, even though the model carries a user name and a title column. Results are never
  grouped by person; the assistant declines if asked to.
- Do not email or share the workbook unless explicitly asked.
