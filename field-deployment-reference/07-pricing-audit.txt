Part 7 of 11: Pricing Audit
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pricing Audit
=============

Purpose: Runs the Pricing Opportunity Agent's audit from chat for a chain, <jurisdiction> or
territory and returns an actionable report: <company> items whose retailer margin sits above the
lowest competitive margin in their store and category, <category 1> items priced over the
<price ceiling>, the parity price and ask per finding, and the gates (missing costs, buy-downs,
allowances, legal restrictions) that limit the answer. The engine is the app's own, proven by
regression, so the chat answer and the app always agree.

Triggers:
- "audit my <chain> stores for pricing"
- "audit <chain> in <jurisdiction>"
- "where are my pricing opportunities"
- "margin gaps at <chain>"
- "price audit for my territory"
- "is <chain> above the <price ceiling>"

Inputs:
- The Pricing Opportunity Agent's tables, pulled into the session's working folder as CSV: the
  <store table>, <product table>, <item master table>, <wholesale cost table>,
  <competitive buy-down table>, <program allowance table>, <state setup table> and <wholesaler table>.
- One <shelf price table> per territory: 13-week realized scan prices per store and SKU, sourced
  from the <data warehouse>.
- The signed-in user's profile (territory or market), read when the request says "my".
- The request itself: chain or account, state, territory, optional category, optional as-of date.

Outputs:
- One Excel workbook, "Pricing Audit - <chain or scope> - <state> - <date>.xlsx", in the
  session's output folder, with sheets Summary, Findings, Above <price ceiling>, Gates,
  Benchmarks and Data Notes.
- A JSON summary of the run (scope, counts, top findings, gates) that feeds the chat reply.
- A plain-language chat reply in a fixed order: scope and coverage, gates, top findings, Above
  <price ceiling> count, what would widen the answer, file name and follow-through offer.
- On request only: the workbook sent by Outlook email, or a weekly scheduled re-run.

Parameters to Fill In:
- <company> - the manufacturer whose items are audited against competitive items.
- <business unit>, <business unit 1> through <business unit 5> - the operating units that own
  <category 1> through <category 5>; each store has a primary wholesaler per unit.
- <category 1> through <category 5> - the product categories; <category 5> is shown on the
  shelf but never benchmarked.
- <our brand 1A> through <our brand 1D>, <our brand 2A> through <our brand 2D>, <our brand 3A>,
  <our brand 4A>, <our brand 5A> - the company's brands the price-family rules split or keep.
- <competitor brand 1A>, <competitor brand 1B>, <competitor brand 1C>, <competitor brand 2A>,
  <competitor brand 2B>, <competitor brand 2C>, <competitor brand 2G>, <competitor brand 3A>,
  <competitor brand 3B>, <competitor brand 3C>, <competitor brand 3E>, <competitor brand 4A>,
  <competitor brand 4B>, <competitor brand 4C>, <competitor brand 4G> - the competitive brands
  with a fixed family rule; any other competitive brand keeps its brand family name.
- <price family A> through <price family J> - the named cost families costs are saved under;
  see the notes file for which brand line each letter stands for.
- <promotional price program> - the consumer price promotion that one <allowance type A>
  program row is paired with in the program list.
- <flavor>, <variant> - the flavor word and the variant names the family tests look for in a
  SKU name.
- <price support program> - the program under which a store elects a pricing option; its
  election mix is reported and its sheet lists the <business unit 1> candidate families.
- <election option A> through <election option E> - the program's election labels; "All" is
  the wildcard that applies to every election.
- <price ceiling> - the pass price in force for a <category 1> family under an election; the
  check flags 13-week prices above it.
- <allowance type A>, <allowance type B>, <allowance type C> - the allowance programs whose
  rows reduce net cost; two exclusive groups apply the smaller rate.
- <allowance amount>, <price point> - a dollars-per-unit allowance and a shelf or pass price
  from the current tables.
- <store table>, <product table>, <item master table>, <wholesale cost table>,
  <competitive buy-down table>, <program allowance table>, <state setup table>, <wholesaler table>,
  <shelf price table> - the app's tables and the per-territory scan price table.
- <account name column>, <account level 2 column>, <account level 3 column> - the store
  master's corporate account name and its two sub-levels.
- <self-distributing flag> - the <wholesaler table> column marking a chain's own DC.
- <store id> - the retail account identifier that keys stores to scan rows.
- <sales data model> - the enterprise sales model whose corporate account names and wholesaler
  types the tables inherit.
- <data warehouse> - the source of 13-week realized scan prices.
- <market>, <territory id>, <jurisdiction> - the geography the tables hold today and the legal
  jurisdiction whose promotion restrictions apply.
- <chain A> through <chain E>, <wholesaler A> - example chains (several self-distributing) and
  the largest wholesaler that serves as their cost proxy.
- <territory role>, <market role>, <account role> - the roles whose territory, market or chain
  bounds a run.
- <date>, <n> - run dates and counts recorded from the company's own regression history.
- Run-time template slots, not company parameters: <chain or scope>, <state>, <yyyy-mm-dd> and
  <chain> in the file name pattern; <proxy>, <dc name>, <id>, <item>, <price>, <parity>,
  <benchmark>, <allowance>, <company item>, <parity price> and <benchmark item> inside quoted
  output text. The run fills them from its own rows.

What the User Says and Gets
---------------------------

The user says "Audit my <chain A> stores for pricing" or "Where are my pricing opportunities at
<chain B> in <jurisdiction>?" and Cowork runs the Pricing Opportunity Agent's audit and hands back
a workbook and a plain-language summary.

What comes back: every <company> item whose retailer margin sits above the lowest competitive
margin in its store and category, with the parity price and an ask sentence; every <category 1>
item priced over its <price ceiling>; and the gates (missing wholesale costs, buy-downs,
allowances, legal restrictions) that limit the answer, named so the user knows what to enter next.

Where prices go in: the app's State Setup sheets, the one-sheet entry for costs, buy-downs and
allowances. This skill only reads.

Files: SKILL.md (the workflow), references/ (rules, gates, report format, regression proof),
scripts/ (engine, audit runner, table pull, regression check, oracle harness).

Skill Instructions
------------------

The skill turns "audit my <chain A> stores for pricing" into a report the <account role> can act
on, using the Pricing Opportunity Agent's own rules and data. The agent's Managed App is the
surface account teams use; this skill is the same audit run from chat, so a person never has to
open the app to get the answer, and the two always agree (the engine here is a verified port of
the app's).

When to Use:

- The user names a chain, account, state or territory and asks for pricing opportunities, a
  pricing audit, margin gaps, or whether items ring above the pass price.
- The user asks the agent to run or re-run an audit, or to send one on a schedule.

When Not to Use:

- Building, seeding, repairing or porting the agent: Pricing Opportunity Agent (Builder).
- Entering wholesale costs, buy-downs or allowances: the app's State Setup sheets (the assistant
  says so and stops).
- A single margin calculation with numbers supplied in chat: the assistant answers inline.
- Non-scan stores: the audit needs scan prices; the assistant says the store has none rather
  than estimating.

Workflow:

1. Resolve the scope. The assistant takes the chain, state and territory from the request. The
  chain is matched loosely against the store master's <account name column> and
  <account level 2 column> (case, spacing and punctuation insensitive, so a trade name resolves to a corporate
  account name that may look quite different). If several accounts match, the assistant lists
  them and asks which; it never guesses. If no state is named, every state the chain has stores
  in is audited, one run per state, because costs and allowances are per state. When the request
  says "my", the assistant reads the signed-in user's profile; their territory or market bounds
  the stores until the platform provides per-user scope (see Reference: Scope and Gates).
2. Pull the tables. The assistant runs the table-pull script (arguments: output folder, the
  territories in scope, a skip-shelf switch). It writes the <store table>, <product table>,
  <item master table>, <wholesale cost table>, <competitive buy-down table>,
  <program allowance table>, <state setup table>, <wholesaler table> and the per-territory <shelf price table> as
  CSVs. Shelf tables are pulled only for the territories in scope; a same-day copy is reused
  when one exists.
3. Run the audit. The assistant runs the audit runner with the tables folder, the account, the
  state, the as-of date (today), a workbook path "Pricing Audit - <chain> - <state> - <date>.xlsx"
  in the session's working folder, and a JSON summary path. Exit code 3 means no account
  matched, 4 means ambiguous (the candidates are in the JSON), 5 means no stores in scope. A
  chain supplied by its own distribution center (DC) is priced from the state's cost proxy, the
  largest wholesaler with costs entered, because a chain DC prints no cost on its invoices; the
  Gates sheet names every DC-to-proxy pair (Reference: Engine Rules, Cost Proxy).
4. Publish the report. The assistant copies the workbook to the session's output folder (the
  file is the deliverable) and reads the JSON summary for the chat reply.
5. Reply in plain language, in this order: scope and coverage (stores, scanning stores,
  elections); the gates first when they limit the answer (costs on file, buy-downs, restricted
  programs, proposed rows); the top findings by weekly volume with the parity price and ask; the
  Above <price ceiling> count with the "verify in store" caveat when the pass price changed
  inside the 13-week window; and what would widen the answer (which wholesaler's prices, entered
  on the app's cost sheet). A gate is never presented as an opportunity, nor an opportunity as a
  gate.
6. Offer follow-through: the assistant emails the workbook through Outlook with the file
  attached (only when asked), or sets up a weekly scheduled prompt with this scope written into
  it.

Example:

User: "Audit my <chain A> stores for pricing."

1. <chain A> resolves to its corporate account (<n> stores, all in one <jurisdiction>); the user
  is a <territory role> in <market>, so the market's tables are the data on file.
2. The table-pull script refreshes the tables; the audit runner runs that <jurisdiction> with
  as-of today.
3. The reply follows the fixed order. Coverage: "<chain A>, <jurisdiction>: <n> stores, <n>
  scanning, <n> <election option C> and <n> <election option B>." Gate first: <chain A> is
  supplied by its own DC, which prints no cost on its invoices, so its stores are priced from
  <wholesaler A>'s costs, the largest wholesaler in the <jurisdiction>; <wholesaler A> has one
  price on file, so there are no margin findings yet; the first five families to price for
  <wholesaler A> are named, to be entered on the app's cost sheet for that wholesaler. What the
  audit can already show: <n> Above <price ceiling> flags in <n> stores, a named <our brand 1A>
  item ringing <price point> against the <price point> <election option C> pass price effective
  <date>; the pass price changed inside the 13-week window, so verify in store. Then the report
  file name.

Failure Handling:

- The table-pull script fails or a table comes back empty: the assistant says which table,
  retries once, then runs on the most recent local copy only if it is from today, and says so;
  otherwise it stops.
- The audit runner exits 3 (no account), 4 (ambiguous; candidates are in the JSON) or 5 (no
  stores in scope): the assistant reports it in plain words and asks; it never runs the nearest
  match.
- No scanning stores in scope: the assistant says the audit needs scan prices and offers the
  Above <price ceiling> check for the stores that do scan, or nothing.
- A state with no costs and no buy-downs: the assistant delivers the Gates and Above
  <price ceiling> sheets, names the wholesalers to price, and says the margin ladder is empty until
  then.
- A chain supplied by its own DC: the assistant never asks for the DC's invoice costs as the
  fix; it names the proxy wholesaler (the Cost Proxy gate) and the families still needed from
  it.
- The as-of date matters: allowances, buy-downs and <price ceiling> rows are date-effective;
  today is used unless the user names a date, and the date is printed in the summary.

Output Format:

One workbook: Summary (scope, counts, rule, read-the-gates note), Findings (store, item, shelf
price, unit cost, allowance, net cost, margin, benchmark item and margin, gap points, penny gap,
parity price, 13-week and weekly units, wholesaler, cost date, ask sentence), Above
<price ceiling> (13-week and 4-week price, pass price, above by, effective date, standing or
verify-in-store), Gates, Benchmarks (per store and category), Data Notes. Plus a chat summary as
in workflow step 5. ASCII only, Title Case headings, no long dashes.

Guardrails:

- Never fabricate: every number traces to a workbook row or a scan row; nothing is estimated,
  filled in or rounded away. When data is missing, the gap is named, not bridged.
- Costs, buy-downs and allowances are read, never written, by this skill.
- The audit is internal: output is never phrased as retailer-facing.
- A state's Restricted programs are reported as applied-as-zero, not as gaps.
- When the account is ambiguous or unmatched, the assistant stops and asks; it does not run the
  nearest match.
- The engine stays identical to the app's: changes go to the app first, then the regression
  checker is re-run against the oracle (Reference: Regression) before this skill adopts them.

References:

- Reference: Engine Rules - the audit rules, exactly as the engine applies them.
- Reference: Scope and Gates - chain resolution, per-user scope, the gates and how to read them.
- Reference: Report Format - the workbook layout and the chat reply template.
- Reference: Regression - how the Python engine is proven against the app's engine.
- scripts/ - the pricing engine, the audit runner, the table-pull script, the regression checker

References
----------

Reference: Engine Rules:

These are the rules the pricing engine applies, in the order it applies them. They are the same
as the Managed App's (its shelf, allowance, store and workbook modules); the regression in
Reference: Regression proves it.

Engine Rules - Scope:

- Stores come from the <store table>; the account filter matches the collapsed account name.
- Only stores with scan rows produce anything. A store's SKU rows are the <shelf price table>
  rows for its <store id>; a row needs a <store id>, a material id and a positive 13-week price
  (the 13-week price field, else 13-week dollars divided by 13-week units).
- Slim shelf rows (keys and numbers only) are enriched from the <product table> by material id:
  name, brand family, manufacturer, category group, <company> flag. A row whose product is
  unknown is skipped.

Engine Rules - Categories:

<category 1> (<business unit 1>, per pack, trigger 1.0 margin point, penny floor $0.10),
<category 2> (<business unit 2>, per can, 2.0 points, $0.10), <category 3> (<business unit 3>,
per stick, 2.0 points, $0.05; a 5-pack is its own unit with a $0.10 floor), <category 4>
(<business unit 4>, per can, 2.0 points, $0.10). <category 5> is shown on the shelf but never
benchmarked. The <business unit>'s primary wholesaler for the store prices every item in the
group, competitive items included.

Engine Rules - Price Family (What a Cost Is Saved Under):

- <our brand 1A> has four cost families: <price family A> (the base non-<flavor> line),
  <price family B> (the <flavor> line), <price family C> (a line identified by its color family or
  name) and <price family D> (a line identified by its <variant> name). The tests run in this
  order: color family or name -> <price family C>; <variant> name -> <price family D>; <flavor>,
  unless the color family marks the <price family E> sub-line -> <price family B>; otherwise
  <price family A>. Names come from the <item master table>.
- <our brand 1B>, <our brand 1C> and <our brand 1D> resolve by brand family. <our brand 2A>
  resolves to <price family G> (its value-priced line) when the SKU name carries a brand the
  <item master table> lists under that line, else to <price family F> (its main line); the
  brand's value sub-brand abbreviation also maps to <price family G>. <our brand 2B>,
  <our brand 2C> and <our brand 2D> resolve by brand family. <our brand 3A> resolves by tip words (filter,
  plastic, wood and one named <variant>) and 5-pack versus single, matched against the
  <business unit 3> family names. <our brand 4A>'s premium line versus its base line resolves by name.
  <our brand 5A> maps to one fixed family.
- Competitive families are a fixed list: <competitor brand 1A> (split into <flavor> box, <flavor>
  soft pack and a non-<flavor> line), <competitor brand 1B> (two named sub-lines),
  <competitor brand 1C>'s value line, <competitor brand 2A>, <competitor brand 2B>, <competitor brand 2G>,
  <competitor brand 2C>, <competitor brand 1B>'s <category 2> line, <competitor brand 4A>,
  <competitor brand 4B>, <competitor brand 4C>, <competitor brand 4G>, <competitor brand 3A>
  (5-pack versus twin pack), <competitor brand 3C> single, <competitor brand 3E> single and
  <competitor brand 3B> single; anything else keeps its brand family name.

Engine Rules - Cost:

The latest <wholesale cost table> row per state, wholesaler and family wins (ties go to the later
row). Unit cost = invoice price divided by the packing factor, where the factor is the invoice
price divided by the derived unit price, rounded. No cost means status "needed" (wholesaler
known) or "no-wholesaler".

Engine Rules - Cost Proxy for Self-Distributing DCs:

A chain's own distribution center (<chain A>'s, <chain C>'s and <chain D>'s, for example) prints
no cost on its invoices, so nobody can enter one. The <wholesaler table> marks these rows with a
<self-distributing flag> of Yes (a blank cell falls back to the <sales data model>'s wholesaler
type for a retailer-owned DC or club). For a store whose <business unit> wholesaler is flagged:

- A cost entered for the DC itself is used when it exists. Otherwise the item is priced from the
  state's cost proxy: the largest wholesaler in the state (by stores served in the
  <wholesaler table>, then name) that is not self-distributing and has at least one cost entered for the
  state. When no wholesaler in the state has costs yet, the proxy is simply the largest, so the
  prices still needed are asked of the right wholesaler.
- The item's cost source is the proxy; its "needed" rows roll up under the proxy, not the DC;
  the ask sentence reads "based on <proxy> cost (proxy for <dc name>) dated ...".
- The run reports the count of SKU rows priced from a proxy and the list of cost proxies (each
  DC, its proxy and the stores in scope it serves). A flag can be set or cleared on the app's
  Wholesalers board; the change takes effect on the next run.

Engine Rules - <company> Allowance:

- Candidate families for <business unit 1> are the <price support program> sheet families
  (<price family A>, <price family B>, <price family H> (a short-length pack line),
  <price family E>, <price family C> in non-<flavor> and <flavor> forms, <price family I> and
  <price family J> (two label-color lines), <price family D>), tried in that order, with the cost
  family last; every other <business unit> resolves on the cost family alone.
- A <program allowance table> row applies when: same state; same <business unit>; its election
  is All or the store's <price support program> election (two legacy labels normalize to
  <election option C> and <election option D>, and a "<price support program> " prefix is
  ignored); same family; status Confirmed, with an amount, effective on or before the as-of date
  and not ended; its selling unit matches the item's (a roll row never reduces a per-can margin;
  a blank unit matches anything); and, when the row carries an item-match pattern, the SKU name
  satisfies it (all words present, a minus-prefixed word absent, pipe-separated alternatives).
- An item-scoped row takes its program for that item; the family row for the same program does
  not stack. The latest effective date wins per program; on a tie a row naming the election
  beats All.
- Exclusive groups apply the smaller rate when both are on file: <business unit 4>'s three
  <allowance type B> variants (multi-pack offer, scan multi-pack offer, non-scan);
  <business unit 2>'s non-scan <allowance type A> and multi-unit <allowance type A>.
- Amount = the sum of the applied programs except the <price ceiling> row. The <price ceiling>
  row is the pass price for <category 1>, not an allowance.
- No Confirmed row but a Restricted row for the family: applied as zero, and the item is not
  "allowance missing".

Engine Rules - Competitive Buy-Down:

The sum of active <competitive buy-down table> rows for the state whose brand matches the family.
When the brand has none and a "lowest-priced <category 1> in store" row is active, the store's
cheapest competitive <category 1> item (lowest 13-week price, ties to most units) gets that
amount.

Engine Rules - Margin, Benchmark, Finding:

- Net cost = unit cost - allowance. Penny profit = shelf price - net cost. Margin percent = penny
  profit / shelf price x 100.
- Per store and category: benchmark = the competitive item with a cost and the lowest margin.
- Finding = a <company> item with a cost whose margin exceeds the benchmark's by more than the
  category trigger, or whose penny profit exceeds the benchmark's by more than the floor.
- Parity price = benchmark penny profit + the <company> item's net cost.
- Above <price ceiling> = a <category 1> item whose 13-week price exceeds the <price ceiling> in
  force by more than $0.001.
- Order: findings by 13-week units, then penny gap; <price ceiling> flags by units, then amount
  above.
- Missing = every "needed" item rolled up by wholesaler and family with store count and units.
- Gaps = categories where no competitive item in a store has a cost (no benchmark possible).

Reference: Regression:

The app's engine is TypeScript; this skill's engine is a Python port. They must agree exactly, so
the port is checked against the app's own code, not against a description of it.

Regression - The Oracle:

The oracle folder holds a copy of the app's engine modules with the Excel connector import
replaced by a stub (only pure functions are used), plus a harness that loads a JSON snapshot of
the tables, runs the shelf-run computation and writes findings, <price ceiling> flags, every
shelf item, benchmarks, gaps and missing prices as JSON. It runs under the bun TypeScript runtime
(installed in the Cowork container) with three arguments: snapshot path, output path, as-of date.

Regression - The Snapshot:

The snapshot builder assembles the snapshot from the local table mirrors. A synthetic switch adds
a regression fixture of wholesale costs (one per wholesaler and family, list price with a fixed
uplift; every row is labelled as a synthetic regression fixture) and three buy-downs, because
the live table has too few costs to exercise the benchmark path. The fixture is never loaded
anywhere. The state's <wholesaler table> rows ride along with their <self-distributing flag>;
self-distributing DCs get no synthetic cost and the state's two largest true wholesalers always
do, so the cost proxy path is exercised in every scope.

Regression - The Check:

The Python engine runs on the same snapshot and as-of date and writes its own JSON; the
regression checker compares the oracle JSON to the Python JSON. PASS requires identical
summaries, the same finding keys with every field equal (numbers within 1e-6), the same
<price ceiling> flags, the same shelf items, benchmarks, gaps and missing rows, and the same finding
order.

Regression - Results on Record:

Each recorded run lists its date, scope and counts (stores, scanning stores, items, items priced
from a proxy, findings, <price ceiling> flags, DC-to-proxy pairs) and its verdict. Before the
cost proxy rule (<date>, no wholesaler flags in the snapshot): a territory scope and a chain
scope (<chain B>) in one <jurisdiction> with synthetic costs, both PASS with zero differences.
After the cost proxy rule (<date>): both earlier snapshots were re-run first and still PASS, so
the rule changes nothing where no wholesaler is flagged; the comparison now also covers the ask
text, the cost source and proxy per item, and the DC-to-proxy pairs; the territory, the two
self-distributing chains <chain A> and <chain C>, and the whole <jurisdiction> (four DCs,
including <chain D>'s and <chain E>'s, all priced from <wholesaler A>) all PASS with zero
differences. Six scopes, <n> findings compared, zero differences.

Regression - When the App Changes:

The app's engine modules are re-copied into the oracle folder (replacing the connector import
line the same way), the change is ported into the Python engine, and both scopes are re-run. The
skill adopts a change only on PASS.

Reference: Report Format:

Report Format - Workbook:

File name: "Pricing Audit - <chain or scope> - <state> - <yyyy-mm-dd>.xlsx", published to the
session's output folder. Sheets, in order:

1. Summary: scope, as-of date, stores in scope and scanning, <price support program> election
  mix, findings, Above <price ceiling> count, weekly units on flagged items, costs / allowances
  / restricted / buy-downs on file, the self-distributing DCs in scope and the proxy each is
  priced from, SKU rows priced from a proxy, shelf price source, the rule in one paragraph, and
  "Read The Gates Sheet First".
2. Findings: state, store, <store id>, account, city, election, category, <company> item, shelf
  price, unit cost, allowance, net cost, margin percent, benchmark item, benchmark price,
  benchmark margin percent, gap points, penny gap, parity price, 13-week units, weekly units,
  wholesaler, cost source (the wholesaler whose price list priced the item; "<proxy> (proxy)"
  for a DC store), cost dated, ask. Sorted by weekly units then penny gap.
3. Above <price ceiling>: state, store, <store id>, account, election, item, 13-week shelf
  price, 4-week shelf price, <price ceiling>, above by, <price ceiling> effective date, read
  (Standing or Verify in store), 13-week units, <price ceiling> row title.
4. Gates: state, gate, item, stores, detail, top needed.
5. Benchmarks: per store and category, the benchmark item, its price and margin, <company>
  items priced and flagged.
6. Data Notes: where each number comes from.

Report Format - The Ask Sentence:

"<company item> rings at <price point>; at <parity price> it matches <benchmark item>'s margin
and keeps the <allowance amount> allowance." The parity price is the shelf price at which the
retailer earns the benchmark's penny profit on our item; the allowance clause names the amount
applied and is dropped when none applies. Internal wording; never retailer-facing.

Report Format - Chat Reply (Order Matters):

1. Scope and coverage: "<chain A>, <jurisdiction>: <n> stores, <n> scanning, <n>
  <election option C> and <n> <election option B>."
2. Gates that limit the answer, plainly: the chain is supplied by its own DC, which prints no
  cost on its invoices, so its stores are priced from <wholesaler A>'s costs; <wholesaler A>
  has one price on file, so there are no margin findings yet; the first five families to price
  for <wholesaler A> are named.
3. Top findings by weekly volume, each with item, shelf price, parity price, benchmark, weekly
  units, and the ask.
4. Above <price ceiling>: count, the items, and the verify-in-store caveat when it applies.
5. What would widen the answer and how (the app's cost or buy-down sheet for a named
  wholesaler).
6. The file name, and the offer to email it or run it weekly.

Plain ASCII, no long dashes, no markdown tables in the chat reply.

Reference: Scope and Gates:

Scope and Gates - Resolving the Chain:

The <store table> carries the <account name column> (the corporate name the <sales data model>
uses), the <account level 2 column> and the <account level 3 column>. Names are matched after
removing case, spaces and punctuation, as a substring, so a chain's trade name finds its
corporate account even when the two share only a fragment, and a hyphenated chain name finds its
headquarters account. When the match spreads over more than one account (less than 80 percent of
the matching stores on the leader) the audit runner exits 4 with the candidates: the assistant
shows them and asks. "Independent" is the account of every store without a chain.

Scope and Gates - Per-User Scope:

Today every table holds one <market> only and the user is the market's own team. When the
platform lands the feed under each user's own row-level security, the same scripts run against
the user's tables: a <territory role>'s territory, a <market role>'s market, an <account role>'s
chain across states. Until then, the assistant reads the signed-in user's profile and states
plainly when the request reaches beyond the data on file (for example a chain's stores in a
<jurisdiction> the tables do not hold).

Scope and Gates - The Gates, and How to Read Them:

Every finding is conditional on the inputs, so the report shows the gates before the findings.

- Wholesale Cost: per wholesaler serving the scope, how many prices are on file and how many
  families the scanning stores still need. Zero prices for the dominant wholesaler means zero
  benchmarks: the report will show Above <price ceiling> findings (which need no cost) and
  nothing else. The fix is the app's cost sheet for that wholesaler and state. A wholesaler that
  stands in for a DC says so ("also the cost proxy for ..."), and a proxy that serves no store in
  scope directly still gets a row, because the DC's stores wait on its prices.
- Cost Proxy: one row per self-distributing DC in scope (a chain's own warehouse, which prints
  no cost on its invoices): the wholesaler its stores are priced from and how many prices that
  proxy has on file. Not a gap to close by chasing the DC for an invoice; the fix, when the
  chain's real cost is known, is to enter it for the DC, which then takes precedence over the
  proxy.
- No Benchmark: a category where no competitive item has a cost in those stores, with the three
  highest-volume competitive families to price first.
- Competitive Buy-Downs: none on file means competitive margins are gross of any buy-down and
  will look worse than they are; the fix is the app's buy-down sheet.
- Allowance Missing: priced <company> rows with no allowance in force; their margin is gross of
  the allowance and will look better than it is. The user checks Missing By Program Level in the
  app.
- Restricted By Law: programs the <jurisdiction> prohibits (the <jurisdiction> on file bans
  every multi-unit discount except on <category 4>, and all <category 5> promotions). Applied as
  zero; not a gap. The assistant never suggests entering a rate for one.
- Proposed Allowances: rows the weekly reader loaded that nobody has confirmed. Not applied.
- Aging Cost: prices older than 90 days; still applied, flagged for a Still Current check.

Scope and Gates - Reading Above <price ceiling>:

The shelf price is a 13-week realized average. When the pass price changed inside that window
the store may already have moved; the 4-week column is the earlier read and the row says "Verify
in store". A standing flag (pass price older than the window) is a pass-through failure to raise.

Script: Pricing Engine  (source role: the Python port of the Managed App's margin engine; the
rules of Reference: Engine Rules as code)
Purpose: Computes, per store and category, the lowest-margin competitive benchmark and every
  <company> item above it, plus Above <price ceiling> flags, missing-cost roll-ups and benchmark
  gaps, from the app's tables, rule for rule as the app does. Nothing in it reads the network.
Inputs: The tables as lists of rows (the CSV or JSON mirror): stores, shelf SKUs, products, item
  master, wholesale costs, allowances, buy-downs and wholesaler rows; an account filter, a
  category filter and an as-of date. Run standalone it takes a snapshot JSON (the same file the
  oracle harness takes), an output path and an optional as-of date.
Outputs: A run object: stores in scope, scanning stores, SKUs on shelf, SKUs with a cost, SKUs
  priced from a proxy, the DC-to-proxy pairs, store-category count and how many have a
  benchmark, the categories with a benchmark, findings, <price ceiling> flags, the
  allowance-missing count, per-store shelves with items and benchmarks, gaps and missing-cost
  roll-ups. Standalone: a comparable JSON (as-of date, summary, findings, flags, items,
  benchmarks, gaps, cost proxies, missing) with numbers rounded to six decimals, and the summary
  printed to the console.
Logic:
  1. Normalization helpers: any value to text (booleans to Yes or No, whole floats without
    decimals), text to number (blank or non-numeric to none), yes-words (yes, true, y, 1),
    whitespace collapse, compaction (no spaces, lowercase), word splitting, Excel serial dates
    between 20,000 and 80,000 to ISO dates (else the first ten characters of the text). Money
    formatting rounds half-up on the exact value and integer rounding rounds halves upward, both
    mimicking the app's runtime so the ask text and packing factors are byte-identical.
  2. Category table: five category groups (<category 1> through <category 5>), four of which can
    produce findings; each carries a category label, the owning <business unit>, a unit name
    (pack, can, stick, device), trigger points (1.0 for <category 1>, 2.0 elsewhere) and a penny
    floor ($0.10, or $0.05 per stick); a 5-pack of <category 3> uses a $0.10 floor. A set of
    <company> manufacturer codes marks our items. Raw category text maps to a group by prefix and
    keyword. Competitive families get default list units and pack factors by category (box of 25
    for <category 3>, roll of 5 for <category 2> and <category 4>, carton of 10 otherwise).
  3. Row parsers. Shelf SKU: <store id> and material id from their columns or from a
    "<store id>|material" title key; 13-week units and dollars; the 13-week price, else dollars
    divided by units; the row is dropped without a <store id>, a material id or a positive
    price; 4-week units and price are kept only when positive; the our-flag comes from its
    column or from the manufacturer code. Product: material id, name, brand family,
    manufacturer, group, our-flag, <flavor> flag (a yes-word, or the <flavor> word without
    "non"), color family, sub-brand, 13-week units. A slim SKU (keys and numbers only) is
    enriched from its product; one with no product and no name receives a placeholder name.
    Store: <store id> (or the title key); the election from the normalized election column,
    else the raw column (blank or opt-out -> "No Election"; a value beginning with the
    <election option D> word -> <election option D>; else as written); name (or "Store <id>"),
    store number, city, state, territory, account (or "Independent"), account level 2,
    ownership, on-recommendation flag, two volume figures and one wholesaler per
    <business unit>. Cost: state, wholesaler, family and printed invoice price required; derived unit
    price (else the invoice price); packing factor = invoice price over derived unit price,
    rounded, at least 1; effective date (else entered-on), source, notes, entered by, entered
    on. Wholesaler option: one per name (first row wins) with store count, type and the
    self-distributing verdict (the flag column when filled, else the type equals the
    retailer-DC-or-club type), sorted by stores descending (missing last), then name. Buy-down:
    state, brand or line and allowance per selling unit required; manufacturer, offer type
    (default "Buy-down"), qualifying quantity (default 1), effective date (else entered-on), end
    date. Allowance: state, family and program required; status from the status text (a
    "needed" word, a missing amount or a missing effective date -> Needed; restrict, prohibit or
    illegal -> Restricted; proposed, pending or review -> Proposed; reject or decline ->
    Rejected; else Confirmed); election (default All), selling unit, end date, source document,
    notes, item-match pattern (lowercased).
  4. Latest costs: rows group by state, wholesaler and family; a row with an equal or later
    effective date replaces the current one, and the count of earlier versions is kept; the
    result sorts by wholesaler then family.
  5. Catalog from the item master: competitive rows versus ours; among ours, the tracked rows
    when any exist, else all. Each family records its group (a <business unit>, or
    "Competitive - <category>"), category, list unit, units per carton or roll and its items
    (item key and brand description, deduplicated); competitive families fill a blank unit and
    factor from the category defaults; a sorted brand list per competitive manufacturer is kept.
  6. Family rules (Reference: Engine Rules, Price Family). A name is made canonical when the
    catalog holds a family of the same name. <our brand 1A>: the color family (or the name when
    no color is on file) is tested for the <price family C> word, then the <price family D>
    word; <flavor> comes from the product flag or the color, else from name words, and a color
    not marked as the <price family E> sub-line sends the item to the catalog's <price family B>
    entry (found by its group letter, else by the <flavor> word without "non", else a default
    name); otherwise <price family A>. <our brand 2A>: the catalog's <price family G> family is
    found by exact name or by the brand word plus the value word; a SKU whose name equals,
    contains, is contained in, or holds every significant token of one of that family's item
    brands maps to it, else to <price family F>; a brand family carrying the value sub-brand
    abbreviation maps to <price family G> directly. <our brand 3A>: the tip comes from the name
    words (the named <variant>; filter tip or its two-letter code; plastic tip or code; wood tip
    or code) with a 5-pack test; every <business unit 3> family is scored (+4 for a tip match,
    +2 when the 5-pack status agrees, +1 for a single when not a 5-pack), the top score wins
    with ties keeping catalog order, and with no score a fallback name by tip and pack is used.
    <our brand 4A>: the premium line when the name carries the premium word, else the base
    line. <our brand 5A>: one fixed family. Any other our-brand: the first catalog family whose
    compact name contains or is contained in the brand family, else the brand family as
    written. Competitive: an ordered list of brand-family prefix rules, each returning a fixed
    family name, three with sub-rules (<competitor brand 1A>: <flavor> soft pack when the name
    says soft, <flavor> box otherwise, the non-<flavor> line when not <flavor>;
    <competitor brand 1B>: one sub-line when the name carries its word, else the other;
    <competitor brand 3A>: 5-pack when the name says so, else twin pack); no rule -> the brand family as written.
  7. Allowance candidate families: for <our brand 1A> only, from name, sub-brand and color: a
    short-length pack marker in the name -> <price family H> in <flavor> or non-<flavor> form;
    the <price family E> sub-brand or name -> <price family E>; the <variant> word ->
    <price family D>; the <price family I> sub-brand -> <price family I> then <price family C>; the
    <price family C> color, two sub-brand names belonging to that line, or its word in the name
    -> <price family C> (<flavor> form by the flag, where the <flavor> color family also counts);
    the <price family J> sub-brand or name -> <price family J> then <price family A>; else
    <price family A> or <price family B> by <flavor>. The cost family is appended last unless
    already present. Every other brand: the cost family alone.
  8. Elections: a label is lowercased, a "<price support program> " prefix dropped and the two
    legacy labels normalized to <election option D> and <election option C>; <election option E>
    is recognized by name; a store has a program election when the label is non-empty and does
    not start with "no"; a row applies to a store when its election is All or matches.
  9. Item match: whitespace-separated words that must each appear in the lowercased SKU name; a
    minus-prefixed word must be absent; pipe-separated alternatives need one present; an empty
    pattern matches everything. In force: Confirmed, with an amount, an effective date on or
    before the as-of date, and no end date or one on or after it. Unit match: blank on either
    side matches; otherwise units are compacted, a trailing "s" dropped, "5pack" and "fivepack"
    normalized to 5-pack and "single" and "stick" to the <category 3> unit.
  10. Resolve the allowance for one item (state, <business unit>, election, family, unit, SKU
    name): the rows matching on state, <business unit>, election, family, in force, unit and
    item match. With none: a Restricted row for the same state, <business unit>, election and
    family applies as zero and is recorded as applied, so the item is not "missing"; else
    nothing. Otherwise programs that have an item-scoped match drop their family-level rows; the
    latest effective date per program wins, a tie going to the row naming the election over
    All; each exclusive group collapses to its smallest amount; the <price ceiling> row is set
    aside; the amount is the sum of the rest.
  11. The shelf run. Stores in scope by account; a cost index by wholesaler and family; the
    self-distributing set from the wholesaler rows; a state's proxy = the first
    non-self-distributing wholesaler by stores served then name that has a cost in the state,
    else the first overall (cached per state). When a "lowest-priced <category 1> in store"
    buy-down is active, the cheapest competitive <category 1> SKU per store is found (lowest
    13-week price, ties to more units). For each shelf SKU of an in-scope store: enrich, skip
    without a group, apply the category filter, resolve the family (cached); the wholesaler is
    the store's for the group's <business unit>; the cost comes from that wholesaler, else, when
    it is self-distributing and the proxy differs, from the proxy (recording the DC-to-proxy
    pair and the store); unit cost = invoice price over packing factor; the unit is 5-pack for a
    <category 3> 5-pack, else the group unit; our items resolve the program allowance over their
    candidate families (the first family yielding applied rows or a <price ceiling> row wins;
    cached per state, <business unit>, election, unit, families and, for <business units> with
    item-scoped rows, the SKU name); competitive items sum the active buy-downs for their family
    plus the floating lowest-price amount when they have none of their own and are the store's
    cheapest; an allowance counts only when a cost exists; net cost, penny profit and margin
    percent follow; status is priced, needed or no-wholesaler; an our-item with a cost and no
    applied rows counts as allowance-missing. A <category 1> item whose 13-week price exceeds
    the <price ceiling> row's amount by more than $0.001 becomes a flag carrying the amount
    above.
  12. Per store, items sort by group order, ours before competitive, then units descending. Per
    finding group: "needed" items roll into the missing list by cost source and family (store
    set, units, units per SKU); the benchmark is the competitive item with a margin and the
    lowest margin; with none, the category becomes a gap for that store with units per
    competitive family; otherwise every our-item with a cost is compared: gap points and penny
    gap, the two triggers, parity price = benchmark penny profit plus the item's net cost, and
    an ask text naming the amount above parity, the benchmark, the parity price, the cost
    wholesaler (with "proxy for <dc name>" when proxied) and the cost date.
  13. Findings sort by units then penny gap; flags by units then amount above; shelves by units
    then store name; cost proxies by stores then name; gaps keep the three competitive families
    with most units; missing rows sort by units then store count and keep the three SKU names
    with most units.
Checks and failure handling: Rows missing their key fields are dropped, never estimated. A slim
  row whose product is unknown is skipped when it has no category group of its own. Allowance
  and family lookups are cached per distinct key so a whole-state scope stays fast. The
  standalone entry prints usage and exits with code 2 when fewer than two arguments are given.
  All rounding mimics the app's runtime so the regression compare is exact.

Script: Audit Runner  (source role: the command-line runner that resolves the scope, calls the
engine per state, builds the gates and writes the workbook and the JSON summary)
Purpose: Runs a pricing audit for a chain, state or territory and writes the report workbook plus
  a JSON summary for the chat reply. No number is invented: every value traces to a table row,
  and every gap is named.
Inputs: Arguments: the tables folder (default the session's working tables folder), an optional
  single shelf CSV (else the per-territory <shelf price table> CSVs in the tables folder), the
  account (default All), state, territory, category (default All), as-of date (default today),
  the output workbook path (required), an optional JSON summary path and a top-rows cap (default
  400, applied times ten to the Findings sheet). It reads the eight table CSVs the table-pull
  script wrote.
Outputs: The workbook (Summary, Findings, Above <price ceiling>, Gates, Benchmarks, Data Notes),
  the JSON summary file, and a one-line JSON on the console (the summary without the top
  findings and gates). Exit codes: 3 no account matched, 4 ambiguous, 5 no stores in scope.
Logic:
  1. Reads each CSV (a missing file is an empty table) and parses every store row with the
    engine's store parser.
  2. Resolves the account unless it is All: the wanted name is compacted, lowercased and
    stripped of apostrophes, periods and commas; every store whose <account name column> or
    <account level 2 column> contains it votes for its account; with no votes the
    <account level 3 column> and the store name are tried as a last resort. No votes: an error with the hint to
    try the corporate name as the <sales data model> writes it, or a store name, and exit 3.
    More than one account with the leader under 80 percent of the votes: the top eight
    candidates with store counts, and exit 4. Otherwise the leader is the account.
  3. The scope is that account's stores (or all), filtered by state and territory when given;
    an empty scope prints the scope and exits 5. The states, <store ids> and territories in
    scope are collected.
  4. Shelf rows come from the single shelf CSV when given, else from each in-scope territory's
    shelf CSV; a row is kept when its <store id> (column or title key) is in scope.
  5. The engine runs once per state with the state's stores and SKUs, the latest costs for the
    state, the state's allowances, buy-downs and wholesaler options, account All (already
    filtered), the category filter and the as-of date.
  6. Gates are built per state in this order. For each wholesaler serving the stores (most
    stores first): a Cost Proxy row when it is a flagged DC (its proxy, the proxy's prices on
    file, whether costs entered for the DC itself exist and take precedence, and the fix: enter
    the DC's own costs on the app's cost sheet), else a Wholesale Cost row (prices on file,
    families still needed for items that scan, "also the cost proxy for ..." when it stands in
    for a DC, and the top five needed families with store counts). Then a Wholesale Cost row for
    each proxy that serves no store directly. Then Aging Cost when any cost is older than 90
    days (count, first six named with dates); Restricted By Law when Restricted rows exist
    (count applied as zero, programs by <business unit> with counts); Proposed Allowances (count
    awaiting review; not applied until a person confirms them); Allowance Missing (count of
    priced our-item rows with no allowance in force; margin shown gross; check Missing By
    Program Level in the app); No Benchmark per gap category (stores with no priced competitive
    item and the top competitive families with units); Competitive Buy-Downs when none are on
    file; and Pass Price rows per election in scope: <election option E> stores have no
    <price ceiling> by design and are not checked; an election with no <price ceiling> rows in force
    cannot be checked until its pass prices are on file; an election with rows for fewer than
    three families lists them.
  7. Summary sheet rows: the scope text (account, states, territory, category), as-of date,
    stores in scope and with scan data, election mix, findings, Above <price ceiling> count,
    weekly units on findings and on flagged items (13-week units over 13), wholesale costs on
    file (the state total and those for wholesalers serving these stores, proxies included),
    self-distributing DCs with their proxies and store counts, SKU rows priced from a proxy,
    allowance rows in force, Restricted rows, buy-downs on file, the shelf price source, the
    rule in one paragraph (benchmark, triggers, floors, margin formula) and "Read The Gates
    Sheet First" (missing costs mean fewer benchmarks and fewer flags, not fewer opportunities).
  8. Findings sheet: one row per finding with the columns of Reference: Report Format; the ask
    sentence is "<item> rings at <price>; at <parity> it matches <benchmark>'s margin" plus "and
    keeps the <allowance> allowance" when one applies; money to two decimals, percentages to
    one; sorted by weekly units then penny gap; capped at the top-rows setting times ten.
  9. Above <price ceiling> sheet: one row per flag; the read column says "Verify in store: pass
    price changed inside the 13-week window" when the <price ceiling> row took effect fewer than
    91 days before the as-of date, else "Standing"; the 4-week price appears when the shelf rows
    carry it.
  10. Gates sheet; then Benchmarks (per store and finding group: the benchmark item, price and
    margin, or "none priced", with counts of our items priced and flagged); then Data Notes
    (shelf prices, costs, cost proxy, our allowances, competitive buy-downs, parity price, Above
    <price ceiling>, and the engine's regression record).
  11. Styling: a bold light header on a dark fill, a frozen header row, column widths by header;
    the workbook is saved.
  12. The JSON summary (account, states, territory, category, as-of date, stores, scanning
    stores, elections, findings, flags, weekly units, costs serving the scope and on file,
    buy-downs, Restricted rows, proxied SKUs, cost proxies, the top ten findings, the first
    twelve gates, categories with a benchmark, report path) is written when a path is given and
    printed without the top findings and gates.
Checks and failure handling: Missing CSVs become empty tables rather than crashes, so a state
  with no costs still yields the Gates and Above <price ceiling> sheets. An unmatched or
  ambiguous account never falls back to the nearest match (exits 3 and 4). An empty scope exits
  5. Dates that fail to parse count as neither aging nor recent.

Script: Table Pull  (source role: the tool script that mirrors the Pricing Opportunity Agent's
tables from the OneDrive workbooks into CSVs)
Purpose: Pulls the app's data tables and the per-territory shelf price tables through the Excel
  workbook API so the audit runs on fresh local copies.
Inputs: Arguments: the output folder (default the session's working tables folder), an optional
  list of territory codes whose shelf tables to pull (default every territory in the <market>),
  and a skip-shelf switch for when a fresh local copy of the very large shelf tables exists. The
  data workbook and the shelf workbook in OneDrive, identified by their drive and item ids.
Outputs: One CSV per table, named after the table, header row included, in the output folder; a

Logic:
  1. Lists the data workbook's tables (name and id) through the workbook API.
  2. For each of the eight data tables, reads the table's range values and writes them row by

  3. Unless skip-shelf is set, lists the shelf workbook's tables and pulls each in-scope

  4. Returns the folder and the row counts.
Checks and failure handling: Large responses that the tool spills to a file are reloaded from
  that file (a spill marker in the JSON, or a "saved to" note in the text). Missing tables do not

Script: Regression Check  (source role: compares a Python engine run with the oracle run on the
same snapshot)
Purpose: Proves that the Python port agrees with the app's engine field for field.
Inputs: Two JSON files in the comparable shape: the oracle's and the Python engine's.
Outputs: A console report per section (counts on each side, keys present on one side only, field
  differences, OK or FAIL, the first eight differences listed), then "RESULT: PASS" or "RESULT:
  FAIL"; exit 0 on PASS, 1 on FAIL.
Logic:
  1. The summaries must be identical.
  2. Findings, <price ceiling> flags and shelf items are indexed by key; every key must exist on
    both sides and every field must be equal, numbers within 1e-6.
  3. Benchmarks: per store, the map of group to benchmark key must be identical.
  4. Missing rows are keyed by wholesaler and family and compared the same way.
  5. Gaps and cost proxies (the DC-to-proxy pairs) must be identical as whole lists; on a proxy
    mismatch both lists are printed.
  6. The finding order (the sequence of keys) must be identical.
Checks and failure handling: Any failed section fails the whole run; the output names the
  section and the first differences so the port can be corrected before the skill adopts a
  change.

Script: Snapshot Builder  (source role: builds the JSON table snapshot the oracle harness and the
Python engine both consume)
Purpose: Assembles one self-contained snapshot of the tables for a state, optional territory and
  optional account, with either the real costs or a synthetic regression fixture dense enough
  to exercise the benchmark and cost-proxy paths.
Inputs: Arguments: output path (required), state (default the <jurisdiction> on file),
  territory, account (default All), category (default All), a synthetic switch and an as-of
  date (default today). The local CSV mirrors in the session's working folder: raw store rows,
  the shelf price seed, the product seed, the item master, the current allowance, buy-down and
  wholesale cost mirrors, the <wholesaler table> CSV from the table pull, and a supplementary
  self-distributing flag file used when the wholesaler mirror lacks the flag column.
Outputs: The snapshot JSON (as-of date, account, category, territory, stores, SKUs, products,
  items, costs, allowances, buy-downs, wholesaler rows) and a console line with the row count of
  each table and the number of self-distributing wholesalers.
Logic:
  1. Filters stores to the state (and territory when given); keeps the shelf rows of those
    stores; loads products and the item master whole; filters allowances, buy-downs, real costs
    and wholesaler rows to the state, treating a missing buy-down, cost or wholesaler mirror as
    empty.
  2. When the wholesaler rows lack the flag column, the supplementary file supplies it by row
    title. A wholesaler is self-distributing when its flag is a yes-word or, with a blank flag,
    when its type is the retailer-DC-or-club type. The two largest non-self-distributing
    wholesalers by stores served are noted.
  3. Without the synthetic switch the real costs are used as-is.
  4. With the synthetic switch: the set of wholesalers is the two largest plus every wholesaler
    named on an in-scope store for any <business unit> that is not self-distributing. Every
    item-master family (ours and competitive) gets one cost per wholesaler: the family's list
    price (a default per category when the item master has none), its list unit (default
    carton) and its units per carton or roll (default 10) give an invoice price = list price
    times a seeded random uplift between 12 and 35 percent (standing in for excise and markup),
    a derived unit price = invoice over the factor, a fixed effective date, and a source,
    notes and entered-by that label the row as a synthetic regression fixture.
  5. Three sample buy-down rows are appended: one on a <competitor brand 1A> family, one on a
    <competitor brand 4A> family and one "lowest-priced <category 1> in store" row, each with a
    sample amount and the fixture label, so the own-brand and floating buy-down paths both run.
  6. Writes the snapshot and prints the counts.
Checks and failure handling: The fixture is never loaded to the workbook; every synthetic row
  is labelled in its source and notes, and the random generator is seeded so the fixture is
  reproducible. Self-distributing DCs receive no synthetic cost, forcing their stores through
  the cost-proxy path in every scope.

Script: Oracle Library - Program Allowances  (source role: the app's allowance module, copied
into the oracle; the <company> allowance rules plus the app's review and entry functions)
Purpose: Parses the <program allowance table>, resolves the allowance and <price ceiling> row for
  one item, and gives the app its coverage, review-queue and save operations. The engine block
  above ports the resolution part; this block records the module's own definitions.
Inputs: <program allowance table> rows (one per state, <business unit>, program, election, price
  family and effective date); for the app functions, the elections the stores in scope hold,
  the as-of date, a reviewer's decision and name, and new-entry fields.
Outputs: Parsed allowance records; a resolved allowance (amount, applied rows, <price ceiling>
  row); in-force, needed, proposed and restricted listings; coverage gaps; new rows and change
  log entries in the workbook (app only; the oracle stubs the writes).
Logic:
  1. Fixed option lists: the five <business units>; the programs (a per-pack allowance, the
    <price ceiling> row, <allowance type B> in plain, multi-pack-offer and scan
    multi-pack-offer forms, a non-scan allowance, a special price promotion, <allowance type A>
    in non-scan and multi-unit forms, and <allowance type A> paired with the
    <promotional price program>); the elections (<election option A> through <election option E> and All); the
    selling units (pack, can, roll, 5-pack, single).
  2. Status meanings: Confirmed is applied by the run; Proposed was loaded by the weekly
    announcement reader and awaits a person's review, shown everywhere and applied nowhere;
    Needed has no rate on file; Rejected is a proposal a reviewer turned down, kept for the
    audit trail, never applied, hidden from working lists; Restricted cannot be offered in the
    <jurisdiction> by law, shown with its reason, applied as zero, never a gap. Three canned
    restriction reasons: multi-unit discounts prohibited by law, price promotions prohibited by
    law, program not offered in the state.
  3. Row parsing, election normalization, item-match patterns, in-force test, latest-per-program
    with the election-beats-All tie rule, selling-unit match, the two exclusive groups and the
    resolution order are exactly as the engine block, steps 3 and 8 through 10. An item-match
    pattern is documented with a sample: brand words that must appear, a line word with
    alternatives, and a minus word excluding a look-alike product.
  4. Allowances in force: the Confirmed rows in force on the as-of date whose election is held
    by a store in scope (or All), latest per <business unit>, program, election and family,
    sorted by <business unit> order, family, program, election.
  5. Coverage gaps (the app's Missing By Program Level view): every <business unit>, program and
    family the state has rows for is a triple; a triple with any election-specific row is
    checked per election the stores hold, a triple with only All rows is checked once; a pair
    is covered when a Confirmed in-force or Restricted row applies to it; the <price ceiling>
    program under <election option E> is never a gap (that option sets no pass price); each gap
    carries the Needed row's notes or a default sentence, and the list sorts by <business unit>,
    program, family, election order. A gap can be rendered as a Needed row.
  6. Listings: needed rows by <business unit>, family, program; proposed rows newest effective
    date first (the review queue on the state gate); restricted rows by <business unit>,
    family, program, newest first. For a proposal, the Confirmed row it would supersede (same
    state, <business unit>, program, election, family, unit and item scope, latest effective
    date on or before the proposal's) is shown beside it.
  7. Review: the row's status changes in place to Confirmed, Rejected or Restricted, an optional
    note is appended, and the change is logged with the reviewer's name; nothing else on the
    row moves; restoring a restricted row is a Confirmed decision.
  8. Source document short form: a document number and any date found in the source text
    (ISO, long month or US format), else the text itself shortened to 48 characters.
  9. Saving: every entry is a new row (title = state, <business unit>, program, election, family,
    effective date; status Confirmed or Restricted; entered-by and entered-on filled), never an
    overwrite, and each save is logged. Marking a gap as restricted by law writes a new row with
    amount zero, effective today, and the reason as its source and note.
Checks and failure handling: Rows without state, family or program are rejected. A missing
  amount or effective date forces status Needed whatever the status text says. The oracle uses
  only the pure functions; the write paths are stubbed and never reach the workbook.

Script: Oracle Library - Domain Model and Sample Set  (source role: the app's domain types, an
earlier simple margin-parity engine, formatting helpers and the bundled sample scan set)
Purpose: Defines the record shapes the app shares (item, store, scan, wholesale cost, buy-down,
  program buy-down, <price support program> election, state setup, finding, missing cost, run
  result), the name-matching rule and the sample dataset the app shows when no live scan source
  is selected.
Inputs: A dataset of those records, a period (week or month) and an as-of date.
Outputs: Findings sorted by units then penny gap, missing costs sorted by units then store
  count, and the count of stores scanned; formatted money, signed points, percent and period
  labels.
Logic:
  1. Thresholds: a margin-gap trigger of 1 point for <category 1> and 2 points for the other
    three finding categories, and a ten-cent single-unit trigger. Unit cost = invoice price over
    packing factor. A buy-down is active when its effective date is on or before the as-of date
    and its end date is open or later. Two names match when they agree after trimming,
    collapsing spaces and lowercasing.
  2. The simple engine, per store: category units are summed from the store's scans; each scan's
    item is costed from the store's primary wholesaler and the item's family; a competitive
    item's allowance is the sum of its active buy-downs, our item's the sum of program
    buy-downs for the store's election and family; a scan with no cost adds its store and
    category units to the missing list for that wholesaler and family.
  3. Per category the benchmark is the competitive item with the lowest margin. For each of our
    costed items: margin, an above-pass-price amount when a <price support program> election
    row exists for the state, election and family and the price exceeds it by more than $0.001,
    gap points, a parity price equal to net cost divided by one minus the benchmark margin
    (equal-percent parity, unlike the live engine's equal-penny parity), the penny gap, a
    margin opportunity when both the points and the ten-cent trigger are met, and dollars at
    stake = (price minus the target price) times units, the target being the parity price or
    the pass price. An item is reported when it is a margin opportunity or above the pass price.
  4. The sample set is sample rows only: a handful of sample items (ours and competitive across
    the four finding categories), ten sample stores in three sample states with sample
    elections and sample wholesalers, sample scans with prices and weekly and monthly units,
    sample wholesale costs with sample entered-by names, sample buy-downs, sample program
    buy-downs, sample pass prices and a state setup row per state (owners, approval mode of ask
    every run, approved for month or approved until revoked, approval dates, last pricing
    announcement date, wholesalers covered). The <jurisdiction> that runs on real tables has no
    sample stores, allowances or pass prices here, only its state setup row.
Checks and failure handling: The sample set is invented and may never supply a number for the
  <jurisdiction> on real data. Scans whose item is unknown are ignored.

Script: Oracle Library - Shelf Run  (source role: the app's SKU-level shelf module, the original
the pricing engine ports, plus the app's family pick lists)
Purpose: Maps each scanned SKU to the price family a wholesale cost is saved under, runs the
  per-store, per-category margin benchmark and produces findings, <price ceiling> flags, gaps
  and missing prices; also builds the competitive family pick lists for the app's cost picker
  and buy-down form.
Inputs: Shelf SKU rows (one per scan store and SKU from the per-territory <shelf price table>),
  the product master behind them, the item catalog, latest costs, allowances, buy-downs, the
  account and category filters, the as-of date and the wholesaler options.
Outputs: The shelf run in the same shape the engine block describes; family groups for the
  picker; competitive manufacturers and their families for the buy-down form.
Logic:
  1. Category groups, triggers, floors, the our-manufacturer set, <flavor> words, 5-pack words
    and raw-category mapping are as the engine block, step 2.
  2. SKU rows, product rows and enrichment are as step 3; a slim row whose product is unknown
    keeps a placeholder name and stays out of the run because its group is unknown, while a
    full row in the original wide layout is honored as read.
  3. Allowance candidate families are as step 7. The module notes why: the family a
    <business unit 1> allowance or <price ceiling> row is published under (the
    <price support program> sheet family) is finer than the list price group a wholesale cost is saved under,
    so candidates are tried in order and the cost family closes the list so an older row keyed
    by list price group still applies.
  4. Family rules are as step 6. Every competitive family name in the rules is the exact string
    the app's cost picker saves, so a saved cost always matches a scanned SKU.
  5. The run is as steps 11 through 13: the cost index gives one lookup per row even for a
    whole-market run of well over a hundred thousand rows; the self-distributing set, proxy rule
    and DC-to-proxy record; the allowance cache keyed by state, <business unit>, election, unit
    and family list, with the SKU name added only for <business units> that have item-scoped
    rows; the floating lowest-price buy-down; the <price ceiling> flag that applies whether or
    not a cost is known, since cost-reducing allowances only matter once there is a cost to
    reduce; and the ask text.
  6. Pick lists (app only): competitive families from products with sales, largest first, each
    named by the run's own rules and carrying category defaults, then the item master's seed
    families not already present; merged with the catalog's our-family groups once products
    have loaded; and competitive manufacturers with their families, so a buy-down's brand or
    line is the family name the run matches.
Checks and failure handling: Identical to the engine. Without the wholesaler list no wholesaler
  is treated as self-distributing. A row whose SKU name and group are both present skips
  enrichment entirely.

Script: Oracle Library - Stores and Family-Level Run  (source role: the app's store model, its
earlier family-level margin run over store-level realized prices, the <sales data model>'s own
pass-through pass/fail findings and the required price grid)
Purpose: Parses the <store table> and the store-level price table (seven tracked families per
  store), computes the retailer margin per store and tracked family wherever a realized price
  and a wholesale cost both exist, and builds the list of prices every wholesaler should be
  asked for.
Inputs: Store rows; store price rows; latest costs; allowances; the families resolved from the
  catalog; the account and category filters. For the grid: stores, the catalog, costs, the
  shelf run's missing list, the state and the self-distributing names.
Outputs: A family-level run (stores in scope, scan stores, families priced, categories present,
  margin rows, pass/fail findings, missing prices, unknown families, no-wholesaler row count);
  the required price grid; a wholesaler coverage map; accounts by store count.
Logic:
  1. Constants: one <jurisdiction> runs on real stores; every other state opens in setup mode
    (wholesalers, costs, buy-downs and allowances) until store data for it is connected; the
    <market>'s territory list and default territory; 13 scan weeks. The five <business units>
    each map to a display group name. Seven tracked family keys: one <our brand 1A> family
    standing in for the main line, the <our brand 2A> main and value families, the
    <our brand 3A> filter-tip 5-pack (five sticks per selling unit) and single families, one
    <our brand 4A> family blending its two lines, and the <our brand 5A> family; each carries its
    <business unit>, category, default family name, label, unit name, units per selling unit
    and an optional matcher that picks the catalog's spelling when it differs.
  2. Store parsing is as the engine block, step 3. The normalized election column holds the five
    options or "No Election"; the raw column carries long forms such as the two-word
    <election option D> label or opt-out; the raw column is consulted only when the normalized cell is
    blank, so a store loaded in long form still resolves to a rung.
  3. Store price parsing: <store id>, a has-scan flag, per tracked family the price, units and
    dollars (absent when the price is not positive), pass/fail days and volume, and the
    pass-through opportunity.
  4. Resolving families from the catalog: per key, the catalog family matched by the rule within
    the <business unit>'s group, else by the default name; until the catalog has loaded every
    family counts as known; afterwards an unmatched family keeps its default name but is marked
    unknown so its cost shows as not available.
  5. The family-level run, per in-scope store: a pass/fail finding when failed days or failed
    volume are positive (category All or <category 1> only); a store without scan is skipped
    for margins; per tracked family with sales, under the category filter: units divided by
    units per selling unit, price times units per selling unit, the cost from the store's
    <business unit> wholesaler, status priced, no-wholesaler, unknown-family or needed (needed
    rows roll into missing with store count and units), the allowance resolved on the family
    and unit for the store's election when a cost exists, then net cost, penny profit and
    margin, with allowance-missing when priced with no applied rows. Stores without scan add to
    a needed price's store count, never its volume. Margins sort by units; pass/fail findings by
    failed volume then failed days.
  6. The required price grid: every wholesaler is asked for the same prices: for each
    <business unit> it supplies at any store in scope, every tracked our-family of that unit and every
    competitive family in that unit's category. Self-distributing DCs are skipped (their stores
    price from the proxy, which the shelf run already names). Prices already on file are
    excluded; a scanned missing row supplies its units and the larger of the two store counts;
    anything the shelf asked for that the grid did not cover stays; sorted by units, store
    count, wholesaler, family. Scan data ranks the asks but never gates them, so a wholesaler
    whose stores do not scan is still asked for the full list.
  7. Wholesaler coverage counts distinct stores naming each wholesaler for any <business unit>;
    account counts list accounts by store count, largest first.
Checks and failure handling: Rows without a <store id> are dropped. Unknown families are
  reported by name, never guessed. Oracle use is limited to the store parser; the rest serves
  the app.

Script: Oracle Library - Workbook Storage  (source role: the app's storage layer over the data
workbook's tables, with the connector stubbed in the oracle)
Purpose: Reads and writes the tables (state setup, wholesale costs, buy-downs, change log,
  wholesalers, item master, stores, store prices, products, allowances) through the Excel Online
  connector, parses cost, buy-down and wholesaler rows, and builds the item catalog.
Inputs: The data workbook and the shelf workbook in the same OneDrive folder (the per-territory
  shelf tables live apart because the data workbook passed 20 MB with them inside and the
  connector stopped answering large reads); table names; rows and entry fields.
Outputs: Pages of rows, appended rows, patched rows and change-log entries (app only); cost
  versions; wholesaler options; the item catalog with family groups, families by name,
  manufacturers and brands by manufacturer.
Logic:
  1. Every table's first column is the title and is the key for updates. Four storage
    operations only: read a page, add a row, update a row by title, append to the change log.
    Reads ask for ISO dates; serial dates are still converted (days since 1899-12-30). Page size
    100; aging threshold 90 days; age text helpers (effective today, n days old, age unknown).
  2. Read a page: blank starter rows (empty title) are dropped after the read, and page fullness
    is judged on the raw page. Large tables try page sizes 1000, 256 and 100 in order; a
    transient refusal is retried once after a 400 millisecond pause before the size steps down;
    a short page is probed once more so a silently capped page size is not mistaken for the end,
    and reading continues at the capped size; the size that worked is remembered for the rest
    of the session; the row cap defaults to 20,000 with a truncated flag. Read-all follows the
    skip continuation page by page up to a page bound.
  3. Add and patch surface a plain "unable to save" or "unable to update" error on connector
    failure. The change log is best effort (a failed log entry never undoes the saved row) and
    records timestamp, user, state, table, row key, field, old and new value and action.
  4. Cost versions: every saved price is a new row titled state, wholesaler, family and
    effective date; a listing shows the latest per group with the count of earlier versions
    (a tie goes to the row appended later); workbook versions overlay the bundled sample costs
    for the same group and add groups the sample set lacks; a saved row carries the derived
    unit price rounded to four decimals and status Active; a save appends and logs a summary of
    price per invoice unit, units and unit cost; "Still Current" re-saves the same values with
    today's effective date.
  5. Buy-down parsing is as the engine block, step 3. A wholesaler typed as "other" joins the
    state's list as an active user-added row, and is logged.
  6. Wholesaler options and the self-distributing verdict are as the engine; setting or clearing
    the flag writes the row's flag column and logs the change, and the engine re-reads it on
    the next run.
  7. Category mapping, category defaults and the catalog build are as the engine block, step 5,
    plus family groups ordered by <business unit> order and then by competitive category,
    families sorted by name within a group, and a sorted manufacturer list.
Checks and failure handling: Connector errors are logged and surfaced as plain errors. The
  oracle's stub throws on any connector call, so only the pure parsing and catalog functions
  run during a regression.
