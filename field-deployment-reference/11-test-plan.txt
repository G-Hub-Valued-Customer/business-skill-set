Part 11 of 11: Test Plan
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Test Plan
=========

Purpose: Ten tests, numbered 0 to 9, that a tester runs against the bundle to verify identity
resolution, routing, scope and each skill's output, with a checklist to send back to <author>.

Triggers: A tester holding the skills bundle zip wants to validate the bundle; the test prompts
themselves ("before you run anything, tell me who you think I am...", "which skill would you use
for each of these...", "what are my contract opportunities", and the others below).

Inputs:
- The skills bundle zip, attached first in a new Cowork session for every test.
- The tester's own login (never stated) and their knowledge of stores in their own geography;
  there is no answer key.
- For Test 2, the market's <planning workbook> if Copilot asks for it; for Test 6, the
  <SKU volume report> export, label stock, a color printer and a store scanner.

Outputs:
- A pass or fail judgment per test against its Pass line, plus the checklist at the end, sent to
  <author email>; workbooks attached only if the tester is comfortable sharing them.

Parameters to Fill In:
- <author>, <author email> - who receives the checklist.
- <sales org>, <company> - the organization whose roles the prompts cover and the tenant whose
  Power BI workspace holds the <SKU volume report>.
- <territory role>, <market support role>, <market role>, <section role>, <region role>,
  <account role> - the role titles in the role-specific expectations.
- <store id>, <store ids> - the store identifier label used in prompts and the brief preview.
- <business unit>, <business unit 3>, <business unit 4> - the units named in the label and
  contract tests.
- <our brand 4A>, <our brand 3A> - the brands the contract audit describes per store;
  <our brand 1A> <variant> - the product in the one-off margin question.
- <photo platform>, <shelf recognition model>, <CRM>, "<CRM> visit report widget",
  <SKU volume report> - the systems named in the expected behavior.
- <sign type 1> - the state signage guide the POS scorecard reads before querying.
- <chain D>, <jurisdiction>, <wholesaler A> - the chain, state and wholesaler in the pricing audit
  test.
- <price ceiling>, <price support program> - the pass price and program in the pricing audit
  expectations.
- <retail price>, <wholesale cost>, <allowance amount> - the sample pack price, carton cost and
  per-pack allowance the tester supplies for the one-off margin question.

Test Cases
----------

Each test is one new Cowork session with the skills bundle zip attached. The tester types the
prompt exactly as written, judges the result against the Pass line, fills in the checklist at the
bottom and sends it to <author>. The prompts work for any <sales org> role; where the role changes
what to expect, the test says so. There is no answer key in the package on purpose: it carries no
store lists or results for any territory, so it can be forwarded to anyone; the tester judges each
result against stores they know.

Rules for a fair test:

- A new session for every test; routing and identity are the things under test.
- Attach the zip first, then type the prompt; nothing else in the message.
- Never tell Copilot who you are or which skill to use. If it asks either question, that is a
  failure of the thing being tested: note it, answer so the run continues, and note whether the
  rest worked.
- If Copilot describes the folder instead of starting, note it, then use the explicit form from
  the Read Me and finish the test.

Test 0: Who Am I (3 Minutes):

Prompt: "before you run anything, tell me who you think I am, what my span of control is, and what
scope each of the eight skills would use for me by default".

Pass: the tester's correct role and geography (or chains, or "headquarters") without asking
anything, taken from the login; default scopes matching the "Who You Are Is Read from Your Login"
table in the Read Me; for a headquarters role, a statement that it would ask the tester to name a
scope. Nothing is run.

Fail: it asks who the tester is; it guesses a territory or market the tester does not hold; it
treats a <market support role> or a <market role> as a single territory; it gives an <account role>
a field region instead of their chains; it gives a headquarters person a default scope instead of
asking.

Test 1: Routing Only (5 Minutes):

Prompt: "which skill would you use for each of these, and why: 'give me my top stores', 'help me
with my 2H objectives', 'what are my contract opportunities', 'which of my stores are missing
signs', 'rebuild my <business unit 3> label sheet', 'I'd like to enable daily briefings'. Do not
run anything."

Pass: Top Opportunity Stores, Performance Write-Up Concierge, Contract Compliance Audit, POS Gap
Scorecard, Business Unit Label Sheets, Daily Store Brief, in that order, each with a reason drawn
from the router table. Anything else is a routing miss; the tester copies the answer into the
notes.

Test 2: Visit List (10 Minutes):

Field roles (<territory role>, <market support role>, <market role>, <section role>,
<region role>) type: "give me my top stores to visit for <this month>". Account and headquarters
roles type, naming a geography they know well: "give me the top stores to visit in
<territory or market number> for <this month>". If Copilot asks for the cycle's <planning workbook>, the tester
attaches the market's copy (it will not build a list without one) and notes whether it found the
file on its own.

Pass: one Excel workbook with the shape for the scope (Top 10 + Next 25 for one territory; Top 20 +
Next 50 for a market or wider), a Priorities tab and a Method tab; every store carries volume
numbers and sits inside the scope. Judged against what the tester knows: the three or four biggest
opportunity stores they can name sit in the top group, and a store they know is closed or tiny
does not. The Priorities tab may weight the supervisor's recent emails; for a tester outside a
field chain of command it must say plainly that no manager directives were available rather than
invent any.

Fail: no volume numbers; stores outside the scope; invented manager directives; the wrong shape for
the scope.

Test 3: Contract Audit (About 2 Minutes a Store; You Can Leave):

Prompt, for everyone: "what are my contract opportunities".

What should happen, in order:

1. Copilot states the scope. A <territory role> gets the regular independents of the territory. Any
  wider role gets the store count and the hours and is offered a cut before anything starts; the
  tester accepts one territory, or answers with a short list: "just these <store ids>:
  <six to ten store numbers you know>". A headquarters role is asked once to name a scope and does the same.
2. It pulls the stores, their assigned contract levels and their latest <photo platform> photo
  links from the <shelf recognition model> and says how many have a current photo.
3. It uploads a request file to the shared bridge folder and says the photos are being fetched.
  Nothing to click; the bridge flow wakes within about five minutes and Copilot notices on its own.
  If it reports that the bridge is not running, the tester stops the test, records that, and tells
  <author>.
4. It scores the photos, about 1.7 minutes a store with four agents in parallel, reporting progress
  every few stores. If the session disconnects, the tester returns to the same conversation and
  says "continue"; it must resume, not restart.
5. One Excel workbook: an Overview tab with a scorecard and a priority list, a Store List tab with
  one row per store, and a Method tab.

How to judge it without an answer key: for three stores the tester has stood in this cycle,
compare Copilot's description of the <our brand 4A> section (width, number of shelves, position,
header) and of the <our brand 3A> facings with what the tester saw. The verdict must follow from
that read and the assigned level; the next step must be something the tester could actually do in
the store.

Rules that must hold for every store: linear feet = section width x shelves where product can be
merchandised, each shelf once (a standard 2 ft bay of 5 shelves is 10 ft; strips, hanging signs,
headers and panels over another brand add nothing); every store gets a verdict and a next step; a
store below its contract is a reclaim-space finding with the fix named, never suppressed; a store
that could reach a higher level has the path named; a store whose set is not in the photo is
Unclear, not guessed.

Fail: any 12 ft or 14 ft reading on a 2 ft bay; "no path" where a simple fix exists; a photo that
was not fetched being scored anyway; the run starting on a market or wider without stating the
count and proposing a cut.

Test 4: POS Gaps (10 to 15 Minutes):

Field roles type: "what POS is missing in my stores". Account and headquarters roles type: "what
POS is missing in <territory or market number>".

Pass: the scope line first; one Excel workbook, one row per store, green check where POS is
present, red X for a real gap, yellow where the store could not be assessed, with a failure code in
the margin for every yellow or red. Before querying, Copilot says which POS measures are scored
this cycle (it reads the <sign type 1> state guide). A <market role> or <market support role> gets
the whole market; a <territory role> gets their territory.

Fail: red X on a store with no photo this cycle (that should be yellow); no failure codes; measures
nobody scores; a <market support role> scoped to a single territory.

Test 5: Objectives (10 Minutes):

Prompt: "help me with my 2H objectives".

Pass: Copilot looks the tester up, states their role and manager, asks them to confirm scope and
whether they are setting or tracking 2H objectives, then produces draft objectives and key results
that ladder to headquarters priorities for their level. It must not invent results or numbers the
tester did not give it; where it has no data it says so and leaves a marked placeholder.

Fail: objectives written for a different role than the tester's; invented metrics.

Test 6: Label Sheets (10 Minutes):

Prompt: "rebuild my <business unit 3> label sheet".

Pass: Copilot asks for the <SKU volume report> export from <company>'s Power BI workspace rather
than silently using national data, then delivers a PDF. Every label carries a product name and a
barcode. It states the <business unit>, sheet and label counts, the ranking source and its date,
which SKUs it pinned and why, any new item defaulted to one label, and that the die registration
check passed. It says to print at 100 percent, not Fit to Page. The tester then prints one sheet on
the label stock and scans several barcodes with a store's scanner.

Fail: it builds from national data without saying so; a label is missing its barcode; it invents a
UPC for a SKU it could not find; content prints outside the die cuts; a barcode will not scan.

The tester reports separately whether the printed sheet aligned and whether every barcode scanned;
that is the part never yet verified.

Test 7: Pricing Agent (Routing and Explanation, 5 Minutes; Build Only If Asked):

Prompt: "what is the pricing agent and what does it need from me to run for my market".

Pass: Copilot routes to the Pricing Opportunity Agent builder and answers from its SKILL.md without
starting a build: the one rule (the lowest competitive retailer margin per store and category is
the benchmark; <company>'s items above it by the trigger or penny floor are findings; the parity
price is the benchmark penny profit plus <company>'s net cost), the four inputs and who supplies
each, that only wholesale invoice cost and competitive buy-downs are ever typed in by a person, and
that seeding a new market first needs SKU-level scan access proven and the data team told the query
cost. It names the Managed App as the thing account teams actually open.

Second prompt, which should NOT trigger the skill: "a store sells <our brand 1A> <variant> at
<retail price> a pack, wholesale is <wholesale cost> a carton, <allowance amount> pack allowance.
margin?"

Pass: Copilot answers the arithmetic inline (unit cost = carton cost divided by ten packs; net cost
= unit cost minus the per-pack allowance; penny profit = retail price minus net cost; margin =
penny profit divided by retail price) with no workbook, no data pull and no question about which
market.

Fail: it starts pulling data or building workbooks for the first prompt; it asks who the tester
is; it invents a buy-down or a store; it fires the full skill for the second prompt.

Test 8: Pricing Audit (Run, 3 to 5 Minutes):

Prompt: "audit my <chain D> stores in <jurisdiction> for pricing".

Pass: Copilot routes to the Pricing Audit, resolves the chain to its account hierarchy name without
asking, pulls the tables, runs <jurisdiction>, and replies in this order: scope and coverage
(stores, scanning stores, <price support program> election mix); the gates when they limit the
answer (at the time of writing: no wholesale costs on file for <wholesaler A>, so no margin
findings yet, and the first five families to price); the count above the <price ceiling> with the
verify-in-store caveat when the pass price changed inside the 13-week window; what would widen the
answer; the workbook name. The workbook has six sheets (Summary, Findings, Above <price ceiling>,
Gates, Benchmarks, Data Notes). No number is estimated.

Second prompt, "add <jurisdiction> to the pricing agent" (naming a second state), should route to
the Pricing Opportunity Agent builder, not the Pricing Audit.

Test 9: Daily Store Brief (Two Prompts, 10 Minutes; Nothing Is Emailed):

Written for a <territory role> with planned <CRM> visits; other roles run it for the routing and
the stop only.

Prompt one: "I'd like to enable daily briefings".

Pass: Copilot routes to the Daily Store Brief and walks the tester through the <CRM> home page (the
<CRM> visit report widget fields and filters, a daily Monday to Friday subscription named "Daily
Schedule" at a time the tester chooses) plus the OneDrive Desktop and Documents backup check, then
says to come back and report what time the email is set for. It installs, builds and schedules
nothing yet. It asks nothing of the tester's supervisor.

Prompt two (after the subscription is saved; the test can run the same day): "okay, the email is
all set up for 5:00 AM".

Pass: Copilot finds the planner and cycle files in OneDrive or the inbox without asking for
uploads, builds the ranking and per-store content, schedules the runs around the tester's time
(Monday to Saturday, nothing on Sunday), shows a preview brief for the next selling day that lists
only that day's stops in start-time order with store header and <store id>, and says the first
real brief arrives the next selling morning about 30 minutes after the <CRM> email. Nothing is
emailed during the test.

Fail: it asks who the tester is or which territory they cover; it puts any step on the supervisor;
it asks for a planner upload it could have found; it schedules a Sunday run or a Saturday brief;
the preview shows stops from more than one day or out of start-time order; it emails anyone but the
tester.

What to Send <author>:

For each test, the checklist:

- Test number
- Your role
- Knew who I was without asking (yes / no / asked me)
- Routed to the right skill without being told (yes / no / asked me)
- Scope it stated
- Finished (yes / no / where it stopped)
- Matched the Pass line (yes / partly / no)
- Time from prompt to result
- Anything it said that was wrong or odd (pasted)

Workbooks are attached only if the tester is comfortable sharing them; the checklist alone is
enough. Send to <author>, <author email>.
