Generic Workflow Scaffold - Field Sales Copilot Skills
======================================================

Prepared October 3, 2026. Company-neutral rewrite of a bundle of eight Copilot Cowork skills, their routing
hub, a one-time photo bridge flow and a test plan. Every step, decision rule, input, output,
tie-breaker, failure path and verification step of the original has been kept in its original
order. Every company-specific detail has been replaced by a placeholder in angle brackets so the
same workflows can be rebuilt for another company, another product set and another data estate.

What This File Is
-----------------

- One plain-text document, ASCII only, with a page break before each part. Eleven parts: the hub,
  the eight skills in the order the original bundle listed them, the photo bridge flow, and the
  test plan.
- A specification, not a skill. It explains what the assistant does at each step and why, in the
  third person. Turning a part back into a working skill means writing its steps in the imperative
  for the assistant, supplying the parameters listed in that part's header block, and testing it
  against the test plan in the last part.
- Nothing in this file names the original company, its business units, brands, competitors,
  internal systems, data model tables, people, geographies, identifiers, links or commercial
  terms. Microsoft platform names (Copilot, Cowork, OneDrive, SharePoint, Outlook, Teams, Excel,
  Word, Power BI, Power Automate, Power Apps, Dataverse) are kept because the workflows run on
  that platform. Generic retail and merchandising words (facings, linear feet, shelf, header,
  backbar, planogram, paddle, POS, UPC, carton, roll, 5-pack, out-of-stock, distribution,
  assortment, margin, wholesale cost, allowance, buy-down, parity price) are kept because any
  consumer-goods sales organization uses them.

How to Read It
--------------

Headings. A title with a line of "=" under it starts a part. A title with a line of "-" under it
is a major section of that part. A title ending with a colon is a sub-section and mirrors a
heading of the original skill, in the original order. Where the original nested deeper, the
sub-section name carries its parent's name joined by " - ".

Header block. Every part opens with five lines: Purpose, Triggers (what the user says), Inputs
(what the skill reads and where it lives), Outputs (what the user gets) and Parameters to Fill In
(every placeholder a new company must supply for that part). The header block is the only
glossary inside a part; the body relies on the placeholder's name and the sentence around it.

Scripts. Code has been removed. Each helper script of the original appears as a block of Purpose,
Inputs, Outputs, Logic (plain numbered steps) and Checks and failure handling. Embedded test
fixtures are described as sample rows and never reproduced.

Numbers. Numbers that describe the method stay as written: list shapes such as Top 10 plus Next
25 and Top 20 plus Next 50, the 13-week business window, the Saturday refresh, the 5:15 AM send,
1.7 minutes per store for photo scoring, 1 x 2 inch labels, time caps, retry counts and page
sizes. Numbers that describe the business are placeholders: contract tier thresholds, required
facing counts, allowance amounts, price points, store counts for a named geography, dates that
are facts about the original company's history.

Placeholder Conventions
-----------------------

Placeholders are lowercase descriptive words inside angle brackets. Plurals and possessives attach
outside the brackets: <business units>, <company>'s. Lettered placeholders distinguish members of
a set: <our brand 2A> is the first of our brands in product category 2; <competitor brand 2A> is
the first competitor brand in that category. Letters for example chains, wholesalers, price
families, sign types beyond the first three, and headquarters objectives are local to the part in
which they appear.

Glossary of Placeholders
------------------------

Company and organization:
- <company> - the manufacturer or parent company whose field sales organization runs these workflows
- <company domain> - the company's email and login domain
- <sales org> - the company's sales and distribution organization, the audience for every skill
- <business unit 1> through <business unit 5> - the company's operating units, one per product
  category; <business unit N> sells <category N>
- <business unit>, <business units> - any or all of the operating units
- <business unit 6>, <our brand 6A> - a sixth, lowest-weighted operating unit or partner product
  line that some skills carry in their weighting tables, if the company has one

Products:
- <category 1> through <category 5> - the five product categories the sales organization sells
- <category group A>, <category group B> - the two broad groups the categories divide into
- <industry> - the industry the company operates in; "<industry> products" is the whole range
- <adult consumer> - the legal-age consumer of the company's products
- <our brand NX> - one of the company's brands in category N; X is a letter within the category
- <competitor brand NX> - a competitor's brand in category N
- <unauthorized competitor product> - competitor products sold without regulatory authorization
- <price family A>, <price family B> - price tiers or families within a brand; letters local to the part
- <variant>, <flavor>, <strength>, <pack style>, <price tier> - product variant descriptors

Programs and trade terms:
- <loyalty program> - the retailer loyalty program whose store targets feed the daily brief
- <consumer rewards program> - a consumer points program
- <promotional price program> - a multi-unit or promotional price program whose pass-through is checked
- <monthly POS kit> - the monthly POS kit communication from headquarters
- <trade announcement> - the periodic trade program announcement carrying allowances and UPCs
- <digital trade program> - the digital trade contract program, with its tier and allowance
- <price support program> - the retail price support program a store elects into
- <election option A> through <election option F> - the price support program's election choices
- <price ceiling> - the pass price a supported item must not exceed
- <allowance type A>, <allowance type B>, <allowance type C> - the per-unit allowance types paid
  under the trade programs
- <allowance amount>, <price point> - a dollar amount from the current trade announcement or shelf
- <tier 1>, <tier 2>, <tier code> - contract levels and the codes the data carries for them
- <tier N threshold>, <required facings> - the space or facing count a contract level requires
- <sign type 1> through <sign type N> - the POS sign programs the scorecard checks
- <volume index> - the store volume measure and its decile
- <HQ objective A>, <HQ objective B>, <HQ metric target> - headquarters objectives and targets
- <sales incentive plan>, <incentive metric>, <incentive metric A> through <incentive metric J>,
  <weight> - the field sales incentive compensation program, its metric names and payout weights
- <behaviors framework>, <behavior 1> through <behavior 5> - the company's named set of rated
  behaviors on the performance review form
- <HR system> - the HR performance management system that holds objectives and the review form

Systems and data:
- <CRM> - the field CRM where planned visits live, with its analytics dashboard and visit report widget
- <photo platform> - the store photo capture platform; "the photo bridge flow" moves its photos into OneDrive
- <shelf recognition model> - the Power BI model built from recognized shelf photos: contract levels,
  POS presence, store attributes and the sales hierarchy
- <data warehouse> - the enterprise data warehouse that holds 13-week business data
- <sales data model> - the sales semantic model that holds shelf prices
- <planning workbook> - the cycle's store-level planning workbook from the user's manager
- <market cycle workbook> - the market-level cycle workbook the daily brief's discovery stages
  alongside the monthly planner
- <SKU volume report> - the exportable SKU volume ranking report
- <distribution gap export> - the export listing items a store should carry but does not
- <loyalty program target list> - the store target list for the loyalty program
- <report id>, <dataset id>, <workspace id> - Power BI identifiers
- <shared folder link> - the link to the shared skills folder
- <label stock code> - the off-the-shelf die-cut label stock product code
- descriptive table and column placeholders such as <store profile table>, <store id column> -
  objects in the company's data models, named by what they hold

People and roles:
- <user> - the person running the skill; <user's manager> - that person's manager
- <author>, <author email>, <author alias> - the skills' author and maintainer
- <territory role> - the field role that owns one territory
- <market role> - the role that manages a market of territories
- <market support role> - the merchandising support role attached to a market
- <section role> - the role that directs a section of markets
- <region role> - the role that leads a region of sections
- <account role> - the roles that manage retail chains rather than geography
- <HQ role> - home-office roles with no sales geography
- <support role> - support and administrative roles attached to a geography
- <colleague> - any other named person

Geography and identifiers:
- <market>, <market id>, <territory id>, <section id>, <region id> - geography levels and identifiers
- <store id> - the retail account identifier every data source keys on
- <jurisdiction> - a state or jurisdiction with a legal restriction on promotions
- <chain A>, <chain B>, <wholesaler A>, <wholesaler B> - example chains and wholesalers; letters
  local to the part
- <city>, <zip> - example locations
- <n>, <date>, <version> - a count, a date or a version that was a fact about the original

Run-time tokens (not company parameters):
- <scope>, <state>, <month>, <year>, <m>, <reason>, <yyyy-mm-dd>, <run id> - slots inside a file
  name pattern, a report line or a quoted message that the running skill fills at run time; a
  state here is a plain geography field, not a legal jurisdiction
