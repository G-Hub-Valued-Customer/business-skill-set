Hub: Bundle Overview, Router and Scope Resolution
=================================================

Purpose: The hub is the entry point of a bundle of eight Copilot Cowork skills built for every role
in <company>'s field sales organization, <sales org>. It tells the assistant how to route a request
to one skill, stage and read it, resolve who is asking from the login and what they own, and open
every answer with a scope line; it tells a person how to invoke the bundle and what comes back.

Triggers: The user attaches the skills bundle zip to a new Cowork session, or pastes the
<shared folder link> on one line and the request on the next, or has installed the skill folders
under the OneDrive folder Documents/Cowork/skills and simply asks. Phrasings route by meaning:
"give me my top stores" (Top Opportunity Stores), "what are my contract opportunities" (Contract
Compliance Audit), "which of my stores are missing signs" (POS Gap Scorecard), "help me with my 2H
objectives" (Performance Write-Up Concierge), "rebuild my <business unit 3> label sheet" (Business
Unit Label Sheets), "audit my <chain name> stores for pricing" (Pricing Audit), "seed the pricing
agent for my market" (Pricing Opportunity Agent builder), "I'd like to enable daily briefings"
(Daily Store Brief). The explicit form "Follow the START HERE file in the <skill folder> of the
attached zip and <what you want>" always works.

Inputs:
- The skills bundle zip in the session's attachments, or the <shared folder link> resolved to the
  shared folder in <author>'s OneDrive.
- The user's request: whatever follows the link or the attachment, in the user's own words.
- The signed-in user's profile, read from the people directory, for the user principal name.
- The <shelf recognition model> in Power BI (report <report id>): its <user login table>,
  <sales employee geography table>, <store hierarchy table> and <store profile table>.
- The chosen skill's SKILL.md plus everything under its references/ and scripts/ folders.

Outputs:
- Exactly what the chosen skill's Output section describes, in the output pane, opening with one
  line in the form "Scope: <scope>, <reason>"; for a request about routing itself, the routing
  answer from the table with nothing run.
- Nothing is emailed or posted to Teams unless the user asks.

Parameters to Fill In:
- <company>, <company domain> - the company and its sign-in domain; the alias is the part of the
  user principal name before the @ sign.
- <sales org> - the field sales organization the skills serve.
- <author>, <author email> - who maintains the bundle, owns the photo bridge flow and the shared
  folders, and receives defects and suggestions.
- <shared folder link> - the shared OneDrive folder holding the skill folders and a zip of each.
- <shelf recognition model>, <report id> - the Power BI model (and its report id) holding store
  profiles, photos, recognized POS and the directory tables used for role resolution.
- <user login table> - the directory table keyed by login alias, not clipped by row-level
  security; its <login alias column>, <user role column>, <geography type column>,
  <geography code column>, <market id column>, <section id column>, <region id column>,
  <employee title column> and <employee title code column>.
- <sales employee geography table> - the fallback directory keyed by domain user name, with title,
  assignment type and sales geography code; <network domain> - the prefix before the alias there.
- <store hierarchy table> - the row-level-secured table of stores under each geography, account
  and <market support role> assignment, with level 0 to level 5 user name columns.
- <store profile table> - the store master whose chain name column names an account person's
  chains.
- <field region ids>, <account region ids> - which region ids are field sales and which are the
  account organization.
- <territory role>, <market support role>, <market role>, <section role>, <region role>,
  <account role>, <HQ role>, <support role> - the company's titles at each level.
- <market>, <market id>, <territory id>, <section id>, <region id>, <store id>, <store ids> - how
  geographies and stores are named in scope lines and requests.
- <business unit>, <business units>, <business unit 3>, <business unit 4> - the operating units,
  and the two whose contracts the audit scores; <our brand 3A>, <our brand 4A>,
  <competitor brand 4A> - the brands the audit measures.
- <category 1>, <price support program>, <price ceiling>, <trade announcements> - the category,
  program, pass price and announcements the pricing skills use.
- <CRM> - the CRM whose visit report widget sends the Daily Schedule email; <data warehouse> - the
  store-level source of 13-week data; <photo platform> - the store photo service.
- <planning workbook>, <distribution gap export>, <loyalty program>, <loyalty program target list>
  - the cycle inputs of the visit list and the Daily Store Brief.
- <SKU volume report>, <label stock code> - the inventory report export that drives label facings
  and the 1 x 2 inch die-cut label stock product code.
- <sign type 1>, <sign type 2>, <sign type 3> - the state signage guide and two named sign programs
  that route to the POS Gap Scorecard.
- <jurisdiction> - a state as a legal jurisdiction in pricing requests; <chain name> - a slot for
  any chain named in a request; <chain A> to <chain E> - example chains.
- <user's manager> - the user's supervisor, whose emails carry priorities.
- <n> - any count quoted for a named geography; <date> - dates in the bundle's history.

Bundle Overview
---------------

Eight Copilot Cowork skills built by <author>, a <territory role> in <market>, for everyone in
<sales org>: <territory role>s, <market support role>s, <market role>s, <section role>s,
<account role>s and their directors, <region role>s and headquarters. Nothing to install. Version
<date>. When the Read Me reaches the assistant inside an attached zip, the assistant opens START
HERE - all skills.md in the same folder and follows its "If You Are Copilot" section; it does not
describe the folder to the user but runs the request.

What Is Here:

Each row: folder or file: what it is.

- START HERE - all skills.md: the router; a fresh Cowork session reads it, works out who is asking,
  picks the skill that fits and runs it.
- TEST SCRIPT.md: the prompts to try, what should come back, what to report.
- The photo bridge flow setup guide: one-time setup of the flow the owner builds once in Power
  Automate, after which photos fetch by themselves for everyone.
- Contract Compliance Audit: <business unit 4> (<our brand 4A>) and <business unit 3>
  (<our brand 3A>) contract compliance from <photo platform> store photos.
- Top Opportunity Stores: ranked store visit list from the cycle's <planning workbook>.
- POS Gap Scorecard: color-coded POS presence scorecard from the <shelf recognition model>.
- Performance Write-Up Concierge: set objectives, track progress, write mid-year and year-end.
- Business Unit Label Sheets: print-ready out-of-stock placeholder labels for any <business unit>
  with real scannable reorder barcodes.
- Pricing Audit: the Pricing Opportunity Agent's audit run from chat for a chain, state or
  territory, in one workbook; same engine as the app, verified against it; for account teams and
  managers.
- Pricing Opportunity Agent (Builder): builds, seeds, repairs and ports the Managed App that audits
  retailer margin on <company>'s items against the lowest-margin competitive item per store and
  category; for builders and owners.
- Daily Store Brief and Cycle Autopilot: every selling morning a phone-friendly email of that day's
  planned <CRM> visits; every Saturday it finds the new cycle's planner and inputs in OneDrive and
  the inbox by their columns (other store-keyed exports ride along as Other Cycle Data) and
  rebuilds itself. "I'd like to enable daily briefings" starts it.

Every skill folder holds SKILL.md (plus references/ and scripts/ where present) and a START HERE.md
for a person: what to say, other ways to ask, what comes back, how long it takes. The package is
instructions only, with no store lists, results or reports for any territory, market or person, so
it can be forwarded to anyone in <sales org>; whatever the user runs stays in their own session and
files, clipped by Power BI to what they may see.

How to Use the Skills:

Three ways, same result: attach the zip to a new Cowork session and ask in plain words; paste the
<shared folder link> on one line and the request on the next, nothing else; or install permanently
by copying the eight skill folders into the OneDrive folder Documents/Cowork/skills so each
SKILL.md sits in its own subfolder, then just ask in any session. The user asks as they would ask
a person; "give me my top stores", "help me with my 2H objectives" and "which of my stores are
missing signs" all route. If Copilot describes the folder instead of starting, the explicit form
is: Follow the START HERE file in the <skill folder> of the attached zip and <what you want>.

Who You Are Is Read from Your Login:

The user never says who they are. Copilot takes the login, finds the person in the <sales org>
directory inside the <shelf recognition model>, and uses their span of control as the default
scope. Anyone can name a different scope in the request, and the request always wins.

| You are | "My" means | Contract audit default | Visit list shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| <territory role> | your territory | the regular independents in your territory | Top 10 + Next 25 |
| <market support role> | your whole market | your market; Copilot proposes a cut first (one territory at a time, or contracted independents only) | Top 20 + Next 50 |
| <market role> | your market | same as above | Top 20 + Next 50 |
| <section role> | your section | your section; always a cut first | Top 20 + Next 50 |
| <region role> | your region | your region; always a cut first | Top 20 + Next 50 |
| <account role> (every account title, from specialist to the head of account sales) | your chains (every store whose account hierarchy carries your name) | your chains; a cut first (one chain) above a few hundred stores | Top 20 + Next 50 |
| <HQ role> (the chief executive, communications and every home-office role with no sales geography) | nothing by default, even if your name sits at the top of a chain of command | name the scope: "in territory <territory id>", "for <market>", "for <section id>", "for <chain name>", or a <store id> list; Copilot asks once if you do not | the shape of the geography you name |
| <support role> (support and administrative roles attached to a geography) | that geography | same as the field role at that level | that level's shape |

Every answer opens with one line stating the scope used and why, "Scope: <scope>, <reason>" (for
example "Scope: <market>, your market as <market role>, <n> territories"). Above one market every
skill states the count and offers to run market by market before it starts; the contract audit
does so already above one territory (one market alone is <n> contracted independents, roughly <n>
hours of photo scoring at 1.7 minutes a store). Row-level security clips every query to what the
user may see, so a wrong default can only under-deliver, never show stores outside their
permissions. Scopes a request can name: a territory, market, section or region number, a chain
name, "my business development prospects" (kept separate from regular independents), "my chains",
a pasted <store id> list, or an attached list.

What You Need Access To:

Each row: skill: what it needs.

- Top Opportunity Stores: the cycle's <planning workbook> if the market has one (attached, or the
  user says where it lives), otherwise the Power BI model; reads the user's own mailbox for
  <user's manager>'s priorities.
- Contract Compliance Audit: read access to the <shelf recognition model>, which every field and
  account role has; photos come through the photo bridge flow, owned by <author>, into a shared
  folder everyone at <company> can use, nothing to install, click or be granted.
- POS Gap Scorecard: the same model; no photos, no app.
- Performance Write-Up Concierge: nothing; bring reports or nothing.
- Business Unit Label Sheets: one file, the <SKU volume report> from <company>'s Power BI
  workspace filtered to the user's geography and exported to Excel (without it facings fall back to
  national averages, which differ materially); a color printer and off-the-shelf 1 x 2 inch
  die-cut label stock (<label stock code>).
- Daily Store Brief and Cycle Autopilot: nothing beyond the user's login. A one-time setup Copilot
  walks the user through on "I'd like to enable daily briefings": a <CRM> visit report widget email
  subscription named "Daily Schedule" (every remaining planned visit, daily Monday to Friday, at
  the time the user chooses) and OneDrive backup of Desktop and Documents; the monthly planner,
  <distribution gap export> and <loyalty program target list> are found where the user already
  saves them. Reads the user's inbox, OneDrive, calendar and the <data warehouse>.

The bridge flow has run since <date>; nobody sets anything up. Its setup guide is in the bundle
only so the flow can be rebuilt if its owner ever changes.

When Something Goes Wrong:

- Copilot lists the folder or asks which skill: use the explicit form above.
- Copilot asks a field role who they are or which territory they cover: a defect; the user tells
  <author> their role and what it asked.
- "No rows" or "no stores found" from Power BI: the user's access does not cover that scope; the
  user tells <author> the scope used.
- Copilot says the photo bridge flow is not running: the user tells <author>, who restarts it in a
  minute.
- A run stops part way or the session disconnects (the audit takes about 1.7 minutes a store, so
  100 stores is about 2.5 hours): the user returns to the same Cowork conversation, still in the
  task list, and says "continue"; it resumes from the last finished store in the same workbook. A
  new conversation cannot see the old one's progress and would start over.
- The visit-list skill takes a minute or two before anything visible happens: its instructions are
  long; normal.
- A label sheet prints misaligned to the die cuts: it was printed scaled; print at 100 percent or
  Actual Size, never "Fit to Page". The skill checks its own registration before handing over the
  PDF, so the file itself is right.
- The label skill says it cannot match a SKU: deliberate; it refuses to guess a barcode and names
  the SKU, which the user sends to <author>.

Maintained by <author>, <author email>. Anyone at <company> may use and share all of it.

Router and Role Resolution
--------------------------

Eight skills, nothing to install; anyone at <company> may use them. The assistant's section comes
first; a person scrolls to "If You Are a Person".

If You Are Copilot:

A user pasted the folder's link, or attached it as a zip, together with a request. The assistant's
job is to run the right skill, not describe the folder: it does not summarize this file or list the
folder back, and does not ask which skill unless routing genuinely fails.

If You Are Copilot - Step 1, Route:

The assistant matches the request to ONE skill on meaning, not exact words. The table is enough;
it does not open the other skill folders or their START HERE files to decide (it may cite a
SKILL.md description when asked to explain a choice). If the request is about routing itself, or
says not to run anything, it answers from the table and stops. TEST SCRIPT.md is for the tester and
is never read to decide anything or to fill in a result.

| The request is about | Skill |
|---|---|
| stores to visit, top stores, focus stores, visit list, where to spend time, a chain, zip, city or state subset of stores, comparing two planners, and similar phrasings | Top Opportunity Stores |
| contracts, contract opportunities, a <business unit 4> or <business unit 3> contract, upgrade or downgrade, reclaim space, not performing, audit, a <store id> list, linear feet, shelf position, and similar phrasings | Contract Compliance Audit |
| POS, signage, signs, missing POS, <sign type 1>, <sign type 2>, <sign type 3>, POS compliance, POS gap | POS Gap Scorecard |
| objectives, OKRs, 1H or 2H, mid-year, year-end, self assessment, performance review, write-up, wins, what can I claim | Performance Write-Up Concierge |
| labels, label sheet, placeholder labels, stickers for paddles, new store kit, barcodes to print, out-of-stock tags, UPC labels | Business Unit Label Sheets |
| audit my <chain name> stores for pricing, audit <chain name> in <jurisdiction>, margin gaps, price audit for my territory, is <chain name> above the <price ceiling>, where are my pricing opportunities | Pricing Audit |
| the pricing agent, build or seed or repair the pricing agent, add a state or market to it, shelf price refresh, buy-downs or allowances table, wholesale cost table, port the pricing agent to another category | Pricing Opportunity Agent (Builder) |
| daily briefings, enable or set up daily briefings, the email is all set up for 5:00, daily brief, route-day guide, my plan today, what stores am I hitting today, refresh the cycle, new planner dropped, cycle autopilot | Daily Store Brief and Cycle Autopilot |

Tie-breakers: auditing, checking or finding pricing opportunities for named stores, a chain, a
state or a territory is the Pricing Audit (it runs the audit); building, seeding, repairing, adding
a state to or porting the pricing agent is the builder (it builds the tool); either beats a store
list, and a one-off margin question for one item is answered inline with no skill. "daily
briefings", "my plan today" or "route-day guide" is the Daily Store Brief (today's stops with
actions); "stores to visit" or "top stores" is Top Opportunity Stores (the ranked list for the
cycle). "contract" beats "POS" (space versus signs). "labels" or "stickers" beats "POS" (a label is
a barcode the retailer scans to reorder; POS is branded signage). "stores to visit" beats
"objectives". A request that truly spans two skills: run the one the sentence is mainly about, then
offer the other in one line at the end; never run two skills unasked. Nothing matches, or a genuine
tie: ask ONE question listing the eight skills in the words of the "You say" phrasings under "If
You Are a Person", then proceed.

If You Are Copilot - Step 2, Stage Only the Chosen Skill:

Two ways the file arrives. Link: the assistant resolves the link to the shared folder, lists it,
opens the chosen skill's subfolder and downloads SKILL.md plus, if present, everything under
references/ and scripts/; it skips the zip and START HERE.md (they are for people); downloads land
in one shared folder under identical names (every skill has a SKILL.md), so it copies and verifies
each file right after downloading it, before the next. Zip: it sits in the session's attachments,
named as the skills bundle zip with a date; the assistant unzips it with Python into a hub
subfolder of the session's working folder, nothing to download; the skill folders sit directly
under the zip's top folder, next to this file. Either way the assistant puts the chosen skill's
files, same layout, where its own SKILL.md expects them under the session's working folder (the
Contract Compliance Audit and the Pricing Opportunity Agent builder each have a named staging
folder; the other six go under a shared skills folder, one subfolder each), replacing the folder's
contents if it already exists.

If You Are Copilot - Step 3, Read the Staged Skill:

The assistant reads the staged SKILL.md completely and follows it from its first step. Where it
says to copy from an installed skill folder, the copy is skipped (the files are already staged).
Where it says to invoke another skill (the built-in Power BI or Excel skill), the assistant does so;
those are built into every Cowork session.

If You Are Copilot - Step 4, Who Is Asking and What They Own:

The request is the user's own words, whatever followed the link or attachment. Scope comes from who
is asking, resolved from the login before any default is applied; each skill's SKILL.md repeats the
rule. The assistant never asks who the user is; it looks it up.

1. The signed-in user's profile gives the user principal name. The alias is the part before the @
  sign, in upper case. The user principal name is the login name, not the display email: a person
  whose display address is first name, initial and last name may sign in under a different short
  alias, so the alias comes from the signed-in account, never the display address.
2. In the <shelf recognition model> (report <report id>), the <user login table> row where the
  <login alias column> equals the alias; the table lists every field and account person and is not
  clipped by row-level security. The assistant reads the role, geography type, geography code,
  market id, section id, region id, employee title and employee title code columns. The geography
  code is six characters for every role, padded with zeros: the first digit is the region, the
  first two the section, the first four the market, all six the territory; a market-level row ends
  in two zeros, a section-level row in four, a region-level row in five. A market-level code is
  never read as a territory; the market, section and region id columns are the parsed pieces. No
  row: the <sales employee geography table>, at the row whose domain user name is <network domain>,
  a backslash and the alias, with the permanent assignment type (temporary rows at market and
  section level are ignored), gives the title and the sales geography code; the all-zeros code is
  headquarters (the chief executive, communications and most home-office roles). No row there
  either: headquarters.
3. Classify from the row. This step decides the class; nothing later promotes a person to a wider
  one.

| The row says | Who | Default scope | Say it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| geography type territory, with the <territory role> role code | <territory role> | the territory code; the contract audit narrows to the regular independents in it, the other skills take the whole territory | "your territory <territory id>" |
| non-geographic type, with the <market support role> title code | <market support role> | the market id: the whole market, never a single territory (that role's geography code is a market-level placeholder value) | "<market>" |
| geography type unit, with the <market role> role code | <market role>, and equivalent market-level titles | the market id | "<market>" |
| geography type section, with the <section role> role code | <section role> | the section id, every market in it | "<section id>" |
| geography type region, with a region id among the <field region ids> | <region role> | the region id | "<region id>" |
| geography type region with a region id among the <account region ids>, or any title containing "Account" | <account role> (every title in the account organization, from specialist to its head; all non-geographic except the head) | their chains, per the account span rule below | "your chains, <chain A> and <chain B>: <n> stores" |
| the all-zeros code, or not found in either table | headquarters (<HQ role>) | no default, even when the alias appears high in a chain of command; the scope the request names. If it names none, ask ONE question offering region, section, market, territory, chain or a <store id> list (and, for the visit list, where the <planning workbook> is), then run | (none) |
| found, but no row above fits (support and administrative title codes) | <support role> | the geography of the row if it has one (territory, unit, section or region type); otherwise headquarters | (none) |

Account span rule: the stores in the <store hierarchy table>'s account-type rows where the alias
appears either in the domain user name column (prefixed with <network domain> and a backslash) or
in any of the level 0 through level 5 user name columns (as the alias at <company domain>); that
table's store id column is the store list, and the <store profile table>'s chain name column names
the chains. One role code is shared by <market support role>s and account people, so the
<market support role> is keyed on the employee title code, never on the role code.

4. The <store hierarchy table> is the store list for the class decided in step 3, not a way to
  reclassify: geography rows (level 0 <territory role>, level 1 <market role>, level 2
  <section role>, level 3 <region role>), account rows (level 0 <account role> up to the chief
  executive), and <market support role> rows (their own row type, alias at <company domain> in the
  domain user name column; the stores that role is assigned to cover, which the skills do not use
  as the default because that role's default is the whole market). Unlike the directory, this
  table is clipped by row-level security: from the person's own login it shows their own span;
  from anyone else's, only the overlap. Verified on <date> from a <territory role>'s login in
  <market>: that manager saw <n> stores, the <market role> <n>, an <account role> <n> (their
  <chain A> and <chain B> stores inside that market), a <market support role> <n>.
5. An explicit scope in the request always beats the default: "territory <territory id>",
  "<market>", "<section id>", "<region id>", a chain name, "my business development prospects",
  "my chains", a <store id> list, an attached list.
6. Row-level security clips every store query to what the person may see, so a wrong default can
  under-deliver but never over-reach. The answer opens with one line naming the scope and why:
  "Scope: <market>, your market as <market role>, <n> territories." The counts in that line come
  from the <user login table> (distinct territory codes of <territory role> rows per market id,
  distinct market ids per section id, distinct section ids per region id), which is not clipped.
7. Size the run before starting. A territory is a few hundred stores; <market> is <n> open stores,
  <n> of them with a <business unit 4> or <business unit 3> contract. Above one market every skill
  states the count, proposes to run market by market, and runs the whole span only when the user
  says so. The contract audit scores photos at about 1.7 minutes a store with four agents in
  parallel (a single agent takes about 5), so already above one territory it states the count for
  the default scope (the contracted stores in it: regular independents for a geography, chain
  stores for an account person, never the market total), the hours, and proposes a cut: one
  territory at a time for a market, one market at a time for a section or region, one chain for an
  account person; it runs the whole span only when told to, in blocks with checkpoints. A default
  scope with zero contracted stores (some chains carry no store-level <business unit 4> or
  <business unit 3> contract in the model) is stated as such in one line with an offer to run a
  chain, a <store id> list or a geography instead; nothing is scored. An attached
  <planning workbook> offered as the visit-list input is checked for a matching market before use.

If the skill needs an input file (the <planning workbook> for Top Opportunity Stores) and none is
attached, the assistant looks where the skill says first (attachments, then the user's OneDrive),
then asks once. The Daily Store Brief needs no attached file: it reads the <CRM> Daily Schedule
email and finds the cycle files in the user's OneDrive by their columns. If the attached zip holds
a test-inputs folder, a file there may serve as the input, and the assistant says which file it
used. It never invents stores, numbers or names to fill a gap.

If You Are Copilot - Step 5, Deliver:

The assistant delivers exactly what the skill's Output section describes, in the output pane, and
stops. It never sends email or posts to Teams unless the user asks.

If You Are a Person:

The user opens a new Cowork session, pastes the <shared folder link> on one line and the request on
the next, nothing else, asking as they would ask a person; Copilot reads this file, picks the skill
and runs it. Without the link, attaching the zip of the folder works the same way.

Each row: skill: what you say; what you get.

- Top Opportunity Stores: "give me my top stores", "who should I visit this month", "build my visit
  list"; a ranked, defensible visit list from the cycle's <planning workbook>, weighted by volume,
  the supervisor's focus, headquarters priorities and the <business unit> hierarchy, as one Excel
  workbook (<territory role>s Top 10 + Next 25, the manager tier Top 20 + Next 50). Attach the
  current monthly planner or say where it lives.
- Contract Compliance Audit: "what are my contract opportunities", "audit my <business unit 4> and
  <business unit 3> contracts", "audit these <store ids>"; every contracted store scored from its
  latest <photo platform> photo (<our brand 4A> linear feet, shelves, header and position versus
  <competitor brand 4A>; <our brand 3A> facings), verdict and next step per store in one Excel
  workbook. Photos arrive by themselves through the photo bridge flow; about 1.7 minutes a store;
  the user can walk away and say "continue" later.
- POS Gap Scorecard: "what POS is missing in my territory", "who is missing <sign type 2> signage",
  "show me my POS compliance"; a color-coded Excel scorecard from the <shelf recognition model>:
  green check where POS is present, red X for a real gap, yellow where the store could not be
  assessed, a failure code explaining every miss.
- Performance Write-Up Concierge: "help me with my 2H objectives", "set my OKRs", "write my
  mid-year"; objective setting against headquarters and supervisor priorities, mid-period progress
  tracking, completed mid-year and year-end sections. Bring reports or nothing.
- Business Unit Label Sheets: "make my label sheets", "rebuild my <business unit 3> labels", "new
  store kit"; a print-ready PDF of out-of-stock placeholder labels, each with the product name and
  a real scannable UPC-A of the reorder unit, sized to land inside off-the-shelf 1 x 2 inch
  die-cut label stock; facings follow the user's own market volume. Bring the <SKU volume report>
  export from <company>'s Power BI workspace. Print at 100 percent, not Fit to Page.
- Pricing Audit: "audit my <chain C> stores for pricing", "where are my pricing opportunities at
  <chain D> in <jurisdiction>", "is <chain E> above the <price ceiling>"; one Excel workbook with
  Summary, Findings (every <company> item above the lowest competitive margin in its store and
  category, with shelf price, net cost, benchmark, parity price, weekly units and an ask
  sentence), Above <price ceiling> (<category 1> items over the pass price, with a verify-in-store
  read when the pass price changed inside the 13-week window), Gates (missing costs and buy-downs,
  restricted programs, proposed rows), Benchmarks and Data Notes. Reads the tables the app writes;
  enters nothing. A chain with no wholesale costs on file gets the Gates and Above <price ceiling>
