Daily Store Brief and Cycle Autopilot
=====================================

Purpose: A scheduled autopilot for a <territory role>. Every weekday it reads the rep's <CRM>
"Daily Schedule" email and emails one phone-friendly brief of that day's planned store visits;
every Saturday it rebuilds the cycle's per-store content from the newest cycle files in OneDrive
and the inbox and from 13 weeks of business data in <data warehouse>.

Triggers: "I'd like to enable daily briefings"; "the email is all set up for 5:00 AM"; "what is my
plan today"; "refresh the cycle" or "new planner dropped, rebuild"; "build my route-day guide for
the cycle"; and similar phrasings asking for per-store actions on planned <CRM> visits.

Inputs:
- The <CRM> "Daily Schedule" subscription email (inbox, each weekday morning): every remaining
  planned visit in the cycle, as a CSV attachment, an HTML table or text.
- The <planning workbook> (the monthly planner, one row per store): the user's OneDrive-backed
  Desktop or Documents folder, or the monthly planner email from <user's manager>. Mandatory.
- The <distribution gap export>, the <loyalty program target list> and the contract audit workbook
  (the Contract Compliance Audit skill's output): the same folders or inbox attachments. Optional.
- The <market cycle workbook> (found by name), any other store-keyed spreadsheet ("Other Cycle
  Data"), and presentations, PDFs and Word files that direct the cycle's work.
- The <monthly POS kit> email and <user's manager>'s cycle emails (inbox); the user's calendar.
- 13-week shipment and scan data by store, category and brand from <data warehouse> (Power BI).
- The signed-in user's profile (role, territory, market, email).

Outputs:
- One phone-friendly HTML email per selling day (Monday to Friday), to the user only, with the
  per-stop content standard for each planned stop in start-time order plus the day's calendar.
- On refresh: the per-store content file (JSON) for every ranked store; with a new planner, also
  the plan workbook (Excel) and the route-day guide (Word, one page break per sales day). All are
  copied to the user's OneDrive folder daily-store-brief; the guide is never emailed.
- A two-line "needs attention" email when a Saturday step fails; nothing otherwise.
- The cycle configuration file, a workspace notes file with the rep's times, and a refresh report.

Parameters to Fill In:
- <company>, <sales org> - the company and its field sales organization, whose cycles run four
  weeks, Saturday to Friday.
- <business unit>, <business unit 1>, <business unit 2>, <business unit 3>, <business unit 4>,
  <business unit 5> - the operating units whose contracts and kit activities the planner, the kit
  and the brief name.
- <category 1>, <category 2>, <category 3>, <category 4>, <category 5> - the five product
  categories in the business table.
- <our brand 1A>, <our brand 2A>, <our brand 2B>, <our brand 2D>, <our brand 3A>, <our brand 4A>,
  <our brand 5A> - the lead brands per category; 2D is a second <category 2> brand whose on-shelf
  count the target list carries.
- <competitor brand 1A>, <competitor brand 1B>, <competitor brand 1I>, <competitor brand 1K>,
  <competitor brand 1L>, <competitor brand 2A>, <competitor brand 2C>, <competitor brand 2G>,
  <competitor brand 3A>, <competitor brand 3B>, <competitor brand 3D>, <competitor brand 4A>,
  <competitor brand 4B>, <competitor brand 4C>, <competitor brand 5A>, <competitor brand 5B> - the
  competitor brands tracked per category in Versus the Competition.
- <loyalty program>, <loyalty program target list> - the <category 2> retailer loyalty program and
  the exported workbook of its target stores and on-shelf counts.
- <CRM> - the field CRM holding planned visit records, with its analytics dashboard, visit report
  widget and email subscriptions.
- <data warehouse> - the Power BI semantic model with 13-week shipments and scan data by store.
- <planning workbook> - the monthly planner (region, section, market, territory, <store id>, store
  name and activity columns).
- <distribution gap export> - the dashboard export of each store's distribution and signage
  measures below goal.
- <market cycle workbook> - the section-level cycle workbook emailed each cycle; found by name.
- <monthly POS kit> - the monthly kit email of cycle activities, due dates and headquarters
  wording.
- <price support program> - the retail price support program whose election column the planner
  carries.
- <sign type 1> - the signage program with a state-by-state guide; <sign type 4> - the
  <business unit 1> flip-style price sign scored against set size and agreed planogram.
- <volume unit> - the planner's cross-category weekly volume measure; <priority store flag> - the
  planner flag for a headquarters-targeted high-upside store; <strength> - a product strength
  keyword in planner activity headers.
- <author> - the method's author, named in workbook contact rows and the guide footer.
- <user's manager>, <territory role>, <market support role>, <support role> - the rep's manager,
  the rep's title, the per-store merchandising support role, and the section-level support role
  that emails the <market cycle workbook>.
- <market>, <territory id>, <store id>, <store ids> - the rep's market and territory, and the
  store number that keys every file.
- <jurisdiction> - a state with its own legal or signage rule; <chain A> - a chain named in one
  example manager quote; <shared folder link> - the skill folder link pasted on day one; <date> -
  a company-history date; <n> - a store count for a named geography.
- <partner product line> - the non-core product line the sales org also sells, which appears as its
  own activity originator with the lowest business unit weight
- <total volume column> - the planner column holding each store's total cross-category equivalent
  units per week over the latest 13 weeks
- <store dimension> - the data warehouse's store table, carrying the territory code and the business
  development flag that filter every query
- <sign type 4> - the business unit 1 state value-price flip sign
- <sign type 5> - the business unit 1 loyalty flip sign for the core brand 1A line
- <price family A> - the first tracked brand 1A price family (its own flip sign; pass-through
  tracked)
- <price family B> - the second tracked brand 1A price family (pass-through; the competitor-threat
  rule)
- <price family C> - the third tracked brand 1A price family (pass-through)
- <required facings> - the contiguous brand 3A facings the business unit 3 base contract requires
- <strength> - the strength of the brand 4A premium line expansion activity this cycle
- <flavor> - the flavor segment in which competitor brand 1A is the threat
- <cycle label> - the cycle's display label, month and year, as configured
- <territory id>, <market id> - six-digit and four-digit codes as the configuration template expects

What the User Says and Gets
---------------------------

Two short conversations, one day apart; then a brief lands in the inbox every selling morning and
the cycle rebuilds itself every Saturday. Nothing to install, nothing to upload.

Day one: in a new Cowork session the user pastes the <shared folder link> and says "I'd like to
enable daily briefings". Copilot walks them through the <CRM> home page (the visit report widget's
fields and filters), a daily Monday-to-Friday email subscription named "Daily Schedule" at the
time they choose (5:00 AM is typical), and the OneDrive backup of Desktop and Documents, where
they already save the planner, the <distribution gap export> and the
<loyalty program target list>.

Day two (or later the same day, once the subscription is saved): in the same session the user
says "okay, the email is all set up for 5:00 AM". Copilot finds the planner and cycle files,
builds the Top Store ranking and per-store content, and schedules the runs around that time. The
first brief arrives the next selling morning about 30 minutes after the <CRM> email, built from
it.

Other ways to ask: "what is my plan today" (one-off brief, nothing scheduled); "refresh the cycle"
or "new planner dropped, rebuild" (rebuild now, not Saturday); "build my route-day guide for the
cycle" (the full Word guide, one page break per sales day).

What comes back: one phone-friendly email per sales day with, per stop in start-time order, the
store header and <store id>, Top Store rank, distribution gaps with the exact fix,
<loyalty program> targeting, the 13-week business table, Versus the Competition with units per week and
what to do today, and a Do This list, plus the day's calendar. Monday to Friday only; Saturday is
the quiet rebuild; nothing on Sunday. Time: ten minutes on day one, five on day two; the daily
email is automatic. For a permanent install the user copies the skill folder into the OneDrive
folder Documents/Cowork/skills and then just says "I'd like to enable daily briefings".

For Copilot pointed at the folder: load SKILL.md. Any set-up phrasing is Workflow A1 (give the
<CRM> and OneDrive walkthrough and stop); "the email is all set up for" plus a time is Workflow
A2 (install, discover, build, schedule around that time, say the first brief arrives the next
selling morning). No step is ever put on <user's manager>.

Skill Instructions
------------------

Version <date>, built by <author> (<territory role>, <market>) with Copilot Cowork. ASCII only,
Title Case headings, concise and scannable. The skill holds no store data; everything a run
produces stays in the rep's own session, OneDrive and inbox.

What It Does:

Two loops in one scheduled task. Legend: when; what happens; cost.

- Daily brief: Monday to Friday, 5:15 AM local; read the <CRM> "Daily Schedule" email, keep
  today's planned stops in start-time order, assemble the brief from prebuilt per-store content,
  add the day's calendar, email it; 3-4 tool calls, about one minute, nothing recomputed.
- Cycle refresh: Saturday, 5:15 AM local; find the newest cycle files in OneDrive and the inbox by
  their columns; new planner: rerun the Top Store ranking and plan workbook and rebuild the
  route-day guide; always: refresh the 13-week data from <data warehouse> and rebuild the
  per-store content; validate, swap, copy to OneDrive; 35 Power BI queries plus local builds,
  5-15 minutes.

The brief is one sales day only. The full-cycle guide (Word) is built on refresh, lives in
OneDrive and is never emailed.

Who It Is For:

A <territory role> with one territory of planned <CRM> visits. The assistant resolves who is
asking as the Hub section describes; the only difference here is that the scope is always the
rep's own territory. Each rep runs their own copy in their own Cowork task (their inbox, OneDrive
and Power BI row-level security). Nothing is ever put on <user's manager> or a teammate.

One-Time Setup Per Rep:

1. Day one, the rep says "I'd like to enable daily briefings". Copilot gives the <CRM>
   subscription walkthrough and the OneDrive backup check (Workflow A1) and asks them to return
   once the subscription is saved.
2. The rep returns with "Okay, the email is all set up for 5:00 AM". Copilot activates the
   autopilot (Workflow A2): installs the scripts, finds the cycle files, builds the ranking and the
   per-store content, schedules the runs around that time. The first brief arrives the next
   selling morning from the first real <CRM> email; nothing is sent on day one.

Workflow for Copilot:

Four workflows: A1 enable, A2 activate, B cycle refresh, C daily brief.

A1. Enable ("I'd like to enable daily briefings", "set up daily briefings"):

Nothing is installed, built or scheduled yet. The assistant gives this walkthrough in its own
words, in this order, as a numbered list the rep follows on a laptop, then stops and waits.

1. The rep opens <CRM>, goes to the home page, scrolls to the <CRM> analytics dashboard and finds
   the visit report widget's table.
2. The rep sets the fields: account number, account name, territory, visit record type, visitor
   name, visit status, planned/completed date. If the builder offers a planned start time, the
   rep adds it; it gives the brief the stop order straight from <CRM>.
3. The rep sets the filters: visitor name is the rep, visit status is Planned, sales cycle is
   Active. No date filter: the email must carry every remaining planned visit in the cycle, not
   one day.
4. The rep clicks the widget's share arrow, chooses Subscribe, daily, Monday through Friday, at a
   time before they leave for the field (5:00 AM is typical), names it "Daily Schedule" and saves.
   It arrives as the <CRM> analytics subscriptions email with Daily Schedule.csv attached.
5. OneDrive check: OneDrive icon, Settings, Sync and backup, Manage backup, Desktop and Documents
   on. The rep saves the planner, the <distribution gap export> and the
   <loyalty program target list> to the Desktop as they land; the browser's download folder should be the Desktop.
   Nothing is ever uploaded to Copilot.
6. The assistant closes with: when the subscription is saved, come back and say "okay, the email
   is all set up for 5:00 AM" (or the chosen time); the first brief arrives the next selling
   morning about 30 minutes after it.

If the inbox already holds the <CRM> analytics subscriptions email with a Daily Schedule.csv
attachment, the assistant says so, skips steps 1-4 and asks only for the time it is set for.

A2. Activate ("okay, the email is all set up for 5:00 AM"):

The time T comes from the rep's sentence (default 5:00 AM). Run time is T plus 15 minutes,
wait-until is T plus 28, expected delivery about T plus 30. Then, in order, the assistant does
the following.

1. Resolves the user (territory, market, role, email) from the profile and the planner; a
   <territory role>'s scope is their own territory (the Hub section).
2. Installs: copies the local build scripts (config loader, ranking builder, workbook builder,
   verifier, guide builder, per-store content dumper, Word guide builder and checker, cycle
   refresh, activity wording) into the session's working folder and the three tool-using scripts
   (daily brief, cycle discovery, <data warehouse> pull) into the script-runner folder; creates
   the cycle configuration from the bundled template with the user's scope and cycle window
   (reference part 3 explains every key); writes the workspace notes file from reference part 4
   with the rep's times filled in.
3. Runs the Cycle Refresh (B) along its new-planner path. If discovery finds no planner, tells the
   rep where it looked and that the autopilot starts the Saturday after the planner lands on the
   Desktop; still creates the schedule.
4. Grants before the schedule. A scheduled run can use only what the task was already allowed,
   and the schedule card can pre-approve the email send alone (scoped to the rep's own address).
   The Saturday refresh also reads Power BI, OneDrive and the inbox and writes cycle files back to
   OneDrive, so those permission cards are raised now, with the rep present, by the refresh in
   step 3 and the dry run in step 5, with the instruction "Choose Always Allow on each card, not
   Allow; that is what lets the morning runs go out without you." Then the scheduled prompt is
   created (reference part 4): weekly, Monday to Saturday, at the run time, inline mode, send
   permission scoped to the user's own address only. The schedule card is approved at once (a
   timed-out card saves nothing) and the scheduled prompts are listed afterward to confirm.
5. Dry-runs the daily brief for the next selling day and checks that the preview renders; sends
   nothing. The first real brief goes out on the next weekday run from the first <CRM> email.
6. Tells the rep what arrives, when (T plus about 30 minutes, Monday to Friday), that Saturday is
   the quiet rebuild, and the three things that can need attention (reference part 6).

Briefs are for selling days only. There are never planned visits on Saturday or Sunday: Saturday
runs the refresh and sends nothing unless a step needs attention; Sunday has no run.

B. Cycle Refresh (Every Saturday, or "refresh the cycle", "new planner dropped"):

1. The assistant runs the cycle discovery script through the script runner (not a dry run). It
   scans the OneDrive Desktop, Documents, Downloads and root folders two levels deep plus inbox
   attachments since the last refresh, recognizes files by their column signatures and never by
   name (reference part 2), keeps the newest of each kind (planner, <distribution gap export>,
   <loyalty program target list>, contract audit, <market cycle workbook>), stages them in the
   session's cycle input folder and updates the configuration. Any other spreadsheet keyed by
   store number (a one-off program export, a new initiative tracker, a program target list) is
   staged as an extra input, and every store's own row from it appears in the brief under Other
   Cycle Data with no code change. New presentations, PDFs and Word files in the same places and
   the inbox are listed as documents for step 2. The assistant reads the result's new-planner
   flag, Other Cycle Data labels and document list.
2. If the planner is new, the assistant sets the cycle's label, month, year, start, end and
   next-cycle end from the planner's month and the <monthly POS kit> email (reference part 3);
   sets run date and built date to today; rebuilds the calendar notes from the user's calendar
   for the cycle window (no-selling days, field rides, calls that overlap stops); clears the
   completed-before list; refreshes the kit deadlines and <user's manager>'s focus list from the
   kit email and the manager's cycle emails (discovery has already cleared the roll price message
   and completed-before); and clears the live plan file so the plan rebuilds from the next <CRM>
   email. Then, new planner or not, it opens each listed document (the kit, a program
   announcement, the manager's deck, a <jurisdiction> guide) and folds anything that directs this
   cycle's work into the cycle notes: a kit deadline into kit deadlines, headquarters wording into
   headquarters quotes, the manager's direction into the focus list, a state rule into the
   state-guide notes. It quotes, never paraphrases, and skips a document with no store-level
   direction, saying so in the log.
3. The assistant runs the <data warehouse> pull script through the script runner (no arguments;
   territory from the configuration; it finds the model by name). About one minute.
4. The assistant runs the cycle refresh script as a Python process with the new-planner flag
   (omitted when the planner is unchanged). It runs the local builds, validates the new per-store
   content (valid JSON, store count, required fields, ASCII), swaps it in atomically, keeps the
   previous content as the previous-content file and writes the refresh report. A non-zero exit
   means nothing was replaced.
5. Publish: the assistant copies the configuration, the per-store content, the captured plan (if
   captured) and the live plan to the user's OneDrive folder daily-store-brief, overwriting. New
   planner: it also copies the plan workbook and the guide document to the session's output
   folder and the user folder.
6. If any step fails, the assistant leaves the previous content in place and emails the user a
   two-line "Daily Store Brief needs attention" note naming the step. It never emails a partial
   rebuild.

C. Daily Brief (Every Weekday):

The assistant runs the daily brief script through the script runner with the wait-until time
(T plus 28). The script does the following.

- Restores the configuration, per-store content, captured plan and live plan from OneDrive if the
  workspace is fresh.
- Finds today's "Daily Schedule" email (polling every 3 minutes until the wait-until time), reads
  every planned visit row (HTML table, text, or CSV/XLSX attachment), saves the remaining plan to
  the live plan file and OneDrive, keeps the rows dated today in start-time order, and notes
  stops added or dropped versus the previous plan and how many visits remain in the cycle.
- Falls back, in order, to the plan saved from the previous email, then the plan captured at
  cycle start, and says which at the top of the email.
- Builds the HTML from the per-store content (every ranked store in the territory, so a store
  added mid-cycle still has content; a brand-new store gets a header plus a note), adds the day's
  calendar, enforces ASCII and sends to the user only. The subject is "Daily Store Brief - " plus
  the weekday and date, the stop count, and the first and last towns. A day with no planned
  visits gets a two-line note.
- Dry run: given a date and the dry-run switch, writes a preview HTML file in the working folder
  and sends nothing; used after any content change.

Content Standard (Per Stop):

1. Header: stop number | start time | store name; address | <store id> | chain or Independent;
   then Top Store rank of N, <volume unit> per week with volume rank, <priority store flag> when
   set, the assigned <market support role>, best time, last call.
2. Distribution gaps: only measures missing or under 100 percent, each with the exact fix (which
   sign, which SKUs to put back, how many cans to goal); "None - every measure is at 100%." when
   clean; "Not in the <distribution gap export>." when the store is absent.
3. <loyalty program>: Targeted or Not targeted, and <our brand 2A> or <our brand 2B> store (from
   the planner). Other Cycle Data follows when any other store-keyed export names the store: one
   line per file, the file's name then its columns and values for this store (for example a
   tracker's target flag, on-shelf count and header-placed flag). Identity columns are dropped;
   values are shown as found, never interpreted.
4. Business - Latest 13 Weeks: one row per category: units per week, change versus a year ago
   with the territory's change in parentheses, the lead brand's share and point change with the
   territory's share in parentheses, then two sentences of plain-language read.
5. Versus the Competition: one line per category with a verdict word (WINNING, AT RISK, TRAILING,
   LOSING, NOT IN THE GAME), then the company's brand with units per week, share, position versus
   the territory and year-over-year change in units and percent; the nearest threat with units
   per week, share and year-over-year change; the number two brand the same way; then "Do:" with
   what the rep can do in this store today. Every share carries its units per week (rules in
   reference part 5).
6. Do This: a numbered list of at most six items, each tied to a line above.

Writing rules: ASCII only, Title Case headings, no AI-hallmark words, short declarative lines,
numbers before adjectives. Mobile layout: a single column, 15px body, 640px maximum width.

Guardrails:

- One email per sales day, to the user only. Never the full-cycle guide, never a 200-page email.
- Fail loudly, degrade gracefully: a broken input never blocks the brief; it goes out from the
  last good content with a one-line header saying what was used, and a refresh failure sends a
  "needs attention" note instead of partial content.
- No step is ever placed on <user's manager> or a teammate. No Power Platform connector, no admin
  request.
- Scope is the user's own planned stores. Business development prospects, other territories and
  other reps' plans are never read.
- Discovery downloads only xlsx, xlsm and csv files modified since the last refresh and under
  60 MB, and only to classify them by their header rows; presentations, PDFs and Word files are
  listed by name for the Saturday read, never downloaded by the script; nothing else in OneDrive
  is opened or kept.
- Scripts never log message bodies, attachments or whole payloads; counts and names only.
- The scheduled prompt's send permission is scoped to the user's own address and never widened.
- Every number in a brief traces to a source: the planner, the <distribution gap export>, the
  <loyalty program target list>, the contract audit, <data warehouse>, the <CRM> email or the
  calendar. A missing input shows as "Not in the <distribution gap export>" or "No
  <loyalty program> list for this cycle", never a guessed value; a store with no prepared content gets a
  header and a note, never invented lines.

When Not to Use:

- Only the ranked visit list or the plan workbook: Top Opportunity Stores.
- A POS presence scorecard for a market or territory: POS Gap Scorecard.
- Contracts scored from store photos: Contract Compliance Audit (<business unit 4> and
  <business unit 3>).
- Editing, charting or reformatting a workbook: the xlsx skill.
- Completed calls, call history or <CRM> reporting on past visits: this skill reads planned
  visits only.
- Building or scheduling Power Automate flows or a Copilot Studio agent: the automation skill.

Files:

Three scripts run through the script runner because they use the session's tools: the daily brief
script (arguments: date, dry run, wait-until, note override, alternate recipient), the cycle
discovery script (since date, dry run, folder list, skip drive, skip inbox) and the
<data warehouse> pull script (territory, model name, output path). The rest run as plain Python: the
cycle refresh (new-planner and skip-Word flags), the ranking builder, workbook builder and
verifier (the Top Store ranking and the nine-tab plan workbook, the Top Opportunity Stores
scoring as implemented in <date>), the guide builder and per-store content dumper, and the Word
guide builder (page break per sales day, linked contents) with its checker. The config loader and
the cycle configuration template are read by every run. The Scripts section describes each
script; the reference covers <CRM> setup, email format, configuration keys, scheduled prompt
text, verdict logic and troubleshooting.

Troubleshooting:

- The brief says "using the plan saved from the previous <CRM> email" every day: the subscription
  changed layout. A dry run of the daily script shows the "rows parsed" log line; the email may
  have come as an image or a new table shape. The fix is the parser or a re-created subscription
  with the A1 fields.
- "No <CRM> schedule email found": the subscription is paused or renamed; the brief still went
  out from the saved plan.
- Discovery finds no planner: it is in Downloads or an unbacked folder; the rep moves it to the
  Desktop. The log names every file it classified.
- Stores show "Not in the <distribution gap export>": the export is stale or missing; the
  Saturday run picks up a newer one once it lands on the Desktop.
- Content looks a cycle old: the refresh report shows the failed step; a failed validation leaves
  the previous content in place on purpose.

References
----------

Reference: Pipeline Notes:

Build notes for the cycle-driven Top Store and route-day guide chain. All scripts run from the
session's workspace root and read their cycle settings from the cycle configuration through the
config loader (an environment variable can point at another configuration path). Nothing is
hardcoded to a month or a territory except the items under Still Hardcoded.

Reference: Pipeline Notes - Still Hardcoded:

- Author contact lines in the plan workbook (Method, Questions and Tuning rows) and the guide
  footer name <author> on purpose: that is who to ask about the method.
- The example configuration is a scrubbed past cycle; a new rep's configuration is copied from
  the template, not the example.
- Nothing else: recipient, territory, market, planner path and contact come from the
  configuration's scope and planner input. A missing scope email or territory stops the daily
  brief and the <data warehouse> pull with a plain error instead of guessing.

Reference: Pipeline Notes - Full Rebuild:

Preferred: the cycle refresh script with the new-planner flag (optionally skipping the Word
build). The chain is the ranking builder (planner to ranking file plus a parse-and-score
checkpoint), the workbook builder (ranking to plan workbook plus a build checkpoint), the guide
builder (ranking, visits, planner and optional inputs to guide blocks and a guide preview) and
the per-store content dumper (imports the guide builder, writes the per-store content file). The
<data warehouse> 13-week file is a prerequisite of the guide builder and is not rebuilt here.

Reference: Pipeline Notes - Scripts:

The notes tabulate what each script reads, writes and takes from the configuration; the Scripts
section carries that detail per script. Two points matter to the operator: the guide builder uses
the live plan when its file exists and the captured plan otherwise, and the workbook and guide
stamp the cycle label, dates, scope and the planner's name, source and modified time into their
footers and method rows.

Reference: Pipeline Notes - Scripts - Details:

- Planner sheet and header detection (guide builder): the first sheet whose first five rows hold
  a row with both a <store id> cell and a territory cell; that row is the header. The ranking
  builder keeps its own string-cell-score detection.
- Month-stamped planner columns (the <price support program> election, the <business unit 2>
  contract and the <business unit 4> contract for the cycle month and the previous month) are
  built from the cycle month. The <business unit 5> contract column carries its own stale month,
  so it is resolved from the header: the month nearest to, and not after, the cycle month.
- Kit due-date years: a bare month/day falls in the cycle-start year when its month is at or after
  the cycle-start month, otherwise the following year.
- The per-store content holds every ranked store in planner scope (<n> in the example), not only
  the planned stops. Planned date and time come from the visit when the store is on the route,
  else empty. The cycle label reads as label plus start and end in month/day form; the generated
  stamp is the built date.

Reference: Pipeline Notes - Optional Inputs:

Empty in the configuration or missing on disk.

- <distribution gap export> absent: the gap block is empty for every store, the presence flag is
  false, the guide prints "Store is not in the <distribution gap export>." and no hang-sign,
  put-back, <our brand 2D> or pass-through items are generated.
- <loyalty program target list> absent: no store is targeted; the line reads "No
  <loyalty program> list for this cycle."; the <business unit 2> sign fix falls back to the brand-equity
  header or <sign type 4>.
- Contract audit absent: no audit line, no <business unit 4> or <business unit 3> next steps;
  planner contract flags still apply.
- Other cycle data (the extra inputs list; empty means nothing shown): the guide builder loads
  each file, finds the header row carrying a store number, and keeps every store's non-identity
  columns as header-and-value pairs (numbers compact, dates month/day, text cut at 60 characters,
  ASCII); one line per file naming the store lands in the guide and in the per-store content,
  which the brief renders under Other Cycle Data. The file labels and the <data warehouse>
  week-end date (best effort) ride at the top of the content for the brief's footer.

Scripts
-------

Script: Activity wording table  (source role: short wording for planner activity headers, keyed by
header text)
Purpose: Gives the route-day guide and the plan workbook a short, readable phrase for every
  activity column in the <planning workbook>, looked up by the header's text (lowercased, spaces
  collapsed) rather than by column position, so a reordered planner does not break the wording.
Inputs: the raw activity header text passed in by the guide builder or the workbook builder; three
  hand-maintained maps held inside the file: a guide short label, a guide "do this" sentence, and a
  workbook "why" label, one entry per known activity of the current cycle.
Outputs: the mapped phrase, or a generated fallback label; one log line per unmapped header on the
  error stream so a new cycle's unfamiliar activities are visible in the refresh log.
Logic:
  1. The caller asks for a phrase by header and map kind (guide short, guide do, workbook why).
  2. The header is normalized and looked up; a hit returns the maintained phrase. Maintained
     phrases carry the activity's origin and timing in parentheses, for example "(kit, due
     <m/d>)", "(kit Things to Do)", "(no close date published)", "(<market support role> work,
     not this cycle)", and the workbook variant adds the close weight it earned, such as "(Q4
     token, 0.20 - not this cycle)" or "(no published due date, 0.75)".
  3. A miss builds a generic label: the originator prefix in parentheses is dropped (for
     example "(<market support role> only):" or "(<territory role> needed):"), trailing "see ...
     for details" tails and parenthetical asides are removed, the text is cut at about 60
     characters on a word boundary with "..." appended, and the words are title-cased. A keep
     list preserves known acronyms, brand spellings, state codes and roman numerals; small
     words stay lowercase except in first position; hyphenated and numeric words are left
     alone.
  4. Each fallback is logged once per header per map kind and cached for the rest of the run.
Checks and failure handling: a missing mapping never fails the build; the fallback label is used
  and logged. The maintainer is expected to read the "no mapping" lines after a refresh and add
  entries for the new cycle's activities.

Script: Top Store ranking data build  (source role: parse the planner, scope it to the territory,
score and rank every store, write the ranking data set and a checkpoint)
Purpose: Turns the <planning workbook> into the ranked store data set that the plan workbook, the
  route-day guide and the per-store brief content all read. It carries the Top Opportunity Stores
  scoring (version 2.0) inside the cycle autopilot so the Saturday refresh can rerun it without
  the user present.
Inputs: the cycle configuration through the configuration loader (planner path, <territory id>,
  run date, cycle start, cycle end, next cycle end, cycle month, cycle notes); the
  <planning workbook> itself, first sheet only; from the cycle notes: the <monthly POS kit> deadline rows
  (title, due date, restricting states, type), the kit's "things to do" bullets,
  <user's manager>'s focus phrases (sender, date, text, level weight) and the manager-directed
  <store ids>.
Outputs: the ranking data set (JSON) in the session's working folder: scope counts, the activity
  inventory, every ranked store with its score components and planner attributes, the Top 10,
  Next 25 and all-other lists, assortment target rows, coverage gap rows, benchmarks and a column
  concept map; a checkpoint file recording stage, inputs, outputs and invariants; a console
  summary (header row chosen, counts, medians, scored activities, the top 40 and bottom 5 stores).
Logic:
  1. Header detection: the first 15 rows of the first sheet are scanned and the row with the
     most non-empty text cells becomes the header; the runner-up is recorded. Every header and
     value is reduced to ASCII; null-like values ("null", "n/a", "-") become blanks; dates are
     normalized.
  2. Records are rows with a <store id>; duplicate ids are counted file-wide and in scope but
     never dropped.
  3. Activity block: the activity columns run from the first header carrying an originator
     prefix (a "<market support role> only" prefix, a "<territory role> needed" prefix, a market
     or section activity code, or a local-activity tag) through the column before the
     right-most "grand total" header. A numeric count row above the header would be the
     preferred anchor; when the row above is a banner, the fallback and the banner's own
     "Activities" label are written into an anchor note for the audit trail.
  4. Scope: a store is in scope when its territory code, zero-padded to six digits, equals the
     configured <territory id>. Temporarily closed stores are set aside and listed. The step-4
     median is the median total equivalent volume (<total volume column>, units per week over
     the latest 13 weeks) of the open stores; negative or missing volume counts as zero for
     ranking but the raw value is kept.
  5. Lifestyle-flagged stores are removed unless the store is manager-directed or its volume is
     at or above the step-4 median (the escalation list). What remains is the ranked population
     N; its median is the step-7 median; zero or missing volumes are listed separately.
  6. Volume percentile = the store's average ascending rank (ties share the average) divided by
     N; scope rank counts down from the largest volume.
  7. Activity inventory, one entry per activity column: flag counts in the file, in scope and
     in N; a work type from a verb ladder with weights sell 1.25, merchandise 1.00, educate
     0.75, validate 0.50, admin 0.25, standing target lists 0.00, unclassified 1.00 ("execute"
     alongside only a validate or admin verb promotes to merchandise; "execute" alone is
     merchandise; verbless gap or opportunity nouns resolve to sell, or to merchandise when a
     shelf noun is also present); the <business unit> the header names, with weights
     <business unit 1> 1.00, <business unit 4> 0.95, <business unit 2> 0.70, <business unit 5> 0.45,
     <business unit 3> 0.40, <partner product line> 0.30; the originator (<market support role>
     only, <territory role> needed, local when a geography code in the header is a prefix of
     the <territory id>, otherwise national or headquarters).
  8. Kit and supervisor matching: header and phrase are tokenized (stop words and brand family
     words set aside, words stemmed, known initiative phrases collapsed into single tokens). A
     match needs at least two shared tokens of which one is not a brand family word, or one
     shared initiative token or phrase. The best kit match is chosen by (non-family shared,
     total shared); a dated kit row beats an undated bullet on a tie. A kit row restricted to
     named states is dropped when none of those states appears among the in-scope stores.
  9. Close weight ladder. Rule 1: a kit match with a due date bands by date (on or before cycle
     end 1.00; within the next cycle 0.50; later 0.20; a bare month/day takes the cycle-start
     year for months at or after the start month, else the following year); a kit bullet with
     no published due date is 0.75; a supervisor-named or local activity is 1.00. Rule 2: with
     no such match, a quarter or month token in the header bands the same way, and a token that
     ended before cycle start is marked expired at 0.20. Rule 3: no derivable close date
     excludes the activity from scoring.
  10. An activity scores when it has a kit match that was not state-dropped, a supervisor match
     or a local tag. Source boost: kit plus supervisor 2.0 times the highest matching
     supervisor level weight; supervisor only 1.5 times that weight; kit only 1.5. Further
     multipliers of 1.25 each: originator "<territory role> needed"; narrow (fewer than 10
     percent of N carry the flag); local. A local activity naming no business unit takes the
     lowest business unit weight. Per-flag contribution = business unit weight x close x source
     boost x work weight x the three multipliers.
  11. Per store: priority = the sum of contributions of its flagged scoring activities;
     must-close = the count of flagged activities whose close weight is 1.00. Gap terms, 0.5
     each: assortment satisfaction below the N median on any of the three tracked business unit
     assortment columns; a missing contract (any contract column with at least one fill in N:
     <digital trade program>, <business unit 1>, <business unit 2> for the cycle month,
     <business unit 3>, <business unit 4> for the cycle month, and the newest-month
     <business unit 5> column) or a distribution gap (<our brand 4A> premium line absent while any of
     three tracked <category 4> competitor brands is present over the latest 52 weeks); a
     pass-through value reading "no pass" or "partial"; the Q flag (a store in the top five
     <volume index> deciles whose performance identifier is the biggest-upside bucket). One
     bucket term by performance identifier: biggest upside 0.50, winners 0.25, outperformers
     0.15, low priority 0. Gap is capped at 2.5.
  12. Score = (1 + priority + gap + 1.2 x min(must-close, 5)) x percentile to the power 1.5.
     The score is halved when the store's only flagged activities are <market support role>
     assigned, or it has an assigned <market support role> person and no other activity. Focus
     business unit = the most common business unit among the store's flagged activities, else
     the business unit whose own-volume column puts the store at its highest percentile,
     labeled "volume read".
  13. Ranking order: stores with volume before zero-volume stores, then score descending, volume
     descending, <store id> ascending. Manager-directed stores are removed from the eligible
     list, which then splits into Top 10, Next 25 and all others (up to 500).
  14. Assortment targets for <business unit 4>: among the top third of N by volume, the peer
     norm is the median SKU count (latest 26 weeks) of stores stocking at least one SKU; a
     store qualifies when its gap to the norm is 2 SKUs or more. Play "Add SKUs" when it stocks
     less than half the norm or its units per SKU are at least 75 percent of the population
     median; otherwise "Drive velocity" (range present, moving slowly: merchandising and
     education, not a sell-in). Upside = gap x units per SKU for Add SKUs, SKU count x the
     velocity shortfall for Drive velocity; sorted by upside, volume, id; capped at 500 rows.
  15. Coverage gaps: days since last call per store; threshold = the larger of 60 days and twice
     the median. Flags: coverage over threshold (2.0), distribution absence (2.0), an
     independent missing a contract (2.0, plus 0.5 when the <digital trade program> contract is
     among the missing). A store flagged only for contract and below the step-7 median is
     skipped. Coverage score = total x (0.5 + 0.5 x percentile); High at 2.0 or more, Medium at
     1.0, otherwise Low; sorted by score, volume, id; capped at 500. Counts of stores
     approaching (150 to 179 days), overdue (180 or more), never called and future-dated are
     kept.
  16. Benchmarks: contract penetration among independents, territory versus the market's open
     non-lifestyle rows; share of independents "not aligned" to the <price support program>;
     <digital trade program> adoption by volume quintile of the market's independents.
  17. Column concept map: every header is assigned to a concept (identity, geography, status,
     volume, coverage, contracts, assortment, own-business-unit and category volume,
     distribution presence, pass-through, decile, performance identifier, headquarters priority
     flags, pricing); unresolved headers and columns empty in scope are listed for the method
     notes.
Checks and failure handling: stops at once when the configured planner path does not exist. Any
  missing expected header stops the run with the header name. Prints the header-row choice and
  runner-up, the anchor note, record and scope counts, both medians, the zero-volume list and
  the scored activity table so a reviewer can audit the run. The checkpoint's invariants
  (records, in scope, closed, lifestyle removed, N, eligible, list sizes) are what the verifier
  re-checks.

Script: Word guide package builder  (source role: single-pass, deterministic build of the route-day
guide .docx from the guide block file, no template)
Purpose: Renders the route-day guide's prepared block list into a complete Word package written
  directly as Word's own XML markup, so the guide looks the same on every refresh: one font, ASCII
  only, hyphen bullets, numbered lists that each restart at 1, a page break per sales day and a
  linked table of contents. Content text is rendered verbatim; nothing is added, dropped or
  reworded here.
Inputs: the guide block file (JSON) in the same folder as the script; the cycle configuration for
  the <territory id>, the cycle label, the user's name (document creator) and the configured
  output name of the guide; an optional output path on the command line that overrides the
  configured name.
Outputs: the .docx package (document body, styles, numbering, settings, font table, footer, core
  and extended properties, content types and relationships); a JSON summary on the console: output
  path, block count, counts by block type (page breaks, headings at three levels, paragraphs,
  section labels, bullet and numbered items, numbered lists, tables, contents entries, back links),
  bookmark names, hyperlink count, missing anchors and the range of numbered-list ids.
Logic:
  1. Page geometry is US Letter portrait with 0.7 inch margins; the text width that results is
     the width every table is scaled to. Cell padding is 4.5 points left and right and 2 points
     top and bottom.
  2. Text measurement uses a metric-compatible sans font so column minimums can be computed:
     a column is never narrower than its longest word (or its whole header when the header is
     10 characters or fewer, or a whole cell value of 12 characters or fewer), plus padding and
     a 4 percent allowance. Relative column weights from the block are scaled to the text
     width; any column lifted to its minimum takes the deficit proportionally from columns with
     slack; widths always sum exactly to the text width.
  3. Block types are rendered in order: a page break (deferred and emitted as Word's native
     hard break in its own one-point paragraph just before the next block); a level 1 heading
     (the title when its bookmark is "top", otherwise a day heading or the "not on route"
     heading, each followed by a right-aligned "Back to Table of Contents" link); a level 2
     heading (bookmarked as the contents anchor when its text is "Table of Contents"); a level
     3 heading; a section label (small caps, extra space before when a table follows); a
     paragraph with an optional named style (subtitle, day line, note, profile, small, with
     breathing room when it follows a table); bullets; a numbered list; a table; the contents
     entries as internal hyperlinks.
  4. Styles: body 10 point; Heading 1 16 point in the primary color with a rule beneath;
     Heading 2 12 point in the secondary color with a hairline rule above (the stop card
     separator) and 14 points before; Heading 3 10.5 point grey; section label 9 point bold
     small caps; list items 9.5 point; contents links and back links as named paragraph styles;
     a footer style with a right tab at the text width. Headings keep with next and keep lines
     together.
  5. Tables: a fixed layout; header row shaded in the primary color with white bold 8.5 point
     text, repeated on every page the table spans and never split; body rows 8.5 point with
     light zebra shading on alternate rows; every row but the last keeps with the next so a
     table moves as a whole; cells align right when the column's filled cells vote mostly
     numeric (numbers, signed changes, percentages, "n/a" and "flat" count as numeric), with
     each body cell following its own value when it is non-empty.
  6. Numbering: one abstract bullet definition using a plain hyphen and one abstract decimal
     definition; each numbered list gets its own numbering instance with a start override so it
     restarts at 1.
  7. Footer: "Territory <territory id> | <cycle label> cycle | Confidential - Internal Use
     Only" at left, "Page N of M" fields at right. Core properties carry the guide title and
     the user as creator, with the build timestamp.
  8. All parts are assembled in memory and written to a compressed zip package.
Checks and failure handling: the block file is opened as strict ASCII and the build stops on an
  unknown block type. Before writing, every part is re-encoded as ASCII (non-ASCII aborts the
  build) and scanned for forbidden symbol fonts. After writing, the summary lists any hyperlink
  anchor without a matching bookmark so a broken contents link is caught before the checker
  runs.

     numbering instances are used; the number of numbered lists and how many restart at 1;
     instances used but undefined; bullet instances used; bullet and numbered item counts.
  7. Full content comparison: the block file is flattened into an expected sequence of units
     (headings, labels, paragraphs, each bullet and numbered item, each table as its rows of
     cell text, each contents entry, each page break, plus the back link that follows every day
     heading); the document body is walked in order into the same unit shapes; the report says
     whether the two sequences are identical and, if not, the index and text of the first
     difference.
  8. Alignment spot check: every non-empty body cell is classified numeric or text (numbers,
     signed changes, percentages, "n/a", "flat") and its justification is read; counts of
     right-aligned numeric and left-aligned text cells are reported and every mismatch is
     listed.
Checks and failure handling: the script only reports; the refresh step reads "content identical"
  and the non-ASCII and missing-anchor counts to decide whether the guide is good. A paragraph
  style outside the known set, or an unknown body element, appears in the sequence with a "?"
  prefix so it surfaces as a difference.

  2. Cycle: the display label (month and year), the month name, the year, the cycle start (a

     <market planning workbook>; a list of extra store-keyed files; a list of documents (presentations, PDFs,

  5. Visits: the path of the plan captured at cycle start and the path of the live plan saved

  6. Calendar notes: a map from date to a one-line note (no-selling days, field rides,

  7. Outputs: the plan workbook name, the guide document name and the per-store content path,

  8. Cycle notes: the kit deadline rows; the headquarters quotes; the supervisor focus items;

Checks and failure handling: the loader treats an input as absent when its entry is null, has no

Script: Cycle file discovery  (source role: find the newest cycle input files in OneDrive and inbox
attachments by their columns, stage the winners and update the cycle configuration)
Purpose: Removes the need for the user to upload anything: each Saturday it finds the newest
  <planning workbook>, <distribution gap export>, <loyalty program target list>, contract audit
  workbook and <market cycle workbook> wherever they landed, recognises them by their header
  columns rather than their names, and stages the newest of each kind for the builds.
Inputs: arguments: a "since" date (default: the last refresh minus 2 days, else 35 days ago), a
  dry-run flag (default true: report only), the OneDrive folders to scan (default Desktop,
  Documents, Downloads; the root is always scanned), a download cap (40) and a size cap (60 MB),
  and flags to skip the drive or the inbox; the current cycle configuration for the territory,
  the last refresh time and the planner it was built on; the user's OneDrive through the file
  tools; the user's mailbox through the mail tools.
Outputs: in a live run, the winners copied into the session's cycle input folder under their kind
  name, extra store-keyed files into a sub-folder, and the configuration updated (input entries
  with path, name, source and modified time; the extra list; the documents list; a last-discovery
  timestamp); in every run, a result with the since date, download count, whether a new planner
  was found, the other cycle data files, the documents noticed, the per-kind winners (name, where,
  modified, source) and every candidate considered; a log line per file classified.
Logic:
  1. Classification by columns, reading only the first six rows of the first six sheets (or the
     first six lines of a CSV): a planner sheet carries the store id, territory and store name
     headers plus at least one of region, section or market; a gap export carries the store id
     and the store-name header with sign, visibility or missing-signage columns; a loyalty
     target list carries the store id and store-name header with the target and on-shelf
     columns; a contract audit has a sheet named "Store List" with the store id and contract or
     linear-feet columns; the <market cycle workbook> is recognised by its name. Any other
     sheet with three or more header cells including a store-number-like header is "store
     data" and rides along as other cycle data. Files whose names mark them as this skill's own
     outputs (the plan workbook, the guide, the content file, the brief) are ignored.
  2. OneDrive scan: each configured folder and the root are listed, descending two folder
     levels into sub-folders modified since the since date (year-only or date-named folders are
     skipped). A file is considered when it was modified since the since date; temporary lock
     files and own outputs are skipped; presentations, PDFs and Word files are only recorded in
     the documents list, never downloaded; spreadsheets over the size cap or beyond the
     download cap are skipped with a log line; the rest are downloaded and classified.
  3. Inbox scan: messages received since the since date (up to 400) with attachments and a
     subject mentioning planner, planning, the list, reserve, export, audit, store list or visit
     are opened; document attachments are listed; spreadsheet attachments are read from the
     saved copy or the message content when available, otherwise fetched (attachments over 3.5
     MB are skipped with advice to save them to OneDrive); each is classified, and a hit
     records the sender, subject and received date as its source.
  4. Winners: for each kind the newest modified candidate wins; for store data every distinct
     file name keeps its newest copy. A new planner is declared when the winning planner's name
     differs from the configured one or it is newer.
  5. Live run: winners are copied to the cycle input folder as "<kind>.<extension>", extras to
     the extra sub-folder (merged with those already configured by name), the newest 40
     documents are listed, and when a new planner was found the previous cycle's roll-price
     message and completed-before list are cleared so they do not carry forward; the
     configuration is saved with a last-discovery timestamp.
Checks and failure handling: a folder that does not exist is logged and skipped; a listing,
  download or mail failure is logged and skipped rather than stopping the run; only counts, names
  and classifications are logged, never contents; a dry run changes nothing. A development
  argument exists to show how one email attachment comes back through the tools (shapes only).

Script: Cycle refresh runner  (source role: run the local cycle pipeline in order with validation
and a safe swap of the per-store content file)
Purpose: Gives the Saturday refresh one command that rebuilds everything local in the right order,
  validates the new per-store content before it goes live, and never leaves the daily brief with a
  half-built file.
Inputs: two flags: "new planner" (rebuild the ranking and the plan workbook, and the guide
  document unless "skip docx" is also set) and "skip docx"; the cycle configuration for the
  content path, the guide name, the cycle label and the territory; the pipeline scripts in the
  working folder.
Outputs: the refreshed per-store content file with the previous version kept beside it as the
  ".prev" copy; the plan workbook and guide document on a new-planner run; a refresh report (JSON)
  with start and finish times, one entry per step (name, ok, seconds, note), the options, the
  cycle, the territory and the output paths; a five-line console summary; a non-zero exit status
  on any failure.
Logic:
  1. The working directory is forced to the workspace root so every relative path resolves the
     same way whether run by hand or by the scheduled task.
  2. The plan: with the new-planner flag, the data build then the workbook build; always, the
     guide content build then the content dumper, the latter pointed at a temporary file.
  3. Each script runs as a child process with a 30-minute timeout; the note records the last
     three lines of output on success or the exit code and last six lines of error output on
     failure; activity-wording fallback lines are counted into the note and echoed so the
     maintainer sees new activities. The plan stops at the first failure.
  4. Validation of the temporary content file: valid JSON; at least 50 stores; every store
     carries a name, address, rank, business rows, versus lines and do-this list, with the name
     non-empty, the rank an integer and the business rows non-empty (versus and do-this may be
     empty lists); no character above 0x7E anywhere in the serialized JSON. The note records
     stores, planned stores, cycle and generated date.
  5. Swap: the live file is copied to the ".prev" name, then the temporary file is moved into
     place in one atomic operation.
  6. New-planner runs then build the guide document and read the builder's summary (a missing
     anchor fails the step), then run the checker and require "content identical" to the block
     file.
  7. The report is written; the summary states OK or FAILED, steps run, ok and failed with the
     elapsed time, whether the daily content was refreshed or is unchanged, whether the guide
     was rebuilt, and the report path.
Checks and failure handling: on any failure the live content file is never touched and the
  temporary file is removed; the exit status tells the calling step to leave the previous content
  in place and send the "needs attention" note rather than publishing a partial rebuild.

Script: Daily brief sender  (source role: read today's "Daily Schedule" email from the <CRM>, keep
today's planned stops in start-time order, assemble the phone-friendly HTML brief from the
per-store content and email it to the user)
Purpose: The weekday morning run. It recomputes nothing: it finds today's stops, pulls each
  store's prepared card from the content file, adds the day's calendar and sends one email to the
  user only.
Inputs: arguments: an optional date (default today in the user's local time zone), an optional
  recipient (default the cycle's configured user email; the run stops with no recipient), a
  dry-run flag (default false), an optional "wait until" local time to keep polling for the
  email, an optional note override and a force flag for weekends; the cycle configuration; the
  per-store content file; the plan captured at cycle start; the live plan saved from the previous
  email; the <planning workbook> for a store id lookup; the user's mailbox, calendar and OneDrive
  through the platform tools.
Outputs: the HTML brief as a preview file in the working folder and, unless dry run, the email
  (HTML body, not saved to Sent Items); the live plan saved locally and copied to the user's
  OneDrive folder for the skill; a result with date, stops (time, id, name), the source note,
  subject, whether it was sent, HTML size and live plan row count.
Logic:
  1. Restore: when the workspace is fresh, the configuration, content, captured plan and live
     plan are restored from the user's OneDrive folder for the skill (listed once, only when
     something is missing; a month-stamped copy is the second choice for the content and plan).
  2. Reference data: the captured plan's rows for today in start-time order and each store's
     captured start time; the set of known store ids from the content and the plan; and, from
     the planner's sheet with the id and territory headers, every store id in the territory with
     name, city, address and zip, so a newly planned store is still recognised even with no
     prepared content.
  3. Find the email: messages received since local midnight are scanned; a match is a <CRM>
     analytics subscription whose preview mentions "Daily Schedule", a subject mentioning daily
     schedule, the <CRM> visit report widget or planned store visits, or a sender from the <CRM>
     with a schedule or visit subject; the newest wins. When a wait-until time is set and
     nothing is found, the script sleeps 3 minutes and looks again until that time.
  4. Parse the rows: first from HTML table rows in the body (a six-digit id cell, date cells,
     time cells, a status cell, a name cell that is not a date, id, status or visit type), then
     from plain-text lines carrying a known id and a date; when the body yields nothing, from a
     CSV or Excel attachment, by column names when the export carries an account number header
     (account number, visit status, account name, the first date or start column), otherwise by
     scanning each line for an id, a date and a time. The log records the subject and the row
     count parsed.
  5. Live plan: rows with a date whose status is not completed, abandoned or cancelled are
     sorted by date then time, de-duplicated on date and id, given store fields from the
     content, the planner or the email's own name, and a time from the email or the captured
     plan; the list is saved locally and copied to OneDrive. The email carries remaining visits
     only, so the saved list is the remaining plan.
  6. Today's stops: the rows dated today, de-duplicated, with times from the email or the
     captured plan, sorted by start time. The note states the source; when the email carries
     dates only it says stop order came from the captured plan; stops added or dropped against
     the previous plan (yesterday's live plan, else the captured plan) are named; the number of
     visits remaining after today and the last date are given.
  7. Fallback when no row is dated today: today's stops from the previous plan, in time order,
     with a note saying which plan was used and why (email read but none dated today; email
     found but no rows could be read; no email found by the current time). A note override
     replaces the note.
  8. Weekend guard: on Saturday or Sunday nothing is sent unless forced; the result records the
     skip and the live plan row count.
  9. Calendar: the day's non-all-day, non-cancelled events as "start-end subject" lines.
  10. HTML: a single column, 15 pixel body, 640 pixel maximum width. A header band with "Daily Store
     Brief - <weekday, month day>", the stop count or "No planned store visits", the territory and
     up to five towns; the calendar line; the source note; a "Route:" line of time and store name
     per stop. Per stop: a rule; "n | time | name"; address, city, zip | <store id> | chain; a tag
     line (Top Store rank of N, units per week with volume rank, Q store, <market support role>
     person, best time, last call); then the section labels, set in small capitals: Distribution
     Gaps (lines, or the green "None - every distribution gap measure is at 100%." or the grey "Not
     in the <distribution gap export>."); the <loyalty program> line; Other Cycle Data when present;
     Business - Latest 13 Weeks as a compact table (rows with no units and no share are dropped);
     Versus the Competition with the verdict word colored (winning green, at risk and trailing
     amber, losing red, not in the game grey) and the "Do:" in the primary color; Do This as a
     numbered list. A stop with no prepared content gets its header, address and id and a note that
     it was added after the content was built, the Saturday refresh will add it, and a one-off brief
     can be requested. A footer names the sources (the "Daily Schedule" email, the planner, the Top
     Store report, the gap export, the loyalty export, any other cycle data files and the
     <data warehouse> with the 13-week end date), defines YoY and gives the contact address.
  11. Subject: "Daily Store Brief - <Day m/d> - <n> stops, <first town> to <last town>" or "no
     planned visits".
  12. Send to the single recipient, or in dry run write the preview only.
Checks and failure handling: any non-ASCII character left in the HTML is replaced with a hyphen
  and counted in the log; a failed restore, mailbox read, attachment fetch, plan save, OneDrive
  copy or calendar read is logged and the brief still goes out from what is available; no message
  body or attachment is ever logged, only counts and names. The captured-plan date quoted in two
  notes is fixed in the script and must follow the cycle start when reused.

Script: <data warehouse> 13-week pull  (source role: pull 13-week shipment and scan volumes by
store, category and brand for a territory from the <data warehouse> Power BI model and save them
as JSON)
Purpose: Supplies the business update and the Versus the Competition section with the latest 13
  weeks and the same 13 weeks a year ago, for every store in the territory and for the territory
  as a whole.
Inputs: arguments: the <territory id> (default from the cycle configuration; the run stops without
  one), the model alias (default: discover the <data warehouse> by name, because session aliases
  are not stable across days) and the output path; the Power BI query tool.
Outputs: a JSON file with one row list per query (per-store category totals for five categories,
  territory category totals, territory brand totals, per-store transaction counts, and per-store
  totals for each tracked brand family) plus the end date of the 13-week window; a result with
  the row count per list and the week-end date; a log line per query.
Logic:
  1. Discovery: search the semantic models for the <data warehouse> by exact name and take its
     alias or id; fall back to reading the alias out of the tool's text; stop with a clear
     message when nothing is found.
  2. Every query filters the <store dimension> to the territory and excludes business
     development prospects, and computes four measures: shipments over the calendar's
     latest-13-weeks flag, the same through the year-ago week-end relationship, and scan units
     the same two ways.
  3. Per-store category queries for <category 1>, <category 3>, <category 4>, <category 2> and
     <category 5> (the model's category group values); a territory-by-category query; a
     territory-by-category-and-brand query ordered by current volume; a per-store transaction
     query (category transaction count and identified-consumer transaction count over the
     window).
  4. One per-store query per tracked brand family: <category 1>: <our brand 1A>,
     <our brand 1C>, <competitor brand 1A>, <our brand 1B>, <our brand 1D>, <competitor brand 1B>,
     <competitor brand 1K>, <competitor brand 1I>, <competitor brand 1L>; <category 3>:
     <our brand 3A>, <competitor brand 3A>, <competitor brand 3B>, <competitor brand 3D>;
     <category 4>: <our brand 4A>, <competitor brand 4A>, <competitor brand 4B>, <competitor brand 4C>;
     <category 2>: <our brand 2A>, <our brand 2B>, <competitor brand 2A>, <our brand 2C>,
     <competitor brand 2C>, <competitor brand 2G>; <category 5>: <our brand 5A>,
     <competitor brand 5B>, <competitor brand 5A>. Each is capped at 1,000 rows.
  5. Result reading: a normal result carries a table with columns and rows; a large result comes
     back as a preview string with the full CSV appended after the JSON, so the CSV block is
     located by its header line and parsed, falling back to the preview rows with a warning.
  6. The 13-week window's end date is read with a one-row query (either ISO or m/d/y form); a
     failure leaves it null and the brief's footer says "latest 13 weeks".
Checks and failure handling: stops without a territory or a discoverable model; logs row counts
  per query and warns when only a preview could be parsed; about one minute per run.

Script: Workbook contract gate  (source role: stage 10 verification of the built plan workbook
through the workbook engine, never by value-only reads)
Purpose: Proves the plan workbook meets its formatting and content contract before it is
  published, and supplies the pass or fail matrix the Method tab prints.
Inputs: the workbook path; optionally the planner path for a source spot check (the stand-alone
  entry point uses a fixed planner path that must be updated per cycle).
Outputs: a result with the list of checks (name, ok, note), the 13-element matrix per store tab
  and the list of failures; the stand-alone entry prints the matrix per tab, each check as PASS or
  FAIL and the failure count.
Logic:
  1. The nine tabs exist in the required order.
  2. For each of the six store tabs, 13 elements: (1) header row 1 begins Rank (Priority on
     Coverage Gaps) then the <store id>; (2) panes frozen below the header and right of the
     State column; (3) exactly one Excel table covering the populated block; (4) the medium
     table style with row stripes; (5) the header band in the primary fill, bold white 10 point
     in the body font, row height at least 30; (6) an autofilter over the whole block; (7) thin
     grid borders on every populated cell (every cell when 100 rows or fewer, otherwise the
     header, the first 20, the last 20 and 49 evenly spaced rows); (8) body font and size,
     top-aligned and wrapped on the same cells; (9) right alignment on numeric columns and left
     elsewhere; (10) explicit column widths; (11) the narrative column last with a width of 100
     to 124; (12) every row's height at least the height its longest cell needs; (13) on
     Coverage Gaps, the days-since shading exactly amber at 150 to 179 and red at 180 or more
     and no other fill (not applicable on the other tabs).
  3. Per store tab also: the exact header sequence (rank or priority, the eight identity
     columns, the tab's own columns); the id and zip stored as text with five-digit zips; the
     identity and narrative cells populated on every row that carries an id.
  4. Across the three merit tabs: ranks continuous from 1; no store id on two tabs; no directed
     store on a merit tab.
  5. Every sheet title, table name, cell value and the file name within printable ASCII, with
     the count of cells scanned.
  6. Panel checks on Read Me, Priorities and Method: every heading row (recognised by its fill)
     in Title Case, allowing acronyms and small words; the title row in the primary fill, bold,
     at least 16 point, at least 32 high and merged; section rows bold, at least 12 point, at
     least 22 high and merged; every populated cell in the body font, top-aligned and wrapped;
     row heights sufficient for the merged width.
  7. Source spot check when a planner is given: three random stores from the merit tabs (fixed
     seed) must match the planner's volume and city.
Checks and failure handling: a failure never stops the build; the matrix and failure list are
  returned to the workbook builder, printed, and written into the Method tab and the checkpoint
  for the reviewer.
