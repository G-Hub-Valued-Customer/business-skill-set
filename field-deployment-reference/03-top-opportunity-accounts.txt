Top Opportunity Stores
======================

Purpose: Builds a prioritized store visit list for a <sales org> field role from the cycle's
<planning workbook>, ranked by volume and weighted against <user's manager>'s stated focus,
headquarters priorities and <company>'s <business unit> hierarchy. A <territory role> gets Top 10
plus Next 25; every manager-tier role gets Top 20 plus Next 50; both get a reference tab of up to
500 stores. Comparing two planners measures decline.

Triggers:
- "give me my top stores to visit"
- "give me my top stores to visit for <business unit 4>" (a steer toward a unit or brand)
- "top opportunity stores this month", "what stores should I focus on", "build my visit list"
- "my top <chain A> stores", "top opportunity stores in zip <zip>", "my top independent stores"
- "compare this planner to last cycle's"

Inputs:
- The current <planning workbook>, attached to the request or located in the user's OneDrive; a
  second planner when a comparison is requested.
- The signed-in user's profile, title and manager chain, from the people directory and the
  <shelf recognition model>'s user table, per the Hub section.
- <user's manager>'s priority mail for this month and the prior month, swept from the user's own
  mailbox, including any attached store list.
- Headquarters priorities: the current <monthly POS kit> and the <sign type 1> state guide on
  <company>'s SharePoint.

Outputs:
- One Excel workbook, nine tabs in fixed order: Read Me, Priorities, Directed, the first visit
  tab, the second visit tab, All Other Stores, Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps, Method.
- Line A (<territory role>): Top 10 and Next 25. Line B (manager tier): Top 20 and Next 50. Both
  carry All Other Stores with up to 500 further ranked stores.
- A five-to-eight line summary in chat, opened with the Scope line.

Skill Instructions
------------------

Overview:

The skill turns the cycle's <planning workbook> into a ranked, defensible store visit list so no
rep is caught without an answer to "what stores are you focused on this month?". It resolves the
user, their role, their supervisor and the cycle's priorities automatically, then ranks stores by
volume weighted against those priorities and the <business unit> hierarchy. The author line
(<author>, <territory role>, <market>, <sales org>, <author email>) takes questions and
suggestions, and every report carries the same contact line.

Version and Validation Status:

Version <version>, released <date>, supersedes the previous major version entirely; a copy
without this block is older and contains the defects corrected here.

What Changed in <version>:

The previous version was audited end to end against a live market planner (32 defects fixed),
and later reviews added capabilities: the output contract C1 to C6 as single source of truth;
thirteen formatting elements verified per tab; scope columns between <store id> and Store with a
computed freeze pane; the span-of-control block replacing one Territory column; request
qualifiers as a third axis with typed routing and a loud miss path; the close-date ladder taking
the kit's published due date as rule 1; work-type weighting (price validation scores zero); the
local activity boost (an activity tagged with the user's own scope code is admitted and weighted
1.25x, and a direct market or territory tag overrides the price rule); exact percentile,
floor-rounding and usable-volume definitions and a written comparison formula; graduated retry,
ten hard gates, a per-partition fan-out contract and an eight-invariant regression check at every
gate; four scale tiers to 250,000 or more records with a two-pass percentile path.

Validation Status - Read This Before Deploying:

Verified by execution: a full Line B build on a live market planner (<n> records, <n>
territories, nine tabs); the Stage 10 gate passing with 570 continuous merit ranks, zero
cross-tab duplicates and the 12-by-6 formatting matrix plus element 13 on Coverage Gaps; all four
steer modes, the qualifier axis and role resolution across four personas from <territory role>
to <HQ role>; discovery and reading of the <monthly POS kit> and <sign type 1> state guide from
SharePoint. Not yet verified: scale (every run was tier T1, under 5,000 records; T2 to T4,
partition fan-out and two-pass percentile are unexercised); kit-table parsing (run against one
kit only; a differently laid-out kit is the likeliest silent regression, which is why an
unreadable date bands to 0.75 or 0.20 and never 1.00); supervisor priorities (every test ran from
a mailbox other than the subject's, so the path never returned real mail); the formatting gate
(needs the runtime to expose border, font, freeze and table-style attributes, otherwise the skill
refuses to publish). First-run checks: the Method tab shows a spread of close-date rules rather
than every activity at 1.00, and the thirteen-by-six formatting matrix is present and passing.

The Output Contract:

The single source of truth for what the skill emits; where a later instruction disagrees, the
contract governs and the later instruction is the defect. Read before Step 1, verified against in
Stage 10.

C1. Exactly Two Standardized Report Lines:

Two shapes, no others; every run emits exactly one workbook in the shape matching the resolved
role, and the shapes differ only in three numbers.

Each row gives the Line A (<territory role>) value, then the Line B (manager tier) value.

- Roles: <territory role>; <market support role>, <market role>, <section role>, <region role>
  and anything above.
- Default scope: one territory; market, section, region or national, the widest level the role
  owns.
- First visit tab: Top 10, ranks 1-10; Top 20, ranks 1-20.
- Second visit tab: Next 25, ranks 11-35; Next 50, ranks 21-70.
- Reference tab: All Other Stores, ranks 36 onward, up to 500 rows; All Other Stores, ranks 71
  onward, up to 500 rows.

"Up to" is load-bearing and this row-count form is authoritative. The reference tab holds the
smaller of 500 and (eligible count minus last visit rank) rows, or zero rows when the eligible
count is at or below the last visit rank, shipping then with its header and the single row
"Every eligible store appears on the visit tabs; there are none left to list." Never a negative
count, never a failure. The eligible count is every store in the ranked population excluding
directed stores: in scope, open, and either not a lifestyle store or one readmitted by the three
escapes, with no volume floor and with zero- and null-volume stores included and ranked last.
Directed stores live on their own tab and never consume a merit rank. On a 200-store territory
the tab is often 110 to 140 rows and complete; nothing may assert it holds exactly 500.

A user-requested size overrides the role default and renames the tabs: one-third to the first
tier, the remainder to the second, every cap and gate unchanged. A request carries up to three
independent axes, SCOPE (whose stores, the org code family), QUALIFIER (which subset, by matching
any other planner column on its values) and STEER (what to lean on: a <business unit>, brand or
initiative); they compose in any order, change the population and weighting, and never change the
nine tabs, the thirteen formatting elements or the two sizes (Step 1b).

C2. The Nine Tabs, in This Order, Always:

Read Me, Priorities, Directed, the first visit tab, the second visit tab, All Other Stores,
Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps, Method. All nine ship on every run; a tab with nothing to
report ships with its header plus exactly one row stating the reason. Tabs are never dropped,
renumbered around or reordered. The six store tabs (Directed, the two visit tabs, All Other
Stores, Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps) all obey C3 and C4.

C3. The Identity Block: Nine Fixed Columns, Plus the Span-of-Control Block:

Identical on all six store tabs, in this order. (1) Rank, except on Coverage Gaps where column 1
is headed Priority, written literally. (2) <store id>, as text. (3) The span-of-control block,
Region, Section, Market, Territory as applicable, coarsest to finest, all text; zero columns on
Line A, zero to four on Line B (legitimately zero when every finer level collapses to one value,
as on a single-market section file or after a narrow qualifier). Then Store, Address, City,
State, Zip (text, left-padded to five), Chain or "Independent", Volume (the scope's ranking
measure), and the tab-specific block of C3b. The block sits between <store id> and Store because
the scope code is the first thing a manager sorts and groups by; on Line A it is empty and Store
follows <store id> directly.

C3b. The Tab-Specific Block: Fixed Headers, Fixed Order, No Substitutions:

After the identity block come exactly these columns, in this order, header strings copied
literally and never renamed, reordered, added, omitted or "improved"; two runs on the same
planner produce byte-identical header rows.

- Top, Next and All Other Stores: Scope rank, Vol %ile, Score, Must close, Q, <business unit>
  focus, Why.
- Directed: Scope rank, Directed by, Date, Instruction, What to do.
- Assortment Targets: Scope rank, Category units per week, SKUs, Peer norm, SKU gap, Assort %,
  Units/SKU, Est. upside u/wk, Play, What to do.
- Coverage Gaps: Scope rank, Vol %ile, Score, Flags, Last call, Days since, Our assortment %,
  Competitor assortment %, Category units, Missing contracts, <price support program>, What to do.

Score is mandatory on every store tab, to two decimals. Scope rank is the store's rank on the
ranking volume measure within the scope, a plain integer under the same header at every level,
never retitled to the level. Category units per week carries the units per week of the category
the tab was built for, named in Method, and the header is not renamed to it. That category is the
<business unit> whose planner columns resolve both a distinct-SKU count and an
assortment-satisfaction percentage; every unit is tested against that pair. In the reference
planner only <business unit 4> carries both; a percentage without a SKU count never builds the
tab. Two or more qualifying units: the highest-ranked in the Step 5 hierarchy, named in Method.
None: the tab ships with its header and one row stating the planner carries no SKU count. A
column whose source does not resolve is still written with its header and blank cells, the
failure named in Method; dropping it changes the workbook's shape.

C3b - The Span-of-Control Block: Show the Levels Below the Requester, Never Their Own:

A reader knows their own level and needs the level beneath it to know whose work each store is.

| Requester's own scope | Scope columns included, in order | Omitted as redundant |
|---|---|---|
| Territory (Line A) | none | Region, Section, Market, Territory |
| Market (<market support role>, <market role>) | Territory | Region, Section, Market |
| Section (<section role>) | Market, Territory | Region, Section |
| Region (<region role>) | Section, Market, Territory | Region |
| National (<HQ role> and national executives) | Region, Section, Market, Territory | none |

The test is mechanical and both conditions must hold: the column is (1) strictly finer than the
requester's resolved scope level and (2) carries more than one distinct value in the report
population. Condition 2 is computed once, over the full ranked in-scope population after any
qualifier or FILTER steer (the same N that supplies the volume percentile), so the column set is
identical on all six store tabs; it settles the awkward cases by itself (a section holding one
market drops Market; a national file with one region drops Region). The block is placed
immediately after <store id> in hierarchy order, every column formatted as text because codes
carry leading zeros. When the requester's level cannot be resolved, every scope column with more
than one distinct value is included; the ambiguity is never resolved by omitting the block.

The Read Me states in one line that the scope columns show whose stores these are, and that
filtering a tab to one of them shows which of these stores sit there, not that unit's own ranked
list: attribution, not delegation. Filtering never produces a sub-unit's report, for two reasons
the skill may never contradict. The tabs hold only published rows (a Top 20 filtered to one
territory returns however many of the twenty sit there, frequently zero; on the reference market
the Top 20 drew from <n> of <n> territories). And rank is relative to the population it was
computed over and is not recomputed by a filter (a store ranked 340th across a market may be 3rd
in its territory). The caps make this structural: a <market role> publishes 570 of roughly <n>
eligible stores, so a filtered territory view shows a third to a half of that territory ranked on
the wrong population, and the Read Me prints the actual figures from the run; on a national file
of 300,000 records the published 570 is under one percent, and holding a given market's top
twenty would require publishing the top two-thirds of the country, which the 20 + 50 + 500 caps
exist to prevent. A subordinate's report is obtained by running the skill at that scope, which
re-filters, recomputes percentiles and medians and renumbers from 1; the Read Me says so.

C4. The Thirteen Formatting Elements:

Elements 1 to 12 apply to all six store tabs on both lines; element 13 applies to Coverage Gaps
only and is scored not applicable elsewhere. All applicable elements are verified
programmatically in Stage 10, and any failure blocks publication.

1. Header row in row 1, no banner or note row above it.
2. Freeze panes at the first column after State, row 2, locking the header row plus Rank,
   <store id>, the span-of-control block, Store, Address, City and State. The cell is computed,
   not fixed: the seventh column on Line A, the eighth with one scope column, the eleventh with
   all four.
3. A real Excel Table over the exact populated block, no blank rows inside it.
4. Excel's built-in medium table style number 2, row stripes on, identical on every tab.
5. An explicit navy header band (solid fill 1F3864, Arial 10 bold, white font) on every header
   cell, applied explicitly because the element-7 gridlines are direct cell formatting and
   outrank the table style; row height 30.
6. Autofilter live on every column.
7. Thin 9BA7B5 gridlines on all four sides of every populated cell, header included.
8. Arial 10, top-aligned, wrap on.
9. Right-align exactly Rank, Priority, Scope rank, Volume, Category units per week, Vol %ile,
   Score, Must close, SKUs, Peer norm, SKU gap, Units/SKU, Est. upside u/wk, Assort %, Our
   assortment %, Competitor assortment %, Category units and Days since; every other column
   left; the list is closed.
10. Column widths set explicitly on every column.
11. The narrative column ("why" or "what to do") last and widest, 100 to 124 characters.
12. Row heights set explicitly wherever wrap is on.
13. Coverage alert shading on Coverage Gaps only: Days since amber (FFF2CC) at 150 to 179 days,
    red (F8CBAD) at 180 or more, black text; a lifestyle store readmitted by an escape is never
    shaded because it is covered as directed rather than on a call cycle; no other conditional
    shading exists.

The Read Me, Priorities and Method panels use the companion styling (navy 1F3864 title band, blue
4472C4 section headers, EAF0F8 label cells, thin 9BA7B5 borders, Arial 10 body). Formatting is
never traded for speed: written per tab, verified per tab, re-verified in Stage 10.

C5. Rank Continuity and the Caps:

Ranks form one unbroken sequence across the three merit tabs: Line A holds 1-10, 11-35 and 36
through the smaller of 535 and the eligible count; Line B holds 1-20, 21-70 and 71 through the
smaller of 570 and the eligible count. The 535 and 570 are derived last ranks (last visit rank
plus 500), never row caps and never carried literally: a resize recomputes the ceiling (Top 20 /
Next 40 ends at 560), and reading 535 as a row cap would ship 535 rows instead of 500 and fail
the "showing n of N" reconciliation. The last merit rank on a full run is 535 or 570, not 35 or
70. Directed is not a merit tab: its stores are ranked 1 to N among themselves and never consume
a merit slot. Directed, All Other Stores, Assortment Targets and Coverage Gaps are each capped at
500 rows independently and each states "showing n of N" on the tab and in Method.

C6. What the Contract Guarantees:

Verified in Stage 10 on every run of both lines: (1) nine tabs in C2 order; (2) each store tab
satisfies every C4 element that applies to it; (3) the C3 identity block is byte-identical in
structure across all six store tabs; (4) ranks are continuous across the merit tabs with no
<store id> on two tabs; (5) every store row has a populated identity block and narrative column;
(6) <store id> and Zip read as text; (7) zero characters above ASCII 126 in the workbook; (8)
every heading in Title Case; (9) each capped tab reconciles against its "showing n of N"; (10)
three random stores re-read from the planner match the workbook exactly.

There Is No Time Limit on This Task:

This governs every later decision. The user expects to come back to a finished, correct
workbook; a thirty-minute correct run is a success and a four-minute run with a truncated list,
an unverified tab or a silently wrong number is a failure. The assistant never shortens a list,
skips a verification, drops a tab or summarizes instead of building because the work is taking a
while; never stops at a partial result; treats a million-row planner like a two-hundred-row one
(chunked, checkpointed, worked to the last row); and works the Build Reliability Protocol's retry
paths when a step fails. The only acceptable early stops are the ten hard gates of the Execution
Architecture, most commonly no store identifier, no volume measure in any tier, an empty scope
after the text re-test, a file that cannot be opened, or a stage stuck after the full ladder and
one rebuild, each with a defined message. A missing <monthly POS kit> is not one (fall back to
the most recent kit), nor is a repeatedly failing stage (classify and work the retry path).
Fatigue, run length, retry count and output size are never on the list.

When to Use:

Anyone in the field organization, at any level, asking where to spend their time this cycle. All
of these resolve without a follow-up question: a plain ask ("what are my top opportunity
stores", "what should my team focus on"); a scope in plain words ("my top stores in section
<section id>", "the whole country"); a chain, place or attribute ("my top <chain A> opportunity
stores", "top opportunity stores in <city>, <state>", "top stores in zip <zip>", "my top
independent stores", "top stores in <county> County"); a category or brand ("my top
<business unit 4> stores", "a focus on the <our brand 4A> premium line", "best <our brand 2A>
opportunities"); an initiative ("the <flavor> expansion", "the <strength> rollout"); a size ("my
top 50", "just the top five"); any combination in any order; two planners with a comparison
asked for ("which stores declined the most"); a manager asking what someone is working on.

When Not to Use:

Editing, reformatting or charting a spreadsheet (the xlsx skill); writing the recap after visits
(a docx task); ranking, scoring or evaluating people (refused: stores only); no planner available
(the skill asks for it and never improvises a list).

Quick Start:

For "give me my top stores to visit for <business unit 4>" with the planner uploaded: (1)
identity, reading the signed-in user's profile, manager and today's date in parallel with the
planner read only; (2) role, geography word first then title, Line A (<territory role>) being
10 + 25 + up to 500 and Line B (manager tier) 20 + 50 + up to 500, one workbook per run; (3)
scope, padding what the user said to six digits, resolving an ambiguous code against the file
and filtering that level's own column as text, then qualifiers (Step 1b) on any other column the
request names, applied before the lifestyle exclusion, a miss reported with the nearest present
values and never as an empty report; (4) priorities from <user's manager> this month and last,
headquarters always, and mandatory-store calls; (5) the planner read from the session's input
folder or OneDrive (an email names it but cannot serve it); (6) parse by choosing the sheet,
detecting the header row, resolving the activity block and mapping columns by concept (Appendix
A); (7) rank by resolving directed stores first, dropping lifestyle stores unless one of three
escapes applies (directed, at or above the Step 4 scope median computed with lifestyle stores
included, or the request named the class), then scoring the rest as (1.0 + priority + gap +
1.2 x must-close + steer) x (volume percentile ^ 1.5) after the <sign type 1> state-guide
validation zeroes anything not executable there, ranking by score descending with zero-volume
stores bucketed last; (8) deliver the workbook, largest tab first, each tab's data and formatting
finished before the next, 50-row chunks each checkpointed by reading the range back, the Stage 10
gate over all thirteen elements on all six store tabs, then publish and a five-to-eight line
summary.

Core Instructions:

Step 1: Identity, Role, and Scope:

Who is asking and what they own is resolved from the login exactly as the Hub section describes
(profile and alias, the <shelf recognition model>'s user table and classification table, the
hierarchy table, explicit scope beating the default, the Scope line). This skill adds the
following.

- The identity reads (profile, manager, current date) run as one parallel batch. The priority
  searches are not batched with them, because priorities depend on the supervisor this step
  resolves and on the target cycle the parse produces; batching them once produced a priority
  set gathered against an unresolved supervisor.
- The manager lookup is mandatory and non-substitutable; a profile card has no manager field and
  answers "who do they report to" with a confident wrong answer. The lookup is repeated on each
  manager returned to walk the chain for Step 2.
- The role decides the line. <territory role> wording with no wider geography word is one
  territory, Top 10 then Next 25. <market support role> or <market role> wording is the whole
  market; <section role> the whole section; <region role> the whole region; vice president,
  president, chief, officer or general manager the whole span, national when the title names no
  geography; all Top 20 then Next 50. Anything else is inferred from the planner (one territory
  present means <territory role> sizing, anything wider means manager tier), asking only if the
  file cannot settle it.
- Abbreviations are expanded before matching (Dir, Sr, Assoc, Mgr, VP, SVP, EVP, Reg, Natl),
  because directory titles are abbreviated and a missed abbreviation falls through to inference
  when the record was unambiguous. Titles are matched case-insensitively, ignoring punctuation,
  taking the most senior token; a director or vice-president title containing "Sales" resolves to
  the senior tier.
- A geography word outranks the <territory role> words and is tested first: Region, Regional,
  Division, Divisional, Section, National, Area, Zone, Country or Enterprise anywhere in the title
  makes it manager tier. Only a title whose most senior token is the <territory role> wording and
  which names no geography wider than a territory gets 10 + 25. Method records the deciding
  token.
- The <shelf recognition model> is the authority when the title is ambiguous or names no
  geography, classified per the Hub section (territory row: Line A over the territory; market,
  market-support and leadership-development rows: Line B over the market; section and region
  rows: Line B over their code; account titles: Line B over the person's chains; no row or the
  headquarters code: headquarters). A headquarters person running someone else's geography gets
  that geography's shape, and the supervisor priorities come from that geography's chain of
  command in the hierarchy table, not the requester's own manager; Method says so.
- If the directory cannot be reached, the run proceeds on role and scope inferred from the
  planner, with Method noting what was assumed. Losing the directory costs the supervisor's
  priorities and the "my" shortcut, not the workbook.
- Both lines carry All Other Stores capped at 500 rows per C1 (routinely 80 to 110 on one
  territory, complete at that size). The user may override the counts in plain language ("my top
  50", "a hundred stores"): the number is honored, split roughly one-third and two-thirds, caps
  and gates unchanged, the size named in the Read Me and the tab names.
- "Manager tier" means <market support role>, <market role>, <section role>, <region role> and
  anything above, one predicate used everywhere and never worded to exclude the
  <market support role>.
- The planner is never the same size twice: nothing hard-codes a row count, column count,
  territory list, median or store count; every threshold is computed from the file and reported
  in Method. The 20 / 50 / 500 shape scales to any span (senior roles have less time, so the
  action list stays short while reference depth stays constant), serving a 200-store territory
  and a 20,000-store planner alike without degrading. The two visit lists never overlap.

One numbering family, four levels. Region, section, market and territory codes share one
six-character numeric family (region A00000, section AB0000, market ABCD00, territories ABCD01
onward) and are the easiest thing in the skill to get wrong.

- A market code ends in 00 with a non-zero third or fourth digit; a territory code shares the
  market's first four digits and ends in a non-zero two-digit suffix.
- A market stem whose fourth digit is zero pads to a code identical to a section code, and in
  the reference planner the Section column holds exactly such a code, so classifying by trailing
  zeros would scope a <market role> to a whole section. An ambiguous padded code is resolved
  against the file, never the digit pattern: the candidate is tested against each level's own
  column, the level returning rows wins, and if more than one does, the level named by the user's
  word is preferred, then the level their role implies (word first, then role, the single
  tie-break used throughout). Method states the reading.
- A department or org string carries the bare stem, not a filter value; it is expanded.
- One level's column is never filtered with another level's code and a stem is never treated as
  a territory. A <territory role> filters Territory to one six-digit value and leaves wider
  columns alone; <market support role> and <market role> filter Market and leave Territory
  alone; <section role> filters Section; <region role> filters Region; a national role applies
  no geographic filter.
- Codes are stored padded to six digits while people speak the short form ("market
  <market id>", "section <section id>"); both are accepted and compared only after padding. A
  code is never classified by digit count alone, and by trailing zeros only when unambiguous.

Each row gives the code pattern, the level it implies, and the column filtered.

- Last two digits not both zero: territory; the Territory column (<territory role>).
- Ends in 00 with a non-zero third or fourth digit: market; the Market column
  (<market support role>, <market role>).
- Ends in 0000 with a non-zero second digit: section; the Section column (<section role>).
- Ends in 00000: region; the Region column (<region role>).
- National role: no geographic filter; the whole file is ranked.
- Four or more trailing zeros, which overrides every row above: ambiguous, not classified from
  the pattern; each candidate level's own column is tested and the level that returns rows is
  taken.

The last row takes precedence because every section and region code also matches its own row.
The collisions are ordinary: a two-digit section stem whose second digit is zero pads to the same
code as the one-digit region stem and a four-digit market stem ending in 00, and a four-digit
market stem ending in 00 pads to the same code as its two-digit section stem; in the reference
planner both live Region and Section values sit in this space. Codes are hierarchical and
left-anchored (a region's digits open every section beneath it, and so on down), so a test that
only checks a trailing 00 mis-scopes a <section role> and a national executive at the first gate.

Scope codes are compared as text on both sides, never as numbers; a typed comparison against a
planner that stores the code as a string returns zero rows and looks like an empty scope. Both
sides are cast to text, trimmed, stripped of any decimal tail and separators, and the column
value left-padded with zeros to the code's length; the same applies to account numbers and zips.
If the requested scope sits at or above everything the file contains (every in-scope row already
carries the code as a left-anchored prefix), no filter is applied, the whole file is ranked and
Method says so; this is a normal path.

Accept Scope in Plain Language and Pad It Yourself:

The user speaks naturally and the skill does the arithmetic. "region <region id>", "my region"
and "my whole region" resolve to the region code padded to six digits; "section <section id>",
"my section" and "my span" to the padded section code; "market <market id>", "my market" and a
bare four-digit number to the padded market code; "territory <territory id>", a bare six-digit
number, "my territory", "my patch", "my area" and "my stores" to the territory code; "the whole
country", "national", "everything", "the enterprise", "company-wide" and "all my markets" (for a
national role) to no geographic filter; "my team", "my people", "everything I cover" and "my
org" to the signed-in user's own level. Padding rule: take the digits given, identify the level
from the digit count or the word used (region one digit, section two, market four, territory
six), and right-pad with zeros to six. The user is never asked to pad and a short code is never
rejected.

When the digit count alone is ambiguous (a bare two-digit number could be a section or a
two-digit region), the number is padded each way, each candidate tested against the matching
scope column, and the reading returning rows taken; if more than one does, the user's word wins,
then the role. Method says which reading was taken; a question is asked only when no reading
returns rows. The filter is applied on the column matching the level, not a string prefix; a
missing level column is derived by left-anchored prefix from the finest code present, noted in
Method. "My" always resolves through the directory. A scope that is not the user's own is
honored, with Method noting whose priorities were used, since the supervisor lookup still
follows the signed-in user unless they name someone else.

Resolving a manager-tier scope code when the user said no number, stopping at the first hit.
(1) A code or place name in the request. (2) The user's own directory record, every field
(title, department, office location, division, cost centre, custom or extension attributes),
with the digits pulled from whatever carries them and padded to the role's level. (3) The
manager's record, read the same way. (4) The file: exactly one distinct value in the role's level
column is the scope. (5) Saved memories from earlier runs, with whatever is settled persisted.
(6) Only then one question, offering the distinct values present with a recognizable label and
store count each. Steps 2 to 4 settle almost every manager-tier run; the skill never asks for a
number the records already hold.

A scope request can carry a <business unit> steer at any level ("top opportunity stores in
region <region id> with a focus on the <our brand 4A> premium line" is a TILT at region scope;
"my top <business unit 4> stores in section <section id>" is a FILTER at section scope), and a
comparison can combine with both. Scope word, "my", a bare number, a unit, a brand, an
initiative, a chain, a place, a store attribute, a count and a comparison verb can arrive in any
order; each is parsed independently, all applied together, and the Read Me states the reading. A
clarifying question is reserved for a scope that returns no rows under any reading.

Resolving a <territory role>'s territory: every record is searched first and asking is the last
resort, because records vary by person and market. The ladder, stopping at the first hit. (1) A
territory or market number in the request. (2) The user's own directory record, every field
(title, department, office location, business phone extension, custom attributes); a four-digit
market stem is as useful as a six-digit territory because it narrows the file to one market.
(3) The manager's record, whose department usually names the market. (4) Saved memories from a
previous run. (5) Only one territory present in the file. (6) A territory or market number in the
planner filename or the planner email's subject or body. (7) The market narrows it to a short
list, offered rather than an open question; this is the common case, since planners are published
at market level and a <territory role> normally holds every territory in the market with a record
naming only the market. The territories present are listed, each labeled with its two or three
largest towns and its store count, and a town name, list position or number is accepted. (8) The
answer is persisted to memory immediately and the save confirmed, so the question is never asked
again.

If a market resolves but a territory does not, the question is narrowed to that market's
territories. When a question is unavoidable it is asked in the user's language (towns and store
counts), never the file's codes. The settled territory is saved under a non-empty key with a
short value naming territory and market; the save is verified, retried once on failure, and the
user told plainly if it still fails. The scope value used and rows kept are always reported. Zero
rows means the filter is wrong before it means the scope is empty: the text comparison and the
level reading are re-tested first, and only then does the skill stop and list the distinct values
at every scope level.

Step 1b: Request Qualifiers - Filter on Any Column the User Names:

A request can name a restriction that is neither an org code nor a <business unit> ("my top
<chain A> opportunity stores", "top opportunity stores in <city>, <state>", "my top independent
stores in <state>"): a column value, as real a population as a territory. Three independent axes
are resolved separately and applied together: SCOPE (whose stores; the org code family), QUALIFIER
(which subset; any other planner column matched on its values: "<chain A>", "in <state>", "zip
<zip>", "independents", "<county> County") and STEER (what to lean on; the unit, brand or
initiative). "My top <chain A> <business unit 3> opportunity stores in <state>" carries a scope,
two qualifiers and a steer, all applied; the user is never asked to restate a multi-axis
request.

Step 1b - Resolving a Qualifier:

Resolved against the file's actual values, never a guess or a hard-coded list.

1. Take the phrases that are not scope, steer, a count or a comparison verb.
2. Classify each phrase by type first, then search only that type's columns, so that a miss has a
   column to report and near-values to offer. The cues, in order, are these.

   - "independent(s)", "chain(s)", "lifestyle": Attribute, resolved by the attribute map below,
     by rule, never by value match.
   - A state name or two-letter code: State, the state column.
   - Ends in "County", "Parish", "Borough": County, the county column only, suffix stripped.
   - Five digits, or "zip" / "postal": Zip, the zip column.
   - Follows "in" and names a place, or is a known city: City, the city column, then the county
     column.
   - A retailer or banner name: Chain, <chain name column>, then <secondary chain name column>.
   - A wholesaler or supplier name: Distributor, any primary distributor column and the
     third-party provider column.
   - A band or status label: Attribute value, in the <volume index> decile, the <our brand 4A>
     premium line performance identifier, <price support program> alignment, the <category 2>
     cluster, trade class, trade subclass and channel columns.
   - None of the above: Unrouted, every remaining resolved column, then the miss table.

   A comma splits a compound place phrase before classification: "<city>, <state>" becomes a
   City phrase and a State phrase, matched independently and applied with AND.
3. Attribute phrases resolve by rule before any value search, because their values are absences.
   "independent(s)" and "indies" mean <chain name column> blank after null-like normalization;
   "chain(s)" and "chain stores" mean it populated; "lifestyle (stores)" means
   <lifestyle store flag> set. Value-searching the word "independent" finds nothing, falls to the miss path and
   widens to the full scope.
4. A state name is normalized to its two-letter postal code through a full 50-state table;
   substring matching cannot do this. Either form is accepted, the code is confirmed present in
   the column before filtering, and if the file stores full names the name is matched instead.
5. The administrative suffix is stripped before matching a county and the phrase routed to the
   county column only; routed loosely, the stem can match a like-named city and scope the report
   to one hamlet inside the county.
6. Values are matched the way people type them, within the routed columns only: case-fold, strip
   punctuation, collapse whitespace, then exact, normalized-exact, prefix, then substring in
   either direction (so "<chain A>" matches a stored "<chain A> #<store number>"). Bidirectional
   matching never runs across the whole sheet.
7. Method records the phrase, type, column, operator and matched value set with the row count
   each contributed.

Step 1b - The Cases That Actually Come Up:

A chain name is matched on <chain name column> first, then <secondary chain name column>; some
banners are booked under a parent company, so few or no rows means checking the other chain
column and a parent-company spelling before reporting a miss. A state is tested as both code and
full name against the distinct values present. A city with its state is split on the comma, the
multi-word city kept as one value, both halves applied with AND; a city named alone that appears
in more than one in-scope state has its states reported and the one inside the user's scope
taken. A county, zip, decile band, performance identifier or distributor follows the same
procedure, zips compared as text left-padded to five. "Independent" and "chain" are attribute
qualifiers: a blank <chain name column> after null-like normalization, consistent with the Chain
column and the independents-only gates, and its complement.

Step 1b - Where a Qualifier Sits in the Pipeline, and Why It Must Sit There:

Qualifiers are applied in the scope-resolution stage, immediately after the org-code filter and
before the closed-store exclusion, the lifestyle exclusion, the percentile and the ranking, so
the qualified set is the population for everything downstream and a chain's stores are ranked
among themselves with percentiles, scope median and peer norms computed over that chain. The
qualifier changes who is in the population, never the method. The downstream guards work
unmodified: a qualifier leaving 12 stores hits the degenerate-scope guard (fewer eligible stores
than the list size means every one is delivered and the tab says so, never padded), and one
leaving one or two stores skips percentile weighting and ranks on raw volume.

Step 1b - A Miss Is Reported Loudly. It Is Never an Empty Report:

A qualifier matching nothing is the highest-risk path because an empty report looks finished.
Three outcomes, never collapsed. (1) The qualifier matched rows: proceed, naming column, value
and row count in the Read Me and Method. (2) The phrase routed to a column type but no value
matched: stop before ranking and ask (hard gate 7), reporting the phrase, the column tested and
the ten nearest values present in that column, ranked by ascending normalized edit distance with
ties broken alphabetically so the same miss always returns the same ten; never an empty workbook
and never a fall-through to outcome 3. (3) The phrase routed to no column type: say which columns
were tested, run the request without the term, label the report as unqualified in the Read Me
and the opener, and name the dropped phrase. Outcome 3 is never taken for a phrase that
classified. "The column is absent" is distinguished from "the value is not in it". The skill
never silently widens, because a report quietly covering the whole span when one city was asked
for is internally consistent and therefore destroys trust.

Step 1b - State It Where the User Will See It:

The Read Me names every qualifier applied, its column and the store count ("<chain A> stores
only, matched on chain name: <n> of your <n> stores"); Method adds the operator and matched
value set. Tab names stay as the contract defines them.

Step 2: Priorities, Three Sources, Always All Three:

A. Supervisor, this cycle, always first. The user's own boss outranks every other source;
headquarters is the cross-check and fallback. Every message from every manager in the chain,
from every folder, is retrieved on every run with no keyword filter, and read afterward.

1. Every person in the chain: the supervisor, their manager, and up to four levels or until the
   chain returns empty.
2. Every folder: Inbox, every Inbox subfolder, Archive and Deleted Items (direction is routinely
   read, actioned and deleted). Folders are enumerated and each swept.
3. Two windows, both run and read for every manager in every folder: the last 30 days (this
   cycle's direction, always live) and the last 60 days (read into the inventory always, its
   store lists directed only under the carry-forward rule in B). Both counts are reported in
   Method with a per-manager statement of whether prior-cycle messages carried forward and why.
4. Filtered by sender only; everything they sent is retrieved and the subjects read.
5. Each message is classified by content, not keyword or length. It is direction when it names
   or attaches a store list (<store ids>, store names, an attached or referenced spreadsheet);
   names a <business unit>, brand, initiative, activity, segment or performance gap to work
   (pass-through, prebook, distribution, assortment, contract, loyalty, backbar, expansion and
   similar); states a deadline, focus, priority or must-do; or forwards or references the planner
   or the <monthly POS kit>. It is chatter only when it does none of these and is purely
   conversational or administrative (a thank-you, logistics with no store or brand content, an
   auto-reply, a payroll, HR, IT, expense or facilities notice, a social note). In doubt it is
   kept, and nothing is discarded on the subject line alone: a subject of a unit name and
   exclamation marks or "<month> Planning" can carry the cycle's direction while "store list"
   can carry none. Method states the counts swept, read as direction, and discarded with a
   one-word reason each.

A keyword search never substitutes for the sweep and runs afterward only to catch other senders
quoting a manager. This is the skill's highest-frequency failure and it fails silently: in the
reference mailbox a keyword search for planning vocabulary returned one message while a sender
search over the same window returned more than twenty, missing roughly 95 percent of the
direction, because managers do not write in planning vocabulary. "No supervisor communication"
requires the sender search to have returned zero messages in the window; when messages were
returned but none read as direction, that is what is said, with count and window. Supplementary
keyword terms: "focus", "priorities", "cycle", "must win", "objectives", the unit and brand
names, and any message with an attached store list. Direction is recognized by what it asks for
(a store list, an underperforming segment, a pass-through gap, a prebook deadline, a planner
attachment), whatever the subject says.

The search is for the signed-in user's own supervisor in the signed-in user's own mailbox, since
a director's instructions to a market manager arrive in her inbox, not her reps'; a plan built
for someone else cannot see their supervisor's mail, and the skill says so rather than reporting
"no priorities found".

The supervisor is resolved with the manager lookup and nothing else; a profile card's silence
about a manager is silence about a field it never returns. Concluding "no supervisor" from one
deletes sources A and B and ranks on headquarters alone, which a real market build did for a
<market role> whose chain (<section role>, <region role>, <HQ role>) was in the directory all
along. Only one person in the company has no supervisor: "no supervisor" is recorded only when
the manager lookup was actually called for the signed-in user and returned empty and the user's
own title names the top of the house. An error is a failed lookup, retried once and then recorded
as such; an empty result for any other title means the directory is treated as incomplete and
Method says so.

The chain is walked upward by repeating the manager lookup, up to four levels or until empty,
and each person's messages are searched in the user's own mailbox with the same terms, weighted
by distance: immediate supervisor 1.00 (sets the cycle), two levels up 0.70 (sets the frame),
three levels up 0.50 (directional, rarely store-specific), four or more 0.30 (national context).
The weights scale the source boost term in the Step 6 scoring formula. Every level is recorded on
the Priorities tab with name, title and weight, including levels that published nothing
("resolved, no cycle communication found", never collapsed into "no supervisor exists"). Where a
higher level contradicts the immediate supervisor, the immediate supervisor is followed and the
disagreement named. A manager whose mailbox is unreachable is named as such and the run
continues.

B. Supervisor, prior cycle, evaluated manager by manager, never once for the chain. For each
manager the rules apply in order, stopping at the first that fires; rule 1 is the narrower case
and runs first, or rule 2 would swallow every carry-forward.

1. Direction in the 30-day window that signals continuation ("much of the same", "same as last
   month", "still in effect", "carry forward", "no change from" and similar phrasings): the
   lookback runs and the prior-cycle list it recovers is directed at full weight.
2. Direction in the 30-day window with no continuation signal: prior-cycle messages do not carry
   forward, because a manager who has spoken this cycle has superseded themselves. Method says
   the lookback was stopped for this manager and names the message.
3. Nothing in the 30-day window: the 60-day pass carries forward in full, directed at full
   weight and labeled with both dates.

Rule 2 is the one an implementer will skip; two independent runs against the reference mailbox
split there (the supervisor published direction on <date> inside the window, so her prior-month
message did not carry forward, and the run that carried it anyway produced a different eligible
pool and top ten). A directed store's weight never decays with the message's age: carry-forward
is a yes-or-no admission and an admitted list carries the full 2.00. If the supervisor published
nothing, the skill says so, names the searches and window, and ranks on the <monthly POS kit>
alone, never inferring priorities a manager never stated. At every level, flagged stores are
directed and lead the plan; otherwise the list runs on headquarters priorities, volume and
closing activities.

C. Headquarters: the monthly <monthly POS kit>, always, never optional. It is published monthly
by <sales org> sales strategy in the Documents library of the <kit library site> on <company>'s
SharePoint, and the same kit governs every market.

The session's input folder is checked before SharePoint: a PDF uploaded alongside the planner
whose name or first page carries the target month and the kit's name is the kit, read directly
and noted in Method as coming from the upload. The SharePoint and graph tools are deferred and
discovered before the first call. Verified reference values for the library (site display name,
site path, library name) shortcut discovery but are a starting point, not a hard-coded path. The
site is resolved, then its drives, then the Documents drive's children. Every file-reading tool
needs a drive id and an item path or id, none accepts a web URL, and a URL passed to the file
reader is rejected and can wrongly look like an unreachable document; the drive is resolved
first, always. A located PDF is fetched by drive id and item path and its text extracted with
layout preserved, because the due-date table's names and dates sit in adjacent cells and a flat
dump interleaves them. The <sign type 1> state guide lives in its own library addressed the same
way, the state file found by its two-letter code in the listing rather than an assumed path.

Otherwise the path is discovered every run, because folder names drift (two month folders differ
only in spacing after a leading numeral). (1) List the library root and read the actual folders.
(2) Select the folder whose name contains the target month and year, ignoring any leading
numeral, punctuation and spacing. (3) Read its children and take the kit PDF (typically "<year>
<month> <monthly POS kit>.pdf", but whatever PDF is there). (4) If the month folder is not at the
root, look under the archived kits folder, then search files tenant-wide or by drive for the kit
name. (5) If the site or library is unreachable, search for the kit by name across files before
giving up.

The kit is resolved for the cycle being planned, not "the latest", and the cycle is decided
before searching, never by asking. (1) A month the user named; a relative phrase ("this month",
"this cycle", "now") is not a named month and falls through. (2) The month in the planner's
filename or title and banner rows. (3) The initiative window printed in the planner or the most
recent kit, if it covers today. (4) The calendar: today's month if today is in its first
two-thirds, the next month if in the last third, because planning happens ahead of the cycle.
The resolved cycle, the rule that produced it and the kit that matched are stated in Method and
the opener.

The run never stops for a missing kit; an empty search means the month folder was not matched.
The ladder: the kit for the target cycle; then the kit for the current calendar month; then the
most recently published kit by folder or file date, with the opener and Method saying which month
it covers and that it is the latest available; then, only if the library is unreachable after a
name search across all files, supervisor priorities alone, with the workbook labeled prominently
as built without headquarters input and the unreachable source named. The user is never asked to
download or attach the kit, and the same holds for every source: if reachable, read it; if not,
say so and continue. A kit one month old is worth far more than no plan. The kit also arrives by
email from <sales org> communications as a newsletter whose body is only a tracking link to
<internal portal>, which cannot be fetched; the email confirms the kit exists and its date, and
the content is read from SharePoint.

Step 2b: Validate Priorities Against the <sign type 1> State Guide Before Weighting Anything:

An initiative that cannot legally or contractually be executed in the user's state is not an
opportunity and is never weighted as one; headquarters priorities are national, execution rules
are not, and the check runs before scoring. The <sign type 1> guidelines live in the same library
as the kit, in their own folder: one PDF per state named by two-letter code, the master
guideline, the flavor restriction guide, and execution guides per <business unit>.

1. Resolve the distinct states in scope from the planner's state column; check each state once,
   cache the reading, apply it per store, and report the states checked.
2. Discover the folder as for the kit: upload folder first for an attached state guideline PDF,
   then list the library and match on name. Never a hard-coded path.
3. Read the state PDF, and the flavor restriction guide whenever a priority names a flavor, a
   flavor expansion or a flavored SKU.
4. For each headquarters and supervisor priority, confirm the activity is permitted in that
   state.
5. If the planner has no state column, fall back to the master guideline and the flavor
   restriction guide, say so in Method and the opener, and treat anything they do not cover as
   unvalidated rather than permitted.

The finding decides the scoring effect.

- Permitted: weighted normally.
- Restricted or prohibited in this state: weight 0, removed from the ranking, named in Method
  with the state rule.
- Permitted with a condition (different POS or configuration): weighted normally, the condition
  carried into the store's action text.
- Guide unreachable: fails closed on the priorities, not the run. Unvalidated priorities are
  held back, the ranking runs on volume and gaps, and Method and the opener name what could not
  be verified and which priorities were held back.
- No PDF for the user's state: fall back to the master guideline and the flavor restriction
  guide, say so, and treat anything they do not cover as unvalidated.

A priority is never silently dropped; every dropped priority is listed in Method with its reason.
This is a compliance guardrail, not an optimization.

From the kit the skill extracts and quotes the initiative window printed at the top (the cycle's
close date), the initiatives grouped by <business unit>, the type published against each, and
any targeting method. The kit's structured initiative table is the authoritative source for close
dates, work types and <targeting program> eligibility, parsed and never inferred. Its columns are
the initiative description (the name, with "Type: Deadline | <work type>" beneath it), matched to
activity headers with the work type read after the pipe; the due column, the initiative's own
close date, banded against the cycle window in Step 6; supporting resources, a link and sometimes
a scope caveat such as "<jurisdiction> only", honored as a state or chain restriction; and the
eligibility flag (Y or N) for <targeting program> targeting. The work type (merchandising, price
validation, education, order entry) is carried into scoring; an unlisted type defaults to full
weight and is named in Method.

The table does not extract cleanly, because name, type line, due value and flag are separate text
runs at different horizontal positions. The parse: (1) extract with layout preserved; (2) if
layout mode is unavailable or ambiguous, OCR the page as a normal tool, not a last resort; (3)
pair by proximity (a due value belongs to the nearest name above or beside it) and confirm every
pairing against a second signal (the type line under the same name, the resources text on the
row, a month in the initiative's name, the matching planner activity column), two agreeing
readings being a confirmed pair and one a candidate; (4) report in Method how many due dates were
confirmed by two readings, rest on one, or could not be paired.

A due date that cannot be read is banded by duration and never defaulted to 1.00, since treating
an unparsed date as "closes this cycle" is the over-weighting bug this section prevents. In this
cycle's kit with no readable date, 0.75; a name or resources text implying next cycle or quarter
end, 0.50; anything reading beyond roughly 90 days, 0.20 (nearly every <sales org> initiative
falls in a 30, 60 or 90 day window, so a far date is a misparse or genuinely low priority, both
handled by 0.20); nothing readable and not in this cycle's kit, 0.00 and excluded. Every
initiative that took a fallback weight is named in Method with the weight.

The due date is per initiative and often falls outside the cycle window: in the reference kit
most initiatives were due at the window's end, but a national expansion of the <our brand 4A>
premium line at a new <strength> was due a month later and an EFT enrollment pair months out, and
treating everything as closing this cycle applied full weight to the single largest activity on
the reference market. The date is read; the window is never assumed. A scope caveat in the
resources column is honored: an allowance-change initiative whose title carries a two-letter code
and whose resources say "<jurisdiction> only" is restricted to that state (the code is a state,
not a region), and an initiative restricted to a state the user does not operate in is dropped
entirely and named in Method. If the kit is present but its window cannot be read, the window is
derived from the target month's own span, Method and the opener say it was derived, and the run
continues rather than zeroing the whole priority and must-close terms. The planner's own activity
tags, which encode origin (Step 4), are then read; both sources are used.

Any store list from anyone in the management chain is a must-cover, with no trigger phrase
required. Only an explicit release ("do not cover", "no action needed", "FYI only",
"informational", "already handled", "for awareness") removes a store; otherwise every store on
the list is directed at weight 2.00. A store list is an attached spreadsheet of accounts, store
names or <store ids> in the body, a forwarded report with stores highlighted, or a message naming
even one store; in the reference mailbox an "underperforming <business unit 4> stores" message
and a "<our brand 3A> pass-through gaps" message were both direction with no mandatory-sounding
phrase.

A store list is not automatically a directive: the actor test of Step 5 runs on every list before
weighting. A list naming another person as the one working the stores is an assignment and
routes to the 0.50 <market support role> down-weight, not to the Directed tab at 2.00; the
reference mailbox carries exactly such a list, whose body describes the stores the
<market support role> will plan against, and the run that directed it inverted the weighting by a factor
of four. Everything else, including a bare list with no framing, is a directive; in doubt,
directed, because covering a store mentioned in passing costs one visit and missing an expected
one costs credibility. Directed stores are eligible and ranked regardless of <business unit> rank
or the lifestyle exclusion, at 2.00, even for a low-ranked unit (a directed <business unit 3>
store outranks an undirected <business unit 1> store), flagged with sender and date.

A named store is matched to a planner row in order: account number; store name plus city; store
name plus street address; store name alone when unique in scope, all as normalized text. A named
store that cannot be matched is listed on the Directed tab with a blank rank and a "named but not
found in this planner" note, the count repeated in the opener; never dropped. A store's identity
is never inferred from an aggregate (a count, percentage or summary figure without a name) by
matching the figure against a planner flag, however uniquely it seems to resolve. A message that
directs work but names no resolvable store is recorded on the Directed tab with a blank
<store id> and the quoted wording, reported in the opener as direction not resolvable to a store,
and leaves the merit ranking untouched; the third part is never skipped.

Step 2b - A Store List Attached by a Manager Is the Highest-Value Object in the Mailbox. Retrieve It:

A store list from the user's boss outranks every other input: more specific than the kit, more
current than the planner, more binding than any priority phrase. Retrieving it is mandatory;
failing to open an attachment is a reason to try the next path, never to report no direction.
The ladder runs for every attachment on every message from every manager in the chain, stopping
at the first path returning readable rows. The mail and graph tools are deferred and discovered
first.

1. List the manager's messages filtered to those with attachments, 50 at a time. The response
   almost always spills to a results file rather than returning inline; the file is read from the
   shell and the message ids taken from it. A spilled response treated as empty reports no mail
   from a manager who wrote twenty messages.
2. Enumerate each message's attachments through the graph query on its attachment collection
   (id, name, size, content type, inline flag). The subject never decides whether a list is
   present; the attachment filename often is the list (in the reference mailbox a workbook named
   for the <market support role>'s stores hung off a message whose subject named a colleague's
   list). The reported size is MIME-encoded, roughly 1.4 times the file, and never used to judge
   a file too big.
3. Fetch the bytes one attachment at a time by requesting the single attachment resource with no
   query parameters at all; the base64 payload is in its content-bytes field, decoded, written to
   the session's working folder and opened with the same spreadsheet library as the planner.
   Selecting the content-bytes field instead is the most common failure and has failed a real
   run: it returns HTTP 400 (the field belongs to the file-attachment subtype), and the error
   text points to the message metadata tool, which returns metadata only, so a reader who
   follows it loops and wrongly concludes the attachment is unreachable. After a 400 the recovery
   is one bare re-issue with the query parameters omitted; no escalation to the raw-value
   endpoint, no fallback to metadata, and the attachment is not reported unretrievable until the
   bare call has been made at least twice.
4. A truncated response is recovered, not reported unread. The ceiling is 102,400 characters of
   response, roughly 76,100 bytes of decoded content after the JSON envelope: the received prefix
   is decoded, the ZIP local-file headers walked and the worksheet part stream-inflated. The
   recovered row count is checked against any per-territory pivot the message body prints (in the
   reference mailbox the counts reconciled exactly). A spreadsheet often declares a far larger
   dimension than it holds (the reference list declared <n> rows and carried <n> real ones), so a
   truncated fetch can recover 100 percent of the data.
5. A file referenced rather than attached is resolved in SharePoint or OneDrive by filename.
6. Each path is retried at least twice before moving on; transient failures are indistinguishable
   from real ones on one attempt.

Four reasonable-looking approaches are forbidden, all tested and all failing: the raw-value
endpoint on the attachment (worst, because it does not error; it returns a text-decoded body with
roughly 37 percent of the bytes silently corrupted into replacement characters); selecting the
content-bytes field or the type annotation (HTTP 400); expanding attachments on the message (the
same size ceiling at the cost of a larger call); and the message metadata tool (metadata and
pointers only, never bytes).

The retrieval gate then applies, a hard gate that fails closed: a run may not publish fewer
directed stores than the mailbox evidence supports. Before the Directed tab is written, every
attachment on a manager message is confirmed as one of: read and its stores matched against the
planner; read and holding no stores in scope, stated in Method; or unreadable after the full
ladder, in which case the run names the file, sender and date, states every path attempted, says
in the opener that a named manager's store list could not be opened and directed coverage is
incomplete, and writes a blank-<store id> row on the Directed tab with the same message. A
directed count of zero is valid only when no manager sent a list; two of three independent runs
on the same mailbox opened the file and found the directed store, the third reported none.

A partially read list is treated as partial: named in the opener and Method, how much was read
stated, only the stores actually read directed. But a partial fetch is not partial data: the
recovered rows are reconciled against any per-territory or per-group count the body prints, and
when they agree exactly the list is reported complete with a note that the fetch truncated and
how recovery was confirmed; when they disagree, or no counts exist, it is reported partial with
what is missing named.

Every priority is quoted verbatim with sender and date. A source that yields nothing is said to
have yielded nothing, clearly flagged, and the run proceeds with what exists. A priority is never
invented.

Step 3: Get the Planner:

Email identifies the planner but usually cannot serve it: the mail tools return attachment metadata
only, and fetching attachment content through the graph query tool truncates near 100 KB while
planners run 1 to 1.5 MB, so the fetch silently returns a fragment. The assistant reads the bytes
from the first source that yields the whole file:

1. The session's input folder, searched for any spreadsheet. This is the normal path and is checked
  first. An attached zip counts: anything the user attached, including a package's test-inputs
  folder, is inspected before OneDrive, and the file used is named.
2. OneDrive or SharePoint: search the drive, then download the file.
3. The email attachment, only for small workbooks; anything truncated is discarded.

When more than one workbook is present, the assistant decides which is which before parsing,
resolving each file's cycle from its filename, banner rows and volume time basis. For a comparison
request the later cycle is the current planner and the earlier is the baseline, stated in the Read
Me and in Method; otherwise the file matching the target cycle is used and the others are named as
present but unused. Planners are never silently merged, and never picked by upload order or filename
sort.

Email still supplies the planner's name, sender and date for Method: the assistant lists the
supervisor's messages that carry attachments and prefers the most recent planner matching the cycle
month. If the uploaded file matches the emailed attachment on name and size, it is the same file;
the assistant says so and moves on without hedging or asking the user to confirm provenance.
Bringing in the planner is the user's normal job, not a degraded path.

A rep's own territory file is ideal, not required. Planners are usually published at market level
and filtered downward, so when no territory-scoped file exists the assistant looks for the market
planner, then section, then region (each contains the level below; Method states the level used and
that it was filtered down), and failing that accepts any workbook whose headers resolve: a
scheduling resource, a performance extract, a chain report. The header dictionary is the test, not
the filename.

Admission is column coverage, not title. A file is usable when the resolver finds at minimum a store
identifier (the <store id> column), a volume measure and any one scope column (territory, market,
section or region); one scope column suffices because finer levels are read off it by left-anchored
prefix and coarser ones by truncation. Everything else degrades gracefully: with no activity columns
the ranking falls back to volume plus performance gaps, still a defensible list. Method names every
column that resolved and every one that did not. The assistant refuses only when one of the minimum
three is absent, says which one and what the file did contain, and never ranks from a file with no
volume measure.

A partial workbook is never analyzed. The assistant confirms the file opens, then counts records
(rows with a non-blank <store id>) rather than trusting the sheet's reported extent, which routinely
overstates by thousands of empty rows. Zero records is a hard gate. A small but non-zero count
cannot be judged against an expectation the skill is forbidden to carry, so no threshold is guessed;
Method reports the record count, the reported extent and the sheet chosen, and if another sheet
resolves the required columns with materially more records, the assistant says so and justifies its
choice.

Step 4: Parse Defensively:

Column names, sheet names and header position change every cycle, so nothing is hardcoded and the
header dictionary in Appendix A governs. The assistant chooses the sheet, detects the header row by
scanning the first 15 rows for the one with the most non-empty text cells (row 1 in a scheduling
extract, row 4 in a planning tool under banner and count rows), resolves the activity block, then
resolves columns by concept, case-insensitively with punctuation stripped, and records the mapping
for Method. If a scope column or the volume measure cannot be resolved, the run stops and says so.

Values are coerced before they are tested, because a planner's types change between cycles:
numeric-looking text is read as a number, date-looking text is parsed as a date (year-month-day,
month/day/year, and year-month-day with a time), and identifiers and scope codes are compared as
normalized text. A typed comparison against the wrong type fails silently and looks exactly like a
column with no data.

A column that resolves but is empty for every row in scope is treated as absent. The assistant
checks each resolved column's non-null count within scope; at zero the column is excluded from every
flag, gate, weight and benchmark and listed in Method as resolved but empty (otherwise an empty
contract column would report every independent as missing every contract, and an empty assortment
column would put every store on the assortment tab). The same rule applies to activity columns:
empty ones are excluded from the priority term, the must-close set, the narrowness boost and the
Method activity inventory, since several reference-planner activity columns claim hundreds of stores
in the count row and carry no flags in the data.

Activity columns are tagged by originator: a headquarters-only prefix naming the
<market support role> as the actor, a prefix saying <territory role> action is needed, a letter plus
market id for market work, and a geography id followed by "Activity", the words "Local Activity", or
a geography id plus the planner's local-plan abbreviation for local work. The count row above the
header is a national count and is never used for scoring: in the reference planner every one of its
values disagrees with the in-file counts and it claims hundreds of stores for an activity no store
in the market carries, so used as a narrowness signal it inverts the ranking.

Foreign market tags are kept, scored and labeled, never silently dropped. A planner circulated
inside one market routinely carries columns tagged for other markets (a dozen in the reference
file); an activity flagged on an in-scope store is work on that store whoever authored it, so it is
scored normally, the authoring tag is recorded in the activity inventory, and Method counts the
columns tagged from another market. The tag is never an admission test in either direction.

Per-activity store counts are computed from the filtered scope after the scope filter is applied,
because a narrow activity is a sharper priority than a blanket one only when judged against the
user's own stores.

Step 5: Population Exclusions and <business unit> Weighting:

Temporarily closed stores are excluded before anything else, with no exception. The assistant
resolves the store-status concept from Appendix A (<temporarily closed flag> and its synonyms) and
drops every flagged store from every list, including the directed-store section and the assortment
tab, because a closed store's historical volume reads as a strong performer and sending a rep to a
locked door destroys the report's credibility. Method reports the excluded count; if the column is
missing entirely, Method says so rather than assuming every store is open.

The population is sorted descending by volume, resolved by stem through the Appendix A tiers: total
<industry> equivalent volume first, then industry or total <category 1> cartons (the planner's
recurring misspelling of the volume word is real and is matched), then <business unit 1> cartons,
then a composite percentile built from the per-<business unit> columns. The first tier that resolves
and is populated is used. Method states the tier, its exact column name and its time basis; the
column suffix (last 13 weeks, year to date, last 52 weeks) is the basis and is reported, never
assumed.

Step 5: Population Exclusions and <business unit> Weighting - Lifestyle Stores Are Removed From the Workbook Entirely:

The lifestyle flag is resolved by exact column name first (<lifestyle store flag>); only if that column is
absent does the assistant accept one whose normalized name is the bare lifestyle word or its flag
form. It never falls back to <chain flag>, <trade class column>, <trade subclass column>,
<channel column> or <store type column>, which share the synonym group but describe different things
(the chain flag would remove roughly three times as many stores). If no lifestyle column resolves,
or it resolves but is empty, no exclusion is applied, Method and the opener say so, and the whole
population is ranked; a missing flag is a reason to report, never to guess.

Otherwise every flagged store is dropped from the workbook: not on the visit lists, Coverage Gaps,
Assortment Targets or the reference tab, not counted in N or any median, not ranked. The flag is
<sales org>'s own classification for outlets where <industry> is incidental to the shopping trip; in
the reference market nearly all are two small-box discount chains (<chain B> and <chain C>) plus
supercenter fuel centers, travel plazas and supermarkets. They are covered as directed (yearly or
every other year) rather than on a cycle, so a long interval is not a coverage gap. The planner's
own flag is used rather than chain-name matching so the rule travels to any market without a name
list.

The pipeline order, stated once and authoritative; no stage may be reordered:

1. Resolve scope and apply every qualifier or filter steer.
2. Apply the closed-store exclusion.
3. Normalize volume: zero, blank, null and negative all become 0.
4. Compute the scope median over this population, lifestyle stores included. This is the escape 2
  threshold and the only median computed before the exclusion.
5. Resolve the directed set from the mailbox.
6. Apply the lifestyle exclusion and its three escapes. What survives is the ranked population.
7. Compute percentiles, every other median and every peer norm over the ranked population, directed
  stores included; a directed store is a real store whose score needs a percentile. This population
  is the N of the formula.
8. Only now split: directed stores go to their own tab and everything else competes for the merit
  tiers. The split is routing, not a population change; the eligible count is the merit remainder,
  but N and every median stay fixed from step 7.

"Scope median" means the step 7 median everywhere (the coverage flag, the contract membership gate,
the units-per-SKU comparison) except in escape 2, which uses the step 4 median. The two differ
materially (about two thirds in the reference territory), so the wrong one admits or excludes the
wrong lifestyle stores.

Three escapes, and only three, each evaluated per run against live data:

1. A supervisor named the store. A directed store is never removed, whatever its class. Directed
  stores are resolved from the mailbox first, then the exclusion is applied to everything not
  directed; reversing the order silently deletes a store the user's own manager named and raises no
  error. Method states that the directed set was resolved before the class filter ran.
2. The store reaches the upper half of its scope by volume. The median of the ranking measure over
  every in-scope open store, lifestyle stores included, admits any lifestyle store at or above it,
  which then competes normally. Computing that median before the exclusion makes the test "half the
  stores in this territory" rather than "half the lifestyle stores", which would readmit about half
  of them.
3. The user asked for them by name. A request naming the class or the chain ("my <chain B> stores",
  "top lifestyle accounts", "what do I have at <chain C>") overrides the exclusion for that run; the
  qualifier axis in Step 1b already resolves these phrases, and the class filter must not silently
  defeat them. The report is built over lifestyle stores ranked among themselves, and the opener
  says the standing exclusion was lifted because the request named the class.

Escape 2 is expected to admit nothing and is kept anyway: in the reference market no lifestyle store
reaches its scope median, but the best misses by about four percent, so a denser market will fire
it, and a rule that costs nothing when idle keeps the skill portable. Method reports the exclusion
every run: the count removed, the count readmitted by each escape and the median used.

Step 5: Population Exclusions and <business unit> Weighting - There Is No Volume Floor. Rank the Whole Population:

Every store is ranked strictly by score, descending, and the tab sizes decide what appears: no
two-thirds cut, no median cut, no minimum volume. The visit tabs take the top 10 and the next 25 (or
20 and 50), the reference tab up to 500 more, and a store that does not appear ranked below the cut
rather than being removed by a threshold. Volume says what a store is worth per visit, not whether
it needs one: in the reference territory a two-thirds floor, calibrated when a third of the
population was <chain B>, cut genuine convenience stores once the lifestyle stores were removed.
Capacity is already the honest constraint (roughly 130 rankable stores against a schedule of about
100), so the ranking does the floor's job by merit.

A store with no volume history is ranked last, never dropped; a store that opened this cycle should
be visible at the bottom, not vanish until it has thirteen weeks of history. Usable volume,
redefined and mandatory: a blank, null, non-numeric, zero or negative volume is included in the
population as 0, counted in N, given the lowest percentile and ranked last in the zero-volume
bucket. Only the closed-store gate and the lifestyle exclusion remove stores from the ranked
population; directed stores remain in it for N, every percentile and every median, and are routed to
their own tab afterwards. The Method funnel reports the count of zero-and-null volume stores.

The volume percentile is computed over the whole in-scope population by the average-rank method in
the scoring formula. It never reaches zero (the minimum is k plus 1 divided by twice N, for k stores
tied at the bottom), so no store is annihilated to a hard zero and the smallest stores are still
ordered by priority and workload. That is why the zero-volume bucket is mandatory: without it a
zero-volume store with enough open work outranks a small store with none. The bucket is a sort key
applied after scoring, and it is what makes "ranks last" true rather than approximate.

The <business unit> hierarchy: <company> limits how much focus each <business unit> can carry.
Values are: rank; business unit and the brand families that resolve to it; weight.

Directed by the supervisor: no rank; any mandatory-store callout; 2.00, which is not a business unit
  weight (see below).
Rank 1: <business unit 1>; <our brand 1A>, <price family A>, <price support program>,
  <our brand 1C>, <our brand 1B>; 1.00.
Rank 2: <business unit 4>; <our brand 4A> premium line, <our brand 4A> <price family B> line, the
  <strength> variant; 0.95.
Rank 3: <business unit 2>; <our brand 2A>, <our brand 2B>, <our brand 2D>, <loyalty program>; 0.70.
Rank 4: <business unit 5>; <category 5>; 0.45.
Rank 5: <business unit 3>; <our brand 3A>, <promotional price program>; 0.40.
Rank 6: <business unit 6>; <our brand 6A> and its shot format; 0.30.

The table is complete and has no default row. A national or headquarters-originated activity whose
originator tag resolves to none of the six takes no business unit weight at all and is reported in
Method as an unmapped originator; defaulting an unknown tag to 1.00 silently promotes it above five
business units at once (in a real build an unmapped <business unit 6> activity defaulted to 1.00 and
became the largest per-store contributor where it should have been the smallest).

A local activity is the exception and is never zeroed by that rule: an activity carrying the user's
own region, section, market or territory id is supervisor-loaded work and the highest-signal
activity in the planner. Its business unit is resolved in this order, stopping at the first that
fires:

1. A brand or product word in the activity's own header, mapped through the brand table.
2. Evidence elsewhere in the planner: a companion column naming the same shorthand with a brand
  attached, or the unit of measure the gap is stated in (a brandless reference-planner gap activity
  resolved to <business unit 3> because a companion column paired its product shorthand with
  <our brand 3A>). Method states the evidence.
3. A supervisor message naming the same shorthand with a business unit or brand attached.
4. Otherwise the local activity takes the lowest weight in the hierarchy (0.30 today), never 1.00,
  and Method records that the business unit could not be determined and the floor was applied. This
  is the one place a floor default is correct: the local activity stays fully protected (admitted
  automatically, close weight 1.00, source boost 1.5, local boost 1.25), so it can never be zeroed,
  and because every real weight is at or above 0.30, resolving the business unit can only raise its
  score, whereas defaulting to 1.00 would rank an unresolved local above a correctly resolved
  lower-weighted one. The default is tied to the hierarchy floor so it moves if the lowest weight
  changes; 1.00 is a real business unit's weight and is never hard-coded here.

An unresolved business unit never removes a local activity from scoring; in a real run this decision
set ranks 2 and 3 of a territory list.

The directed 2.00 is listed for comparison only. It is applied once, as a multiplier on the finished
score, never as a business unit weight inside the priority term (applying it in both places squares
it). Directed stores rank on their own tab, never inside the merit tiers, and since every directed
store receives the same 2.00 it does not reorder that tab; it is applied so the Score column matches
the formula.

The business unit weight scales the priority term only. The final order is the combined score,
descending, not raw volume; volume enters as the percentile raised to the power 1.5, which keeps big
stores at the top without making every other term decorative, so a high-volume <business unit 3>
store can outrank a small <business unit 1> store. Business unit rank alone never reorders the list.

Brand names resolve to their business unit; the user names brands, never business units. Any brand,
product or category word in the request is mapped before a steer is applied, case-insensitively and
tolerating the punctuation people type. Values are: what the user names; the business unit.

<category 1> words: <our brand 1A>, <price family A>, <our brand 1C>, <our brand 1B>,
  <price support program>, <category 1> and its slang; <business unit 1>.
<category 4> words: <our brand 4A> in any spelling, its premium and <price family B> lines, the
  <strength> variant, <business unit 4>, <category 4>, <volume index>; <business unit 4>.
<category 2> words: <our brand 2A> and its nickname, <our brand 2B>, <our brand 2D>,
  <loyalty program>, <category 2> and its slang; <business unit 2>.
<category 5> words: <business unit 5>, <category 5> in any phrasing; <business unit 5>.
<category 3> words: <our brand 3A> and its abbreviation, <business unit 3>,
  <promotional price program>, <category 3> in any phrasing; <business unit 3>.

A word naming a whole category steers to the <company> business unit that competes in it, but the
category volume columns include competitors: the assistant ranks on our own business unit's column,
uses the category column only to size the opportunity, and says which was which in Method.

A named initiative ("the flavor expansion", "the <strength> rollout", "the <loyalty program>
expansion", "the loyalty push") is a steer too, not a business unit. Its distinctive words are
matched case-insensitively against the resolved activity column names and the <monthly POS kit>
initiative titles, and a match is a tilt toward the stores carrying that activity. If nothing
matches, the assistant says so in one line, names the unresolved words and runs the request without
that term; it never stops.

Step 5: Population Exclusions and <business unit> Weighting - The <market support role> Down-Weight Applies to the <territory role> Line Only. Check the Report Line First:

Before halving anything for <market support role> coverage, the assistant checks which report line
is being built; this gate comes first and has no exception. Line A (requester is a
<territory role>): the halving applies, because the rep is choosing which doors to walk through and
a store the support person already covers is a duplicated trip. Line B (requester is a
<market support role>, <market role>, <section role>, <region role> or above): the halving never
applies and the store is not flagged as de-prioritized, because the <market role> deploys the
<market support role> and discounting those stores would misrepresent the manager's own deployment
back to them. Method states the decision either way: down-weight applied (Line A) or not applicable
(Line B).

Step 5: Population Exclusions and <business unit> Weighting - Finding the <market support role>'s Stores: The Territory Code First, the Mailbox Second:

Once the run is confirmed as Line A, the <market support role>'s store set is identified in this
order, stopping at the first that resolves; the territory code is authoritative whenever present and
is never skipped in favor of the mailbox.

1. The support territory code, derived from the user's own market id: that role carries a territory
  id in the same scope column as every <territory role>, formed as the market id followed by a fixed
  suffix (<market support role territory suffix>). It is derived from the market id, never by editing the user's
  territory digits, and compared as text. Every store carrying it is a covered store.
2. An assignment list from the mailbox, classified by the actor test below; used when the code
  returns nothing, and as a supplement when the code resolves but the list names stores outside it.
3. Activity columns carrying the headquarters-only tag or otherwise naming the <market support role>
  as actor; per-store flags rather than a roster.

Not every market has a <market support role>, and an absent support territory is a normal result.
When the derived code matches zero rows, Method says exactly that, naming the code, and the
assistant falls to source 2; when none of the three resolves, no halving is applied and Method
records that no such coverage was identified. The assistant never invents a covered set, never
assumes the pattern holds where the planner does not show it, and never stops for the absence (the
reference planner's market has no support territory, so its coverage comes from the mailbox, which
is expected). The code is derived, not pattern-matched: a territory ending in the suffix in a
different market is irrelevant, and the test is equality against the one derived code.

Covered stores are down-weighted by half because someone else is already working them: a
headquarters-only tagged activity, or any work assigned to the <market support role>, means POS and
inventory work there is already covered this cycle, so the store's score is multiplied by 0.50 when
its only open work is support-assigned. The store stays on the list and visible, so a genuinely big
opportunity still surfaces. A store carrying support work alongside its own rep-actionable work is
not halved, because the rep has their own reason to be there. The store's row states that the open
work is support-assigned, so a rep who sees it ranked low knows why and can override.

An assignment list that arrives by mail receives the same halving and is not a directive: a manager
who forwards the stores the support person will work has said those stores are covered, not that the
rep must cover them. It routes to the 0.50 rule, never to the Directed tab at 2.00; reversing this
inverts the weighting by a factor of four on exactly the stores the rule exists to de-prioritize. On
Line B the list is still identified and reported (name, sender and date in Method, a note on the
affected rows) but not weighted: the line gate suppresses the halving, never the reading.
Classification is line-independent and decides the tab; the line decides whether the 0.50 applies.

The actor test is who the message names as the actor, and it is the only test. Values are: what the
body says; what the list is; the weight.

Another actor named: the stores another person or role will work, plan against or is assigned
  ("stores that <colleague> will plan against", "support stores for this month"); an assignment
  list; 0.50 when the store's only open work is that support work.
No other actor named: the stores have a gap, are underperforming, need attention or should be
  covered; a directive; 2.00, Directed tab.
Nothing either way: a bare list of stores from a manager; a directive; 2.00, Directed tab, the
  default reading stands.

A store on an assignment list that also carries its own rep-actionable work is not halved; the
store's own open work is checked first, and list membership alone never halves. Method names the
list, its sender and date, how many in-scope stores it touched and how many were halved.

A directed set is a set of specific <store ids> built from a store list, never from an activity
column. A mandatory-store callout becomes a store set only when a supervisor message names specific
stores: as an attachment, as an inline list of <store ids> or store names, or as a referenced file
resolved by the attachment ladder; the rows matched to the planner by <store id> populate the
Directed tab and carry the 2.00 finished-score multiplier. A planner activity is loaded work that
scores through the priority term (and the local-activity path when it carries the user's own scope
id); it never places a store on the Directed tab and never carries 2.00. When one message both loads
an activity and attaches a store list, the directed set is the attachment and the activity scores
separately: a store on the list that also carries the activity receives the 2.00 once, from list
membership, and a priority contribution once, from the activity. If no store list resolves there is
no directed set from that message, and a directed set is never synthesized from an activity column's
flagged population, which would invert the weight by up to a factor of five and flood the Directed
tab. If an attachment was detected but could not be read after the full ladder, the retrieval gate
governs: the assistant fails loudly, names the file, sender and date, and writes the Directed row
with a blank <store id>.

A directed store carries 2.00, double the top business unit, above everything; the instruction
outranks every business unit weight and every exclusion and is applied once, on the finished score.
Directed stores sit in their own labeled section ("Directed Stores: Must Hit This Cycle") with the
sender, date and quoted instruction, ranked among themselves by the normal score, ahead of and
outside the ranked lists, so they consume no slots in the first or second tier; the tiers that
follow are merit-ranked and exclude them, so a Next 50 tab holds fifty merit-ranked stores whether
the directive named none or thirty. Directed membership never depends on the score; a directed store
with no volume, no activities and a zero score is still listed. The opener states the directed count
so nobody mistakes the section for part of the 50.

Step 6: Score, Close Date First, Never Activity Count:

An activity with no close date is weightless. The planner carries activities as binary flags with no
dates of their own, so the close window is derived by working these rules in order and stopping at
the first that fires; rule 1 is primary and sufficient on its own.

1. Named by the kit: the initiative's published due date is read from the kit table and banded
  against the cycle window. On or before the window's close is this cycle, 1.00; within the
  following cycle is 0.50; later is 0.20. The column name is not consulted and the window is not
  assumed, because the kit publishes a date per initiative and they routinely differ (in the
  reference kit the largest activity in the market was due in the following cycle and weighted
  0.50). Named by the supervisor for this cycle closes this cycle, 1.00; naming work for the cycle
  states it is due in it, and the same holds for a local activity carrying the user's own scope id,
  which is supervisor-loaded by definition.
2. Period tokens in the activity's own column name, for activities rule 1 did not catch: a month
  name, a quarter token with or without a year, a "<month> Start" or "START <date>" phrase. Every
  token is resolved against the selling cycle window, never against the calendar or fiscal quarter,
  because field selling stops when the cycle closes, not on a month boundary. A token whose calendar
  end falls on or before the current cycle's close, or in the gap between that close and the next
  cycle's open, bands to 1.00; only a token that resolves into a later cycle's window bands down, by
  the same table as rule 1. A run that banded a current-quarter token to 0.50 because the calendar
  quarter ended a few days after the window had applied the financial calendar to a field planner,
  and it moved a store from rank 4 to rank 1 between two real runs. Precedence: the kit's published
  due date always wins, then the selling cycle window, then a period token resolved through the
  window; a calendar or fiscal quarter end is never an authority, only an input the window converts.
3. Anything with no derivable close date is dropped from scoring entirely, not down-weighted or
  ranked low. A store is not more important because it carries five undated flags.

The most damaging way to implement this wrongly fails silently: rule 2 has concrete mechanics (a
token to match), so an implementer treats rule 1 as commentary and inverts the ladder. The absence
of a date token in a header is the normal case, not evidence of no close date, because planners name
activities by what to do rather than when. A market build made this error and scored the two largest
<business unit 4> activities (a national <strength> expansion and a flavor expansion, several
hundred stores each) at zero although both were named in the kit, and nothing in the output looked
wrong. Rule 1 and the priority match share one matching test but answer two questions: matching a
kit initiative decides admission as a scoring activity under condition (c) below, and the
initiative's own due date decides its close weight (1.00, 0.50 or 0.20). A kit-matched activity is
always admitted and not always closing this cycle; the assistant reads membership and date from one
matcher and never assumes the date from the membership.

Local Activities Are Cannot-Miss Work: The Supervisor Scope Boost:

An activity whose header carries the user's own region, section, market or territory id was loaded
by their own leadership and is the single strongest coverage signal in the file; national
initiatives arrive for everyone, a local activity was put there by the person the user reports to,
about their own stores. The test extracts any scope number from the header, normalizes it, and
checks whether it equals or is a left-anchored prefix of the user's own scope code:

1. Scope tokens are pulled from the header. They appear as a bare market id followed by "Activity",
  a letter-prefixed market id, a spelled level ("Section <n>", "Region <n>", "Market <n>"), or a
  leading number on a local-plan activity.
2. Each is normalized to digits and right-padded to six, exactly as scope codes are padded
  everywhere else in the skill.
3. The unpadded digits from step 1, not the padded form, are compared left-anchored against the
  user's own six-digit code. For a <territory role> the own market id, section number and region
  number all match and other markets' ids do not; for a <market role> (market id padded with zeros)
  the same tokens match and fail. Running the prefix test on the padded value breaks every example,
  because a padded section number is no longer a prefix of the territory code; step 2's padding is
  for comparing a tag against a scope column, step 3 uses raw digits. A national user has no scope
  code, so nothing is local to them: an empty code would prefix everything and hand 1.25x plus
  automatic admission to every tagged activity, so on a national run the local boost is 1.00
  throughout, activities are admitted on the kit and supervisor tests alone, and Method says so.

A local activity is still subject to the <sign type 1> state guide gate. Automatic admission means
it needs no kit match, not that it skips compliance: if the <jurisdiction> guideline restricts or
prohibits the activity, it is dropped and named in Method like any other priority, because a
supervisor cannot authorize what the state rules do not permit.

When the activity is the user's own, three things follow: it is always a scoring activity, admitted
on the same footing as a supervisor-named priority whether or not it matches the <monthly POS kit>;
it closes this cycle under Step 6 rule 1; and its contribution is multiplied by a local boost of
1.25. An activity carrying another unit's id gets no boost and no automatic admission; it is another
market's work that rode along in the extract and scores only if it matches a stated priority on its
own merits. A foreign tag is never treated as local and never dropped silently; Method reports both
counts.

A direct local match overrides the price rule; a broader one does not. A tag at the user's own
market or territory level takes the 1.25 boost and overrides a work weight of 0.00, so the activity
scores whatever its work type, because the user's own <market role> or the user loaded it as a
direct instruction about their own stores. A tag at the user's own section or region level takes the
1.25 boost but the price rule still zeroes it, because many <market role> holders load work at
those levels and a section-tagged price validation is closer to a national instruction than a
personal one. Method reports every local activity, its tag level, whether the override fired and
its store count, so a supervisor can confirm their item was received.

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type:

Selling is the highest-impact thing a rep does, and the score says so: persuading a retailer to
lower a shelf price, take a new SKU, sign a contract or place an order changes what the store sells;
setting a shelf is real work below it; confirming a state that already exists is below that; a
survey builds nothing. The old binary (business-building or weightless) collapsed all of that into
two buckets and mis-typed the single highest-value activity in the planner, so work type is a
graduated scale.

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type - The Six Work Types. This List Is Closed:

Every activity resolves to exactly one type; there is no seventh and no "other". Values are: type;
work weight; what the rep actually does at the store.

Sell: 1.25; persuade the retailer to change something commercial (lower a retail price, pass a
  discount through, take a SKU, add distribution, sign a contract, make an election, place an order
  or prebook). The highest-impact work and the only type above baseline.
Merchandise: 1.00; physically change the store (set, place, install, reset, hang signage, build a
  fixture, execute a planogram). Baseline real work with a store outcome.
Educate: 0.75; change what a person knows (clerk and consumer engagement, retailer training, walking
  a retailer through a compliance issue).
Validate: 0.50; confirm a state that already exists (validate, verify, audit, cycle count, inventory
  check). Necessary, but nothing changes because the rep went.
Admin: 0.25; produce a record, not a store outcome (surveys, forms, photo uploads, logging).
Target: 0.00; not an instruction at all but a segmentation marker naming the store's standing
  ("Priority Store", "Focus Store", "Top 10%"). It admits the store to the population and appears on
  its row, but asks for nothing, so it scores nothing.

The work weight multiplies the activity's contribution to the priority term and nothing else. A
Target marker still counts toward the must-close count if it carries a close date, still appears in
the activity inventory and still shows on the store's row; it is considered, not scored.

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type - Assigning the Type: Read the Verbs, Highest Wins:

The whole header is scanned for every verb it contains and the highest type present is taken. When a
header asks for two things the rep has to do the harder one, so the harder one sets the weight. Tier
1 verbs each set a type outright, matched case-insensitively on word boundaries: Sell on "sell",
"selling", "influence", "prebook", "pre-book", "order entry", "offer"; Merchandise on "merchandise",
"merchandising", "install", "reset the", "planogram", "pog", "add the"; Educate on "educate",
"education", "engagement", "engagements", "train", "training", "conversation", "compliance",
"violation"; Validate on "validate", "validation", "verify", "verification", "audit", "evaluation",
"cycle count", "check"; Admin on "survey", "Microsoft Forms", "photo", "log your", "log the",
"upload".

Tier 2: "execute" and "execution" are generic and set no type of their own; they mean "complete the
named work", so the named work decides (the word appears in 16 reference-planner headers and would
otherwise stamp them all as merchandising). Three rules govern it: "execute" promotes a Validate or
Admin header one step to Merchandise, because "validate and execute" asks for more than validating;
"execute" with no Tier 1 verb at all resolves to Merchandise, the default field action; and
"execute" never promotes a Sell, Merchandise or Educate header, which are already at or above that
level (an education call "Execute and Complete Survey" is Educate, because education outranks the
survey and execute does not lift it further).

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type - Verbless Headers: Read the Noun:

Roughly half the activity columns in a real planner carry no verb; they are neither defaulted nor
dropped. The noun is tested in this order:

1. It names a gap or an opportunity ("opportunity", "gap", "potential", "needed", "election",
  "partial or no", "no pass"): paired with a shelf noun ("backbar", "pog", "planogram", "fixture",
  "sign", "display", "space", "set") it is Merchandise; otherwise it is Sell, because a gap is
  something a rep closes by persuading someone. This is the rule that types a distribution
  opportunity, a potential contract opportunity store and a pricing opportunity as selling work.
2. It names only the store's standing ("priority", "focus store", "target store", "velocity", "top
  10%"): Target.
3. Nothing matched: the header is reported in Method by name as unclassified and weighted 1.00. An
  activity the rules did not recognize is never silently discounted; it is surfaced so the
  dictionary can be extended.

A price or a pass-through is selling work, not validation, and getting this backwards is the most
expensive single error in this section. An earlier version zeroed any activity whose subject was a
price, which is right for confirming a price change and wrong for everything else: a
<promotional price program> pass-through activity asks the rep to convince a retailer to lower the
retail price so the consumer sees the promotion, the highest-leverage conversation in the cycle, and
is unambiguously Sell, as is a flavor expansion sell-in. The verb still distinguishes them: in the
reference planner three columns name the same pass-through and are correctly unequal, selling the
price to the store leader is Sell at 1.25, adding the promotion sign is Merchandise at 1.00, and
verifying the pass-through is Validate at 0.50.

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type - The Kit's Type Field Is Evidence, Not an Override:

Where the <monthly POS kit> publishes a type for an initiative, it is recorded in Method and used to
break a genuine tie, but it does not override the verb ladder: the kit types an initiative once
nationally while the planner may carry it as two or three columns asking for different work (the
flavor expansion appears once as a merchandise column and once as a sell-and-complete-order-entry
column), and a single national type cannot be right for both. Where the kit type and the ladder
disagree, the ladder is followed and the disagreement noted in Method. Method states the count of
activities in each of the six types and names every activity typed Target or Admin with its store
count, so a rep can see that a large flag was deliberately scored low.

Not All Activities Are Business Building: Weight by Work Type - The Frozen Reference Assignment: All 72 Columns of the Reference Planner:

The frozen reference assignment is the verb ladder applied to all 72 activity columns of the
reference planner and frozen as ground truth: 28 Sell, 24 Merchandise, 10 Validate, 5 Educate, 3
Target, 2 Admin. An implementation that classifies the reference planner and does not reproduce the
table exactly has a defect in its classifier, not a difference of opinion; the classifier is run
against the table before anything is scored and the match rate is reported in Method, with anything
under 72 of 72 a failing gate. The table is not reproduced here because every row names the source
company's chains, brands and programs; a new deployment freezes its own table the first time its
planner is classified and reviewed. The pattern it encodes: pass-through, pricing, distribution,
contract, allowance, election, order-entry and prebook opportunity headers are Sell; chain
merchandising executions, sign and fixture installs, planogram sets and "validate and execute"
expansions are Merchandise; engagement logging, education calls and compliance conversations are
Educate; audits, cycle counts, inventory evaluations and value validations are Validate; survey and
photo-update headers are Admin; volume priority and focus-store markers are Target. One
inconsistency to resolve when rebuilding: the frozen table lists two rep-action chain
<price family A> "execute" headers as Sell while the Tier 2 rule types the same header Merchandise,
and the frozen table is declared ground truth. On a planner the table does not cover, the ladder is
the authority and the table is the worked example; every header the rules did not recognize is
reported in Method by name and store count so the trigger-word lists can be extended.

Close-date weighting, applied to each activity's contribution. Values are: when it closes; weight;
how it is reached.

This cycle, or late within this cycle: 1.00; rule 1 (named by the kit or the supervisor), or rule 2
  with a token resolving into the current window. "Late" means past the item's own date while the
  current window is still open, not carried over from a window that already closed. When the kit
  used is a fallback from an earlier month, its initiatives are capped at 0.75 rather than 1.00 and
  Method says so.
Expired prior cycle (the token resolves to a selling cycle whose window closed strictly before the
  current window opened): 0.20, and zero toward the must-close count; a prior-quarter token while
  planning the following quarter's cycle is the worked example. It stays visible in the priority
  term at the floor weight and is labeled prior-cycle carryover on the store's row, so a rep who
  still owes the work can see it, but it never outranks current-cycle work and never inflates the
  workload count. Only current-cycle work is ever 1.00. Method reports the expired activities by
  name.
Next cycle: 0.50; rule 2 only.
Later than next cycle: 0.20; rule 2 only.
No derivable close date: 0.00, excluded; rule 3.

A scoring activity carries 1.00, 0.75, 0.50 or 0.20, each with one defined trigger. A published,
readable due date bands to 1.00, 0.50 or 0.20 by the cycle-window test. The 0.75 value is reserved
for an initiative that appears in this cycle's kit but whose date cannot be read (the
unreadable-date table in the kit-parsing section is authoritative and also caps a fallback kit from
an earlier month at 0.75); 0.75 is never produced from a date that was read, and an unreadable date
is never defaulted to 1.00. A 0.75 activity contributes zero to the must-close count, which requires
exactly 1.00, and Method names every activity it fired on. Matching the kit admits the activity and
its own date weights it; supervisor-named and local activities carry 1.00; rules 2 and 3 date the
activities that were never named, which still matter for the must-close count and the Method
inventory even though they never enter the priority term.

Then, for each eligible store, the assistant combines: the volume percentile within scope, applied
multiplicatively as the tie-shaper rather than the sole rank driver; priority alignment, each dated
activity matching a supervisor or headquarters priority, scaled by its business unit weight and
close-date weight and raised where both sources name it, where it requires rep action and where it
reaches few stores; the must-close count, the workload signal, rather than the raw flag count; the
performance gap (the <performance identifier column>, assortment satisfaction below the scope
median, contract or distribution gaps, pricing pass-through opportunities and the headquarters
question marker); the user's steer (Step 7); and the mandatory override, directed stores carrying
2.00 applied once to the finished score on their own tab. A store is never ranked on how many
activities it carries: a high-volume store with two closing-now activities outranks a mid-volume
store with nine undated ones, where "undated" means the Step 6 ladder found no close date after rule
1 was applied, not merely that the header carries no month.

Volume weighting is universal; it applies to every opportunity the skill surfaces on every tab, and
any later signal inherits it. Values are: output; how volume enters.

Visit lists (10 and 25 for a <territory role>, 20 and 50 for the manager tier): volume percentile
  raised to the power 1.5, multiplicative, the tie-shaper rather than the dominant term.
Directed stores: own section, ranked among themselves by the same score.
Assortment Targets: top-third volume floor, then sorted by estimated upside, itself a units-per-week
  figure.
Coverage Gaps: total multiplied by (0.5 plus 0.5 times the volume percentile).
Comparison mode: change metric times volume weighting, so small-store swings cannot lead.

Contract and program gaps carry a volume-economics gate wherever they appear, because these programs
cost the retailer real money and a small store declining is a rational decision rather than an
execution gap. The contract gap is scored flat (0.5 as one gap term in the visit-list formula, 2.0
on Coverage Gaps) and volume enters exactly once through each tab's own multiplier; scaling the
contract term by percentile separately would apply volume twice and bury contract stores. The one
extra rule is a membership gate on the Coverage Gaps tab only: a row whose only flag is Contract
must clear the scope median volume to appear at all. Coverage Gaps admits stores by flag, so the
test is meaningful there; the visit lists admit by rank, so the test is undefined on them and would
silently remove qualified stores. One weight everywhere, one membership gate on one tab.

The formula, in plain words and implemented exactly:

- Volume percentile: the in-scope population is ranked ascending by the ranking volume measure, tied
  values share the average of their ranks, and each store's average rank is divided by N, which is
  every store in the ranked population after the closed-store and lifestyle exclusions, directed
  stores included (the eligible count, the merit remainder after directed stores are routed away, is
  a different number). Usable volume is as defined in Step 5: blank, null, non-numeric, zero and
  negative values are included as 0, counted in N and ranked last. The range is above 0 and up to 1,
  with k stores tied at the minimum each receiving k plus 1 over twice N; tied volumes receive
  identical percentiles, which is what makes a rerun reproduce the same list. The percentile is
  never computed over a subset or a sample.
- Priority: the sum over scoring activities of business unit weight times close weight times source
  boost times work weight times local boost. The work weight is the closed six-value scale assigned
  by the verb ladder, with no default of 1.00 except for an unrecognized header named in Method; a
  Target-weighted activity still counts toward the must-close count when its close weight is 1.00.
  The local boost is 1.25 when the header carries a scope number that equals or
  left-anchored-prefixes the user's own scope code, else 1.00; a local activity is admitted
  automatically without a kit match, and at the user's own market or territory level the tag also
  overrides the work weight (the direct local override), while a section or region tag gives the
  boost but not the override. A scoring activity is one that is (a) flagged at this store, (b) has a
  derivable close date by the Step 6 ladder, and (c) matches a supervisor priority or a headquarters
  kit priority by the matching rule below, or carries the user's own scope number; satisfying (c)
  satisfies (b) automatically through rule 1. An activity matching no stated priority contributes
  nothing, however many there are, which is what stops the term becoming a weighted activity count.
  The source boost is 1.5 when named by a supervisor or by headquarters and 2.0 when named by both,
  scaled by the naming level's distance weight when a supervisor named it: immediate supervisor
  1.00, two levels up 0.70, three up 0.50, four or more 0.30. Headquarters naming is national and
  never distance-scaled; when both name it, the 2.0 is scaled only if the nearest naming supervisor
  is above the immediate level, and where several levels named the same activity the nearest one's
  weight is used. A further 1.25 applies if the activity requires rep action, which means exactly
  the rep-action-needed originator prefix on the header (matched case-insensitively and tolerant of
  the separator) and never an inference from verbs; when no column carries the prefix, no activity
  takes the multiplier and Method records that. Another 1.25 applies if the activity is flagged at
  fewer than 10 percent of N. A local activity matching no kit or supervisor phrase takes source
  boost 1.5 and close weight 1.00, because a supervisor loading work into the planner is naming it,
  and the rep-action and narrowness multipliers apply to it normally. There is no base case: an
  activity that matches neither source and is not local never reaches this term.
- Gap: the sum of the performance-gap terms present. Four flat terms score 0.5 each, and each has a
  named source so two implementers score the same stores: (a) assortment below par, any resolved
  <assortment satisfaction column> whose value is below that column's own in-scope median, each
  column against its own median; (b) contract or distribution gap, 0.5 once if either fires: any
  resolved contract column blank after null normalization, or a competitor distribution column
  reading yes while our own business unit's distribution column reads no for the same category (in
  the reference planner <competitor brand 4A>, <competitor brand 4B> or <competitor brand 4C>
  carried against <our brand 4A> premium line not carried, a real gap the assortment percentage
  cannot surface because a store with zero distribution has no assortment figure); (c) pricing
  pass-through, a resolved pass-through column reading anything other than full pass-through, plus
  any store carrying an activity whose header names a pass-through opportunity; (d) the headquarters
  question (Q) store, which fires only when both source columns hold: the <volume index> decile or
  quartile column in the top two quartiles and the <performance identifier column> reading the
  biggest-upside value. A fifth contributor, the <our brand 4A> premium line performance bucket,
  scores the identifier alone on its graduated scale (biggest upside 0.50, winners 0.25, outperform
  0.15, low priority 0.00; see the bucket framework in Appendix A). The Q term is computed from both
  columns and never inferred from the bucket, because the identifier's own volume axis is quartiled
  within market and disagrees with the decile column on more than a third of biggest-upside stores
  in the reference planner. A store that is biggest-upside and top-two-quartile scores 0.5 plus
  0.50, the one intended double count; a biggest-upside store in the bottom half scores the bucket
  0.50 and the Q term 0.00; no other gap term shares a column. A term whose source column does not
  resolve, or resolves empty in scope, is not scored, is named in Method as not evaluated and never
  becomes a default. The maximum is exactly 2.50 (four flat terms at 0.50 plus one bucket whose
  largest value is 0.50); there is no term (e), the cap is never set below 2.50, and a sixth flat
  term may not be added without raising the cap, because a cap under the reachable maximum silently
  clips the highest-signal stores.
- Must-close: the count of activities closing this cycle, capped at 5 first and then multiplied by
  1.2, for a maximum contribution of 6.0 (never cap the product). An activity counts when it is
  flagged at this store and the Step 6 ladder resolved its close weight to exactly 1.00; nothing
  else counts. Four deliberate consequences: a close weight of 0.50 or 0.20 contributes zero (the
  reference kit's largest activity, due in the following cycle, enters priority at 0.50 and
  must-close at 0); an activity with no derivable close date contributes zero; a low-weight activity
  counts in full when its close weight is 1.00, whatever its work type, because a Validate, an Admin
  survey and even a dated Target marker still have to be performed; and admission as a scoring
  activity is irrelevant, because an activity matching no priority still counts if it closes this
  cycle. Priority measures alignment and must-close measures workload; they are different
  populations on purpose, neither a subset of the other, and Method reports both counts per store.
- Steer: 0.5 when the request named an initiative or activity and this store carries it, else 0. A
  business unit steer does not enter here.
- Score: the bracket (1.0 plus priority plus gap plus 1.2 times the capped must-close count plus
  steer) multiplied by the volume percentile raised to the power 1.5.
- Zero-volume bucket, applied after the score and before the rank is assigned: stores with a
  positive ranking volume form bucket 1, ordered by score descending; stores whose volume is zero,
  blank, null or negative form bucket 2, ordered by score descending among themselves; bucket 1
  ranks entirely above bucket 2. The bucket is a sort key, never a score adjustment: the score is
  not zeroed, no penalty term is added, and the store's real score is published in the Score column.
- Directed stores: the 2.00 is applied once, as a multiplier on the finished score, never also as a
  business unit weight inside priority; directed stores rank among themselves on their own tab and
  never enter the merit tiers.

Defining the match, because the whole priority term rests on it. An activity header is a sentence, a
supervisor priority is a phrase from an email, and a headquarters priority is a kit initiative
title. They are matched this way, and every match and near-miss is recorded in Method:

1. Both sides are normalized: case-folded, punctuation stripped, whitespace collapsed.
2. Stopwords and short tokens are dropped. A distinctive token is four or more characters and not a
  stopword (the, and, for, with, this, from, your, execute, validate, store, stores).
3. An activity matches a priority when at least two distinctive tokens are shared, or when one
  shared distinctive token is a specific initiative name, which identifies one piece of work: the
  <strength> variant name, "flavor expansion", "backbar", the <loyalty program> name, the
  <promotional price program> names, "pass through", the <price family A> name, and any initiative
  name the kit introduces this cycle. A business unit or brand-family name (<business unit 1>
  through <business unit 6>, <our brand 1A>, <our brand 2A>, <our brand 2B>, <our brand 2D>,
  <our brand 3A>, <our brand 4A> in any spelling) is not a specific initiative name and never
  matches on its own; it requires a second distinctive token. Without this rule one supervisor email
  about <business unit 4> would link to every <business unit 4> activity in the planner and lift
  each from source boost 1.5 to 2.0; two real runs split on exactly this, one scoring every top-ten
  store 0.2 to 0.6 higher and reordering the list. A supervisor's business unit mention is still a
  stated priority: it enters Method as a family-level priority (listed separately from
  initiative-level ones, with counts of each), steers the business unit weighting, and can admit an
  activity under condition (c) when combined with a second token; it does not manufacture a
  supervisor match against every activity in the family.
4. "Named by headquarters" means it matched an initiative title in the resolved <monthly POS kit>;
  "named by supervisor" means it matched a phrase quoted from a supervisor message under source A or
  B. Both gives source boost 2.0, either alone 1.5.
5. A one-token match on a generic word is not a match; "execute" in both an activity header and a
  kit line means nothing, which is why step 2 removes it.
6. Tokens are stemmed before comparison on a four-character normalized stem, the same rule Appendix
  A uses for column matching, so plural and tense variants are the same token; a match that depends
  on plural agreement is a coin flip, not a rule.
7. The test runs for every activity against every priority phrase and records the outcome. Method
  carries one line per matched activity naming the priority it matched, the shared tokens, whether
  each is a family name or an initiative name, and the resulting source boost; an implementer who
  cannot show the tokens did not run the test.

Two worked examples from the runs that split: a supervisor subject asking for engagements at
underperforming <business unit 4> stores matches an activity for logging <business unit 4> clerk and
consumer engagements (shared stems: the family name plus the engagement stem, two distinctive
tokens, one not a family name), and since the activity is also kit-named its source boost is 2.00.
The same subject does not match the <business unit 4> flavor expansion merchandise activity, because
the only shared stem is the family name; it stays headquarters-named at 1.50. Method reports the
counts: activities examined, matched headquarters, matched a supervisor phrase, matched both, and
flagged at stores but matching nothing. A run where nothing matched is a real result and must be
visible, not a silent zero across the priority term.

What the multiplicative shape does: the bracket ranges from 1.0 to roughly 12, and the volume factor
ranges from 0 to 1 and can only scale it down. Among the stores competing for a top place all sit
high in the scope by volume, so the volume factor varies only slightly between them while the
bracket can vary several-fold; volume is therefore the tie-shaper, the ordering inside the visit
lists is set mainly by priority alignment and must-close workload, and the Read Me and Method
describe it that way rather than calling volume the dominant term. No store scores exactly zero,
since the average-rank percentile never reaches 0, so a new store with real directed work sits above
a dormant one; the zero-volume bucket, not the percentile, is what guarantees that a zero-volume
store ranks last.

A business unit steer never enters the formula as a term: a tilt acts by changing the business unit
weight inside priority, a filter acts by changing who is in the population and which column supplies
the volume percentile, and the steer term exists only for a named initiative or activity, a
membership fact about the store. Adding a business unit steer as a separate additive term would
apply the same preference twice.

A Q store scores the headquarters question term and separately the bucket term, 1.0 in total,
because by definition it is a biggest-upside store in the top two <volume index> quartiles (the
Appendix A definition, the only one) and a big store that is also underperforming is a stronger
signal than either fact alone. A low-priority bucket store is never a Q store, whatever its band,
because that bucket is bottom-half by construction. No other gap term is double-counted.

The tie-break is mandatory and deterministic: score descending, then volume descending, then
<store id> ascending. "Volume" means whichever column supplied the volume percentile for this run
(the scope's normal measure in tilt, initiative and none modes, the business unit's own column in
filter mode), so the tie-break never silently switches measures; without it a rerun on the same file
can return a different list, and ties are real, since several reference-planner stores share the
exact volume value at the tab-size cut line.

The must-close count is a workload term, not an activity count: it counts every activity closing
this cycle, whether or not it matched a priority and whether or not it is weightless, while priority
counts only the matched ones scaled by work weight. An activity dated by rule 2 with no priority
match therefore contributes to must-close and nothing to priority, which is correct, not a leak, and
both counts are stated in Method. Every figure is computed with a code tool, never mentally.

Step 7: The User's Steer, Two Modes That Are Not the Same Request:

A category steer arrives in one of two shapes, and reading it wrong produces the wrong list. The
assistant decides which the user asked for, applies it, and names the mode in both the Read Me
and Method tabs. The brand or category word is first resolved to its <business unit> through the
Step 5 table.

The four modes are listed below with their wording, population and ranking metric.

- Lean wording ("with a focus on <business unit 4>", "leaning <business unit 4>",
  "<business unit 4> is my priority"): TILT; all eligible stores; overall volume with that unit's
  contributions up-weighted.
- Possessive or "for" wording ("my top <business unit 4> stores", "top stores for
  <business unit 4>", "best <business unit 4> opportunities"): FILTER; only stores carrying that unit's volume
  or activity; that unit's own volume column.
- An initiative or activity named rather than a category ("the flavor expansion", "the
  <strength> rollout"): INITIATIVE, a tilt toward one activity; all eligible stores; overall
  volume with the steer term added for stores carrying it.
- No unit, brand or initiative named: NONE; all eligible stores; overall volume.

TILT is a lean, not a restriction. Every eligible store stays in scope and one exact transform
is applied to the Step 5 hierarchy: the named unit's weight becomes 1.25 and every other unit's
weight is multiplied by 0.60; directed stores keep 2.00. The resulting weights (legend: row is
the unit tilted toward; values are the weights for <business unit 1>, <business unit 4>,
<business unit 2>, <business unit 5>, <business unit 3>, <business unit 6>, in hierarchy order):

- Tilt toward <business unit 1>: 1.25; 0.57; 0.42; 0.27; 0.24; 0.18
- Tilt toward <business unit 4>: 0.60; 1.25; 0.42; 0.27; 0.24; 0.18
- Tilt toward <business unit 2>: 0.60; 0.57; 1.25; 0.27; 0.24; 0.18
- Tilt toward <business unit 5>: 0.60; 0.57; 0.42; 1.25; 0.24; 0.18
- Tilt toward <business unit 3>: 0.60; 0.57; 0.42; 0.27; 1.25; 0.18
- Tilt toward <business unit 6>: 0.60; 0.57; 0.42; 0.27; 0.24; 1.25

A tilt toward a lower-ranked unit deliberately inverts the standing hierarchy for that run; the
Read Me and Method say so and print the row used. The tilt moves the priority term only (no
compensating term is added), and the named unit goes to 1.25 rather than 1.00 because
<business unit 1> already sits at 1.00. A large store with strong opportunity in another category must
still surface; a result in which every store carries the named unit means the wrong mode ran,
which is verified before delivery.

FILTER restricts the population, so it is applied in the scope stage alongside qualifiers,
before the closed-store exclusion, the lifestyle exclusion and the percentile, so that the
scope median and percentile are computed over the set the report is about; the other three
modes do not restrict the population. The filter keeps stores carrying the unit's volume or
activities and ranks on the unit's own volume column; supervisor, headquarters and close-date
weighting apply inside the filtered set; the surviving count is stated. When the unit has no
volume column, the filter falls back in order to (1) its SKU, distribution or share-of-trade
column, then (2) membership by activity flags or contract columns alone, ranked on the scope
volume measure, with the basis named in the Read Me and Method. A category request is never
refused for a missing column.

Fewer survivors than the list size are delivered with the count. Zero survivors means the count
is reported, the mode switches to TILT on the same unit, the workbook is labeled a tilt, the
switch is stated in the Read Me and the opener, and the volume basis reverts to the scope's
normal ranking measure (a basis bound to an empty column collapses every percentile toward
zero), with the reverted column named beside the switch. An empty workbook is never an
acceptable answer.

"Opportunity" is filler: the assistant strips "opportunity", "opp", "best" and "top", then reads
the remaining grammar (possessive or "for" is FILTER; an explicit lean is TILT). Genuinely
ambiguous wording gets TILT, noted in one line, since an over-broad list is recoverable and a
wrong filter hides opportunity; the assistant does not stop to ask. Every mode still honors
supervisor and headquarters priorities and never releases a directed store.

Time comparison runs only when the user asks; otherwise the single <planning workbook> is ranked
with no mention of trend. A request for decline, drop, decrease, growth, fall, slip, loss or any
period-over-period comparison requires two <planning workbooks>; with one present the assistant
stops and asks for a second from another month, never substituting gap indicators for measured
change. With two files it:

1. Detects each file's header row and maps columns by concept independently.
2. Filters both to the same scope using each file's own territory column.
3. Confirms the metric matches on concept and time basis (last 13 weeks to last 13 weeks, year
  to date to year to date); a mismatched basis is reported and only aligned metrics compared.
4. Joins on <store id>, listing stores present in one file only rather than dropping them.
5. Computes per-store change and percent change on each shared metric.

The comparison score: percent change is current minus baseline over the absolute baseline when
the baseline is non-zero; a zero baseline with a non-zero current is a new-distribution case,
listed with percent change blank. Direction is plus one for growth, gain or increase, minus one
for decline, drop, decrease, fall, slip or loss. The score is direction times percent change
times the volume percentile raised to the power 1.5, multiplied by the normal score's bracket
(one plus the priority term, plus the gap term, plus 1.2 times the must-close count capped at
five, plus the steer term), every term computed as in the normal score over the current
cycle's file and the percentile the current cycle's. Ranking is by comparison score descending,
current volume descending, <store id> ascending; stores in only one file are listed separately,
never scored. The output shape is unchanged (Top 10 plus Next 25 for Line A, Top 20 plus Next 50
for Line B, plus the reference tab of up to 500 rows). A comparison combines with scope and
steer ("which <business unit 4> stores declined the most in <region id>"): scope, then steer,
then rank the filtered population on the change metric, with every gate, exclusion and cap
holding.

Step 8: Deliver - One Format for Every Role, Two Sizes:

| Report line | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| Line A, <territory role> (10 + 25 + up to 500) | Excel workbook "<scope label> <month> <year> Store Visit Plan.xlsx" |
| Line B, manager tier (20 + 50 + up to 500) | Excel workbook, same filename pattern |

Only the three counts differ: same nine tabs, identity block, thirteen formatting elements,
filename pattern and verification (contract section C1). The scope label is a level name plus
its identifying number (market and <market id>, section and <section id>, region and
<region id>, territory and <territory id>, or "National"), never a person's name; the number stops one
market's run overwriting another's in OneDrive, and any narrowing qualifier is appended (a unit
name, a chain such as <chain A>). The tabs, in this order, for both lines without exception:

1. Read Me, designed rather than typed: a banner plus titled sections. Rows 1 to 3 are a solid
  title band, scope and cycle in white 16pt bold on navy 1F3864, then name, role, the scope
  actually used, prepared date and cycle window on 2E5496 (the scope named at the level the
  run used). Section headers sit on blue bars (4472C4, white bold 12pt) in Title Case: How
  This List Was Built; What Is in This Workbook; Reading the Columns. Label cells are shaded
  EAF0F8, bold navy, thin 9BA7B5 borders; font, palette and borders match the store tabs.
  Content, in order: (1) scope, cycle window and who it was built for, in the band; (2) the
  steer mode and volume basis directly under it, in the category the user named (TILT: all
  stores, the category weighted heavier, stores strong elsewhere still appear; FILTER:
  category stores only, ranked by its volume, "N of your M stores carry" it; INITIATIVE: all
  stores, stores carrying the initiative weighted heavier; NONE: all stores on total volume),
  then one line per qualifier naming the phrase, the column it resolved to and the store count
  (a qualifier dropped for resolving to no column is reported here, not in Method), one line
  naming the volume column and its time basis (a synthesized measure is called a composite
  ranking measure), and one sentence if a filter switched to a tilt; (3) the tab map, every
  tab in order under its exact name with its actual row count, "showing n of N" where capped,
  and one line for any empty or short tab and why; (4) the column glossary, including the Q
  marker (the headquarters-question store flag) and one line on anything the planner did not
  carry; (5) one line that the tabs are filterable Excel Tables with rank, <store id>, store
  and address locked while scrolling; (6) a two-sentence tuning note in plain language:
  thresholds come from the user's own scope, local commercial rules are inputs the skill does
  not assume, and a mismatched result can be calibrated by contacting <author> at
  <author email>.
2. Priorities: supervisor and headquarters <monthly POS kit> priorities, quoted, attributed,
  dated, with the initiative window.
3. Directed: must-hit stores and what directed them. Columns: the contract C3 identity block
  (Rank, <store id>, the span-of-control block, Store, Address, City, State, Zip, Chain,
  Volume), then Directed by, Date, Instruction (the quoted words), What to do. Rank is the
  position among the directed set, 1 to N.
4. Top 20 (manager tier) or Top 10 (<territory role>): merit-ranked, ranks 1-20 or 1-10.
5. Next 50 (ranks 21-70) or Next 25 (ranks 11-35): merit-ranked.
6. All Other Stores: remaining eligible stores, merit-ranked, capped at 500 with "showing n of
  N"; a lookup, not a visit list.
7. Assortment Targets: shelf opportunity, separate from coverage.
8. Coverage Gaps: an exception list of quiet problems, outside the ranking.
9. Method: the full audit trail.

Tabs are named for the size of the list, not the rows that survived: "Top 20" stays "Top 20"
with eight stores and "showing 8 of 20" on the tab. Only an explicit user-requested size
renames a tab. An empty tab is styled exactly like a populated one (table, computed freeze
pane, TableStyleMedium2, Arial 10, explicit widths, full 9BA7B5 grid on the header row and the
single explanatory row, bordered across the block's full width). When a callout names no
stores, the planner carries no assortment measure or no store trips a coverage flag, the tab
carries the header plus one plain-language reason row ("No directed stores this cycle"; "This
planner carries no assortment measure, so no shelf gaps could be calculated"; "No store in
scope tripped a coverage flag") with the Excel Table over both; a table over an empty block
fails the build. The reason is repeated in the Read Me tab map and Method. No tab is ever
dropped or renumbered.

The All Other Stores Tab:

The visit lists are deliberately short; this tab covers the rest, which on a mid-sized
territory often carries more weekly volume than Top 10 and Next 25 combined. It is a lookup,
not a plan, and says so: reps build routes in <CRM> by filtering on activity and chain type
(store numbers cannot be imported there), so the user is told to sort or filter this tab the
same way and work its highest-ranked stores. It holds the eligible stores below the last visit
tier, merit-ranked with continuous rank numbering, in the standard columns plus a short line on
why each store ranks where it does.

This tab, Directed, Assortment Targets and Coverage Gaps are each capped at 500 rows on both
lines, stating "showing n of N" on the tab and in Method. Below 500 every row is included and
the tab says so; above, the highest-ranked 500 are taken and the overflow count stated. The cap
counts rows on the tab, not ranked positions: a <market role>'s tab runs ranks 71 through 570,
and slicing the ranked list at 500 before removing the first seventy silently drops seventy
stores. The assistant takes the 500 ranked stores after the last visit tier, confirms the tab
holds the smaller of 500 and the eligible count minus the last visit tier position, and writes
the note in one footer row in column A, one row below the last data row with no blank between,
italic grey, never bold, never above the header, never in the frozen pane, never merged.

The note is written if and only if n is strictly less than N; a full tab writes nothing.

| Case | Which tabs | n | N |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shortfall (the pool ran out) | the two visit tiers | rows written | the tab's named size (10, 25, 20 or 50) |
| Cap (more qualified than fit) | All Other Stores, Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps | rows written, at most 500 | the count that qualified for that tab |

A Top 20 holding eight writes "showing 8 of 20"; All Other Stores capped at 500 of 1,240 writes
"showing 500 of 1,240". N is never the eligible pool or the scope population. The build never
asserts this tab holds exactly 500: a 200-store territory yields roughly 120 to 160 eligible
stores, so an 85 to 125 row tab is complete, and a failed assertion burns the retry budget the
formatting pass needs. The visit lists are never extended to absorb overflow.

The Directed cap is announced loudly, since a section, region or national directive can name
thousands of stores, and the directed set is cut by volume rank, never by score: score carries
the volume percentile raised to the power 1.5 and the zero-volume bucket, so a score cut would
silently delete the no-volume directed stores the no-floor guarantee protects. The set is
ranked by volume descending then <store id> ascending, the top 500 published, and the rows then
ordered by normal score so the tab reads best-first. The full directed count is stated in the
opener, on the tab, in the Read Me and in Method ("512 stores were directed this cycle; the 500
highest-volume are listed here"), saying volume, never "highest-scoring".

The Assortment Targets Tab:

This tab never touches the ranking (shelf opportunity versus where to spend time), and the tab
and Method say so. It is built for <business unit 4> only, the one unit whose planner columns
carry both a SKU count (the "<business unit 4> SKUs" stem, any period suffix) and an assortment
percentage; <business unit 2> and <business unit 1> carry only the percentage, the misleading
half. The pair is the admission test, not the unit name: a <business unit 2> filter or
<business unit 3> tilt still yields a <business unit 4> assortment tab, explained in one line on
the tab and in the Read Me tab map, and computed over the same filtered population as the rest
of the workbook. A future planner carrying both measures for another unit gets a tab for it
too, saying which. If no unit carries both, the tab has the header and one row saying the
planner carries no SKU count; the percentage alone is never substituted and the tab never
dropped. Assortment satisfaction and scanned SKU counts are scan-derived, not physical audits,
so ranking on the percentage alone sends reps to sell in product already on the shelf.

The gates are applied in this order.

1. Volume floor: the top third of scope by the ranking volume metric, exactly the floor of N
  divided by 3 (floor, not round or ceiling), taking stores by ranking volume descending with
  boundary ties broken by <store id> ascending. Deliberately stricter than the visit lists,
  which carry no floor, because a shelf recommendation only pays at high volume; the
  departure is stated in Method.
2. SKU count greater than zero; zero is a new-distribution conversation, not an assortment gap.
3. Below the peer norm: the median SKU count among stores that cleared this tab's floor and
  carry a SKU count greater than zero; population and norm stated in Method.
4. Gap of at least 2 SKUs.

Each survivor is classified by units per SKU (category units per week over SKU count) against
the scope median of units per SKU, computed over in-scope stores with a SKU count greater than
zero (not all in-scope stores, not only those above the floor), with population and median
stated in Method beside the peer norm. The two periods must match before dividing: in the
reference planner the numerator covers the last 13 weeks and the SKU count the last 26, so the
count runs high and units per SKU low, pushing stores toward "Drive velocity" and understating
upside. The assistant prefers a SKU count whose period token matches the volume column's;
failing that it uses what is there, states both tokens in Method and the What to do wording,
and treats the ratio as directional. It never silently divides across periods or invents a
scaling factor.

Conditions are evaluated in order, stopping at the first match.

| Condition (in order) | Play | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| SKUs below 50% of peer norm | Add SKUs | Less than half the range present; sell in regardless of velocity |
| Units per SKU at or above 75% of scope median | Add SKUs | Moves what it stocks normally; shelf is the ceiling |
| Units per SKU below 75% of scope median | Drive velocity | Range present but not moving; merchandising and <retailer engagement program> work, not a sell-in |

Upside is shown with its arithmetic: Add SKUs is SKU gap times the store's units per SKU; Drive
velocity is SKU count times (scope median units per SKU minus current units per SKU).

Columns: Rank, <store id>, the span-of-control block (contract section C3), Store, Address,
City, State, Zip, Chain, Volume, category units per week, SKUs, Peer norm, SKU gap, Assort %,
Units/SKU, Est. upside u/wk, Play, What to do. Sort is estimated upside descending, volume
descending, <store id> ascending, never by Play (the final key keeps the 500 cut reproducible;
Play is an attribute whose sort order inverts value). What to do notes when a store also sits on
a visit list. Method says that low assortment with low units per SKU is a velocity story, not a
distribution gap, and names the run's clearest example.

The Coverage Gaps Tab:

Also outside the ranking: where something is quietly wrong that no other report surfaces. A
coverage gap store is any store in the ranked population (the visit lists' own: open,
non-lifestyle, no volume floor; a lifestyle store only if readmitted by a Step 5 escape) that
trips at least one scored flag on a column that resolved and carries data. It is an exception
list, never the roster:

- Coverage (Flag 1, weight 2.0): days since the <last call date column> exceed the larger of 60
  days and twice the scope median; <chain B> and <chain C> exempt and excluded from the median.
- Distribution absence (Flag 1b, weight 2.0): our unit's distribution column reads N while a
  competitor's reads Y; <chain B> and <chain C> exempt.
- Competitive assortment (Flag 2, weight 3.0): competitor assortment minus ours exceeds 25
  points on the column's own scale.
- Contract (Flag 3, weight 2.0, plus 0.5 when the <digital trade program> is missing): a
  resolved contract column is blank after null-normalization, independents only, and a store
  whose only flag is Contract must also clear the scope median volume.

<price support program> alignment (Flag 4) is shown, never scored, and never confers membership.
The two ways this tab goes wrong are a flat 60-day line (floods a 22-day territory, hides gaps
in a 38-day one) and a chain contract test (a chain headquarters matter no rep can close). Sort
is by weighted score, never flag count.

The Coverage Gaps Tab - The 180-Day Coverage Alert:

Separately from the weights, the "Days since" cell is shaded by how long the store has gone
uncovered; the flag decides membership, the shading decides visibility.

| Days since last call | Cell shading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 150 to 179 | Amber FFF2CC, black text | Approaching; work into the next cycle |
| 180 or more | Red F8CBAD, black text | Overdue; six months without a call |
| under 150 | no shading | Normal |

180 days is flat and does not flex with the call cycle: the flag scales with the territory, the
alert asks whether a store has been left too long by any standard, and a scaled alert would fire
at eight weeks in a fast territory. Lifestyle stores are exempt and never shaded; they are
removed upstream, but the exemption is stated so no future change re-admits them. Both counts
are reported in the Read Me on every run, including zeros, as "Coverage alert: N stores
approaching 180 days, M stores overdue", counted over the whole ranked population rather than
the displayed rows (the threshold can exceed 180 days and the cap can push a store off), and the
Read Me says the counts may exceed the shaded rows. Days are whole days from the
<last call date column> to the run date counting neither endpoint, so exactly 180 is overdue. A blank or
unparseable date is in neither band and is reported as no recorded call; a future date is a
data error, reported, not shaded, never treated as zero. The tab keeps its score order and is
never re-sorted by staleness.

The Coverage Gaps Tab - Flag Evaluation and Weighting:

Only flags whose columns resolved and carry data run; an absent or all-empty column means that
flag is skipped and named in Method, never read as a gap (an unpopulated contract column would
otherwise flag every independent), and a store is admitted on the flags that did evaluate. If no
flag can be evaluated at all, the tab is built with one row saying so. Flags are weighted, never
counted: Competitive 3.0, Coverage
2.0, Distribution absence 2.0, Contract 2.0 plus 0.5 when the <digital trade program> is among
the missing. The store's total is multiplied by 0.5 plus 0.5 times its volume percentile within
scope, then banded: High at 2.0 and above, Medium at 1.0 and above, Low below (banding at the raw
weights 3.0 and 1.5 was wrong, since the multiplier never exceeds 1.0 and High then required two
flags; at 2.0 and 1.0 a lone Competitive flag reaches High from roughly the 33rd percentile and
any 2.0 flag clears Medium at every percentile). Thresholds are stated on the tab and in Method.
Sort is score descending, volume descending, <store id> ascending.

Flag 1, Coverage, weight 2.0 on this tab only and never in the visit-list ranking: days between
the current date and the <last call date column> (matched by stem across header variants such
as last call, last visit and similar), flagged above the larger of 60 days and twice the scope
median because cycles differ sharply by territory. <chain B> and <chain C> carry the
<lifestyle store flag> and are removed by the Step 5 exclusion before any tab is built: they are covered as
directed, sometimes yearly or every other year, and <sales org> has not been asked into
<chain B> since <date>. The guard is retained in case a future change re-admits lifestyle stores: they
are exempt from this flag and the alert, "Days since" renders "not scored" and the store is
marked covered as directed; a run showing one on this tab has failed the lifestyle exclusion.
They are excluded from the scope median too. Every other chain is scored normally; the
exemption is not extended to grocery, mass merchandise or any chain merely showing a long
interval (regional convenience and dairy chains run median cycles in the twenties). Banners
booked under a parent company name are matched on the chain column, and a rep's claim that
another banner is coverage-as-needed is a change to request, not infer. The
<last call date column> lags visits by up to two weeks, so the tab prints a visible note that it is a prompt to
confirm, not fact, and not used in ranking; the median is shown beside each figure.

Flag 1b, Distribution absence, weight 2.0: our unit's distribution column reads N while any
competitor's reads Y, the binary sibling of Flag 2 (on the shelf at all, versus how much of the
range). Both sides and the competitor are shown; a store failing this flag has no assortment
figure and cannot fail Flag 2. <chain B> and <chain C> are exempt because they carry no
<our brand 4A> distribution by national decision.

Flag 2, Competitive assortment: the competitor assortment satisfaction column minus the
<business unit 4> assortment satisfaction column, flagged above 25 percentage points. Scale
warning: in the reference planners these columns are fractions from 0.0 to 1.0, so a literal
"greater than 25" test matches nothing. The scale is confirmed every run from the column's own
maximum: at or below 1.0 the threshold is 0.25 (or both sides are multiplied by 100); above 1.0
it is 25. The same applies to Assort % on the assortment tab. Null handling is stated in
Method: our side zero-filled, the competitor side required non-null. Both figures are shown
side by side, never the delta alone, with the store's category units; a store also on the
assortment tab is noted as two independent reads. <chain B> and <chain C> are exempt here too
(every one shows zero SKUs and units, roughly two-thirds show competitor presence, and a
national listing decision is not an execution miss); with the coverage exemption and the
independents-only contract and <price support program> flags, those banners cannot trip any
flag and do not appear, and a row for one means the Step 5 exclusion failed and the workbook is
not published. The principle for any future flag: a gap the field cannot close is not worth
printing; the rep must both call the store and control the thing measured.

Flag 3, Contract gaps, independents only, volume the real gate: a missing value in any resolved
contract column (the <business unit 1> contract, the <business unit 4>, <business unit 2> and
<business unit 5> monthly contracts, the <digital trade program> contract, the
<business unit 3> contract), listed by name. A store with the <parent chain account name column> populated is
out of scope, since no field role carries chain headquarters responsibility. Volume is applied
once by the tab's multiplier and never again inside this flag. A store whose only flag is
Contract must clear the scope median volume: the programs cost the retailer money (the
<digital trade program> runs about <program monthly cost> a month against a few dollars a quarter back
at low volume), so a small store declining is rational, and in the reference planner adoption
rose steeply by volume quintile; the assistant recomputes this from the user's file every run
and reports the quintile figures in Method, never quoting prior ones. Volume, not geography,
predicts adoption, so the gate is a volume test, not a state test. A contract is never
suppressed because local peers also lack it. Jurisdictions without the program's loyalty
component show lower adoption, which the volume weighting already handles; no hard-coded state
table is built. The peer benchmark is one line in Method: each contract's penetration among
peers of the same segment (the <digital trade program> and each unit's contract, as
percentages among independents), computed from the file, one level wider than the user's slice
(territory against market, market against section, region against the file; the whole file
when nothing wider exists, and Method says so), treated as directional.

Flag 4, <price support program> alignment: shown, never scored, independents only, in its own
column with zero weight. The share of in-scope independents reading "Not Aligned" is reported
once in Method beside the same figure one level wider, with no commercial interpretation,
because pricing rules, minimum price laws and program mechanics differ by state. The same
restraint applies throughout the output: report what the data says, attribute it, and leave
the sales judgment to the person who knows their state.

Columns, in mandatory order: Priority (High / Medium / Low), <store id>, the span-of-control
block, Store, Address, City, State, Zip, Chain or "Independent", Volume, Vol %ile, Score, Flags
(named, never counted), Last call, Days since, Our assortment %, Competitor assortment %,

text. Both auxiliary tabs carry the contract C3 identity block in the same order as every other
store tab so the same computed freeze works, with Flags and Score after Volume. No routing or
clustering output is built from latitude and longitude (routes live in <CRM>), and nothing is
built on the key decision maker email column, populated for roughly one store in nine in the
reference planner; its fill rate is checked each run.

Every store tab (Directed, Top, Next, All Other Stores, Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps) is
visually and structurally identical:

- Header row on row 1 with no banner or note row above it; a caveat goes in the Read Me row for
  that tab and in Method.
- Columns 1 and 2: Rank (or Priority), <store id>; then the span-of-control block (region,
  section, market, territory as applicable, coarsest to finest; empty on Line A); then Store,
  Address, City, State, Zip, Chain, Volume; then the tab-specific columns.
- Freeze panes at the first column after State, row 2, the same computed cell on every store
  tab (the State column plus one, never hard-coded). Rank, <store id>, scope columns, store,
  address, city and state lock; Zip, Chain and Volume scroll.
- A real Excel Table over the full block, TableStyleMedium2, row stripes on, autofilter
  confirmed live on every column before publishing.
- An explicit navy header band on every tab: fill 1F3864, Arial 10 bold, white text, never
  left to the table style.
- The narrative "why" or "what to do" column last and widest.
- Thin 9BA7B5 gridlines on all four sides of every populated cell including the header, each
  side set explicitly per cell, never the style's partial rules or an outline.
- Arial 10, top-aligned, wrap on; numerics right-aligned; an explicit width on every column;
  an explicit row height wherever wrap is on; the narrative column 100 to 124 wide.

Formatting is read back through the workbook engine, not a value read: the built workbook is
opened with the spreadsheet library and its freeze pane, table collection (range and style per
table) and per-cell border, font, fill and alignment attributes read directly. If the
environment cannot expose them, the gate cannot run and the workbook is not published. All
thirteen elements of contract section C4 (the layout above, plus the alert shading on Coverage
Gaps only: amber FFF2CC at 150 to 179 days, red F8CBAD at 180 or more, none below 150) are
verified programmatically on every one of the six store tabs and recorded as a thirteen-by-six
pass matrix in Method; checking four and calling it verified is what shipped a bare All Other
Stores tab beside styled visit tabs. The shading gate does not require the shaded count to
equal the Read Me figures, which are population-wide; it checks that every displayed row in a
band carries the right fill and no other row carries any. Any failing element blocks
publication and routes to the Stage 10 repair loop.

The contract C3 identity block is identical on all six store tabs: nine fixed columns on Line
A, nine plus one to four scope columns on Line B.

| Column | Source |
|---|---|
| Rank (or Priority on Coverage Gaps) | position in that list |
| <store id> | the <store id column> and its synonyms; the lookup key, always first after Rank |
| The span-of-control block | the scope levels below the requester, coarsest to finest (contract section C3); empty on Line A; sits between <store id> and Store because managers sort and group by it |
| Store, Address, City, State, Zip | store name, street address, city, state, postal code |
| Chain | the <parent chain account name column>, or "Independent" when blank |
| Volume | the ranking metric for this run |
| Scope rank | position in the ranked population, immediately after Volume on every store tab, closing the identity block; directed stores carry a real Scope rank; the header is "Scope rank" at every level, never a territory- or volume-specific variant |
| Must close (visit tabs) | every activity closing this cycle at the store; 0 on every row when the planner carries no activity block |
| Q (visit tabs) | the headquarters-question store marker; blank throughout when either source column is absent |

| Why (visit tabs) | the reason, naming priority and gaps |

Every scope level below the requester's own is a required text column, in hierarchy order
between <store id> and Store, populated from the same column the scope filter used (a
<market role> needs the owning <territory role>, a <section role> or <region role> the levels beneath,
an <HQ role> all four). Contract section C3b is authoritative on header strings, and the Read Me
states the population the rank is computed over. Filtering a tab to one territory shows which
of these stores sit there; it does not produce that <territory role>'s own report, which needs
a run at their scope. <store id> and Zip are written as text for leading zeros (the zip resolver
matches zip, store zip code, zip code, postal code and similar); a numeric source column has
already lost the zero, so the value is left-padded to width (five digits for a zip) and the
padding noted in Method.

The skill says "business impact", never "stores that matter", and no list, header or summary
implies omitted stores are unimportant. Every heading in the workbook or any message about it is
Title Case (first and last word and all major words capitalized; articles, coordinating
conjunctions and prepositions of four letters or fewer lowercase unless first or last; the
first word after a colon capitalized), never all caps or sentence case; table column headers
stay sentence case ("Must close", "Days since", "Est. upside u/wk").

ASCII only is a hard rule and a publication gate: every character in a cell, sheet name, table
name, file name or chat reply is ASCII 32 to 126, with no em or en dash, curly quote, ellipsis
character, bullet, middot, arrow, multiplication or division sign or non-breaking space. The
three entry points and defenses: (1) planner text (store names, addresses, chain names carry
curly apostrophes, accents and mojibake), normalized once at parse time before it reaches a
cell (curly quotes and dashes to ASCII, accents to base letters, anything else above 126
dropped), never as a late cleanup pass, with the row never dropped, the name never blanked and
every change logged for Method; (2) priority and email text quoted into the Priorities tab,
sanitized the same way with the wording kept verbatim; (3) text the skill writes itself,
composed in ASCII from the start ("->", "x", "..."). The gate walks every cell value, sheet
name, table name and the file name and asserts each character code is at least 32 and at most
126, testing both bounds (an upper bound alone passes tabs and newlines), reports the count
scanned in Method, and blocks publication on a single miss. It is item 7 of contract section
C6 and runs in Stage 10.

Readability rules are checked before publishing; they are not cosmetic.

- Labels in column A, prose in column B; a label that cannot fit column A in two lines moves to
  column B with a short label beside it. Overflow is never solved by shrinking the font.
- Wrap without an explicit row height clips text. The height is computed for every populated
  cell in the row against its own column width and set to the largest: character count divided
  by (column width times 0.88), rounded up to whole lines, times 15 points. Computing from
  column B alone is the specific mistake.
- The 15pt per line is deliberate padding and a floor (Arial 10 renders about 12.75pt, about 18
  percent headroom); no measured line height is ever substituted.
- Every fixed row height is a minimum: the title band at 32 and section headers at 22 to 24
  grow to the computed height when their text wraps.
- The reason column is 100 to 124 wide, wrap on, height set to the longest entry; column A is
  bold on the Read Me tab; full 9BA7B5 gridlines on every store table.
- Verification is programmatic, not by eye: every populated cell is walked, text length
  compared to width and height, and anything that cannot fully display is fixed.

Execution Architecture: A Supervised Swarm, Not One Long Script:

One agent doing everything in sequence is the single biggest risk to this skill, because a long
run drifts and a silent write failure surfaces forty steps later. The run is a supervisor with
delegated workers and a hard gate between every stage.

Execution Architecture - Roles:

The supervisor (the assistant itself) owns the gates and never does the work. It sequences
stages, launches workers, verifies each returned artifact against the file itself and never
against the worker's report, and decides whether the run advances, retries or stops. Only it may
declare a stage complete. Workers own exactly one stage each and are cheap, replaceable and
disposable: a worker receives the stage contract, the input artifact path and the output
artifact path, nothing else, and cannot advance the run, declare its own work verified or skip
anything. Every worker writes a checkpoint file to the session's working folder before
returning; the checkpoint is the deliverable and the worker's prose summary is never evidence.
A worker failure is isolated to its own partition: when a worker returns nothing, returns a
malformed checkpoint or times out, that partition alone is retried through the graduated ladder
while every other partition's verified output stands. A stage fails only when a partition has
exhausted the ladder and one rebuild from its own checkpoint.

Execution Architecture - The Checkpoint Contract:

Every stage writes a per-stage JSON checkpoint in the session's working folder carrying at
minimum: the stage name; status complete or failed; inputs (file, hash or size, rows in);
outputs (file, rows out); the stage-specific invariant counts the supervisor re-checks; and an
unresolved list of any column, store or figure the stage could not settle. A stage that cannot
write its checkpoint has failed regardless of what it says; absence of the file is a failure
signal, not an ambiguity.

Execution Architecture - The Stages, in Order, Each Gated:

Each stage lists its dependencies, what its worker produces, and the supervisor gate that must
pass before the run advances:

1. Identity and role (no dependency). Produces user, role, report line (A or B), supervisor
  chain. Gate: the role resolves to exactly one line; the geography-word test ran before the
  <territory role> test; the manager lookup in the people directory was actually called.
2. Planner acquisition (no dependency). Produces the file bytes, sheet chosen, header row. Gate:
  the file opens; the sheet is justified by resolvable columns; the header row was chosen by
  string-cell score and recorded with its runner-up.
3. Parse and column map (depends on 2). Produces activity block bounds, full column map, record
  count. Gate: store identifier, volume measure and at least one scope column resolved; the
  activity block anchored by the Appendix A ladder (the count row first, then an exact
  rightmost grand-total header, then the prefix fallbacks, then "no activity block exists",
  which is a valid outcome) with the anchor and index recorded; resolved-but-empty columns
  listed; the record count from non-blank <store ids>, never the sheet extent.
4. Scope and qualifiers (depends on 1, 3). Produces scope code, level, resolved qualifiers,
  filtered row set. Gate: scope compared as text on both sides; ambiguous codes resolved against
  the file, not the digit pattern; the filter ran on that level's own column; every qualifier
  phrase resolved to a column and matched value set or was reported as a miss with the nearest
  present values, never silently dropped or widened; qualifiers applied before the closed-store
  exclusion and the floor; the row count reconciles at each step of the funnel.
5. Cycle and priorities (depends on 1, 3). Produces target cycle, supervisor and headquarters
  priorities, directed stores. Gate: the target cycle resolved by the stated ladder and named;
  a <monthly POS kit> present or the fallback ladder walked and the substitute month named;
  every priority carries a source and date; directed stores matched to rows or reported
  unmatched; the retrieval gate passed (every attachment on every manager message enumerated
  and read, read and empty in scope, or declared unreadable in the opener with every path
  attempted named). A zero directed count with an unread manager attachment outstanding fails.
6. <jurisdiction> <sign type 1> validation (depends on 3, 4, 5). Produces permitted or
  restricted per priority per state. Gate: every distinct in-scope state checked or explicitly
  recorded as unreachable; every dropped priority named with its rule; never skipped, and
  unknown is not pass.
7. Volume and scoring (depends on 4, 6). Produces volume basis, per-store scores and ranks.
  Gate: volume tier and time basis stated; the lifestyle exclusion ran after the directed set
  was resolved, with counts recorded (removed, readmitted per escape, scope median used); no
  volume floor applied and zero-or-null-volume stores ranked last rather than dropped; the
  percentile computed by the exact stated method over the full ranked population;
  degenerate-scope guards evaluated; three random stores recomputed and matched; the
  <market support role> line gate evaluated and recorded, with halving on Line A only, the
  <market support role> store set sourced from the derived territory code (the market code plus the
  <market support role territory suffix>), then the mailbox, then the <market support role>
  headquarters-only columns, and an absent <market support role> territory recorded as normal.
8. List assembly (depends on 7). Produces directed, visit, reference and auxiliary rows. Gate:
  counts exact per contract C5; zero overlap; ranks continuous across the three merit tabs;
  directed excluded from merit and cut by volume rank; auxiliary tabs have total sort orders.
9. Workbook build (depends on 8). Produces the Excel file tab by tab. Gate, per tab: rows
  verified and all thirteen formatting elements verified, largest tab first, each finished
  before the next starts.
10. Final audit (depends on 9). Produces the pass matrix. Gate: every item in contract section
  C6 and every Uniformity Check passes, including the formatting matrix (elements 1 to 12 on
  all six store tabs, element 13 on Coverage Gaps only). Any fail routes to the repair loop,
  never to publication.

Stage 10 is the contract gate, the only thing between a build and the user; it never passes on
"unknown" and never passes a tab whose formatting matrix has a hole.

Execution Architecture - Loop Logic at Every Gate:

At each gate the supervisor runs the stage worker, reads the checkpoint from disk (not the
worker's reply), and re-verifies the invariants against the actual artifact. On pass it
advances; on fail it increments the attempt count and classifies the failure:

- Transient (empty read, lag, timeout, throttle, partial write): wait 30 seconds, re-read, and
  if still wrong re-send the same work, with no attempt limit; the wait escalates 30s, 60s,
  120s, then 120s thereafter.
- Scoped (one chunk, tab, column or store failed): narrow and retry the narrowed unit (a 50-row
  chunk to 25, then 10, then 1; one tab rather than the workbook; one column rather than the
  map), up to 3 times, then rebuild that unit from its checkpoint. Still failing, degrade that
  unit only: write the rows without the optional feature, record the loss in Method, continue.
  One column never stops a workbook.
- Logical (an invariant genuinely violated: counts disagree, ranks discontinuous, a lifestyle
  store present, a formatting element absent): repair in place (renumber ranks, rewrite the
  missing tab, re-apply the formatting call) and re-verify, up to 3 repair cycles per
  invariant.
- Hard gate (one of the gates named below): stop and report. These are the only stops.

With no forward progress on a stage after the full ladder, the supervisor escalates once by
rebuilding the stage from its last good checkpoint. Still no progress means stop, publish
nothing, and report which stage, which invariant, what was tried and where the checkpoint sits
so the run can resume. A stuck stage never ships a partial workbook, however honestly labeled;
this is what keeps a 300,000-record run and a 2,200-record run of the same role the same
report. Retry is graduated, not counted.

"No forward progress" is the termination condition, defined per stage type and recorded in the
checkpoint each attempt:

| Stage type | Forward progress means |
|---|---|
| Reading or writing rows (parse, build) | rows read, or verified rows landed, higher than the previous attempt |
| Resolving references (identity, scope, qualifiers, cycle, priorities) | at least one unresolved item resolved or one new candidate eliminated |
| Computing (percentile, medians, scoring) | scored records higher, or a previously failing sub-block completes |
| Assembling (list assembly, tab construction) | tabs with both rows and formatting verified higher than the previous attempt |
| Validating (<sign type 1>, final audit) | failing checks lower than the previous attempt |

Every stage maps to exactly one row; a stage mapping to none is a defect in the table and is
treated as stuck only after the full ladder and one rebuild. The comparison is against the
previous attempt's recorded measure, never an absolute threshold, and the test is strict
improvement. An identical measure twice in a row after the matching retry path means one rebuild
from checkpoint; identical again means stuck.

There are exactly ten hard gates and no others; all stop the run before or instead of publishing.

| # | Gate | Where it fires |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No store identifier resolves | Parse |
| 2 | No volume measure resolves in any tier | Parse |
| 3 | Zero records: no row carries a <store id> | Parse |
| 4 | The file cannot be opened at all | Acquisition |
| 5 | No scope column resolves and no level can be derived | Scope |
| 6 | An empty scope after the text re-test and the level re-read | Scope |
| 7 | A named qualifier routed to a column but matched no value | Scope; report the nearest values |
| 8 | A comparison was requested and only one planner is present | Cycle |
| 9 | The runtime cannot expose workbook structure, so the formatting gate cannot run | Build |
| 10 | No forward progress after the full ladder and one rebuild | Any |

Gates 7 and 8 stop before a workbook exists and ask the user one question; the rest stop
outright. Nothing else stops a run: not run length, file size, tab count, retry count, a
missing <monthly POS kit>, an unreachable <sign type 1> state guide library or an unresolvable
optional column. Gate 10 is the only one reachable mid-build, and the rule is the same: stop,
report, preserve the checkpoint, publish nothing. A worker's optimism never advances the run:
"wrote 250 rows" is a claim, a range read showing 250 populated rows is evidence, and where they
disagree the file wins.

Execution Architecture - Fail Closed, Always:

- A gate that cannot be evaluated has failed; unknown is not pass.
- Partial output is never delivered. A tab that fails in list assembly or the build is repaired
  through the loop; if it cannot be, the run stops and publishes nothing, names the tab and the
  exact ranks or rows affected in the chat reply, and preserves the checkpoint. A partial
  labeled in the Read Me is still a partial. "Empty" has two meanings: a tab with nothing to
  report is complete, carries its explanatory row and ships; a tab that failed to build blocks
  publication.
- No stage may weaken an earlier guarantee, and every gate re-checks the previous one. The
  lifestyle exclusion, directed-store separation and closed-store exclusion hold from the
  moment they are set. From stage 4 onward every gate runs the regression check against the
  current artifact, not the earlier checkpoint's claim:

  | Re-check at every gate | Fails if |
  |---|---|
  | Record count | the working set no longer reconciles to the parse count minus every documented exclusion |
  | Scope purity | any row appears whose scope code is outside the resolved scope |
  | Closed-store exclusion | any store flagged temporarily closed has reappeared |
  | Lifestyle exclusion | any store carrying the <lifestyle store flag> appears on any tab without qualifying under one of the three escapes, or the directed set was resolved after the class filter |
  | No volume floor | any store was dropped from the ranked population for low, zero or null volume rather than ranking low |
  | Qualifier and FILTER membership | any row is present that the qualifier or category filter excluded |
  | Directed-set ordering | the lifestyle exclusion ran before the directed set was resolved from the mailbox |
  | Percentile stability | three sampled stores still carry the percentile the scoring stage assigned |

  A regression failure is a logical failure routed to repair, not a stage retry, naming the
  invariant and the stage that set it; silent re-admission grows more likely with every added
  worker, which is why the check runs at every gate.
- Any unresolved item propagates to the final Method; nothing quietly disappears between stages.

Execution Architecture - What Actually Runs in Parallel:

Only stages 1 and 2 are independent, and exactly those two launch together; every later stage
consumes an earlier checkpoint. Stage 3 is not independent: priorities need the supervisor
that identity resolves; the <sign type 1> check needs the parsed state column; the scope
ladder reads distinct values from the parsed file; and the parse's close-date derivation needs
the cycle window, which is why cycle resolution sits in stage 5 after the parse and reads the
planner's filename and banner rows. Launching the three together once produced an unvalidated,
headquarters-only priority set that dropped the state-restricted exclusions and the
directed-store matches. The assistant never parallelizes across a gate to save time.

Execution Architecture - Fanning Out Inside a Stage: How a Large File Actually Gets Worked:

Parallelism inside a stage never crosses a checkpoint, so it is always safe and at scale
mandatory. Below roughly 5,000 records one worker per stage is fine; above that the stage fans
out under this contract exactly:

1. Partition deterministically: contiguous blocks of 25,000 records by <store id> ascending, so
  the same file yields the same boundaries on every run.
2. One worker per partition, capped at eight concurrent workers.
3. Every worker writes its own per-partition checkpoint with its bounds, rows in, rows out and
  invariants; a worker that cannot write it has failed, whatever it reports.
4. The supervisor verifies the merge, not the workers: every partition index present with no
  gaps, partition row counts summing to the stage's expected total, no <store id> in two
  partitions. A missing partition is the failure that looks most like success.
5. One failed partition is retried alone through the ladder while the others stand; the stage is
  never re-run whole for one block, and the run never advances with a partition missing.
6. Nothing needing the whole population is computed inside a partition: the volume percentile,
  every median and every peer norm are global, workers return raw values, and the supervisor
  computes the distribution once over the merged set. A per-partition percentile is a different
  number wearing the same name.

| Stage | Fans out? |
|---|---|
| Parse, scope filter, qualifier application, per-store scoring | Yes; row-parallel, no cross-row dependency |
| Priority and <sign type 1> lookups | Yes; one worker per supervisor level, one per state |
| Percentile, medians, peer norms | Never; global by definition, computed once by the supervisor |
| List assembly, ranking, workbook build, final audit | Never; inherently ordered |

Build Reliability Protocol: Mandatory, Never Traded Away for Speed:

The report may take as long as it needs; it may not be wrong. Every rule in this protocol costs time
and buys correctness. The assistant never skips a verification because the build is running long and
never shortens a list to finish sooner.

Build Reliability Protocol - The Two Failure Modes, and Why They Look Identical:

1. A write reports success and lands nothing. A success acknowledgement from the workbook write tool
   means the request was accepted, not that the cells exist; a 100-row batch has returned success
   and written zero rows.
2. Verification lags the write by 20 to 30 seconds. The value-read service trails the write service,
   so a read taken immediately after a write can return empty for data that is fine.

Both look like "the data is not there". Every empty read is treated as unconfirmed, never as failed,
until the wait-and-re-read step below settles it; re-sending on a false alarm wastes a build, and
concluding a real drop corrupts the report.

Build Reliability Protocol - Writing:

1. Chunk at 50 rows, each chunk one write call carrying one cell-range patch.
2. One kind of operation per call. Data writes are never mixed with table creation or other
   structural operations; a table applied in the same call as a failed write has left a sheet
   permanently unwritable, acknowledging every later write and landing nothing.
3. Data first, structure second, per tab, never one structural call for the whole workbook. The
   assistant writes and verifies every row of one tab, applies that tab's table, freeze pane,
   borders, fonts, alignments, widths and row heights in separate calls, verifies those landed, and
   only then starts the next tab. One structural call spanning nine tabs is how a single dropped
   call un-styles half a workbook.
4. Formatting writes get the same treatment as data writes, because they fail silently the same way.
   After each tab's structural call the assistant re-reads the tab through the artifact model and
   confirms the table exists with the right style name, the freeze pane sits one column right of
   State, and a sampled interior cell carries all four borders. If the read is wrong it waits 30
   seconds and reads again, then re-sends, then splits the structural call into smaller ones, then
   rebuilds the tab.

Build order is by actual size, largest first, and each tab is finished before the next begins: the
assistant counts the rows each store tab will hold, builds the store tabs in descending order of
that count, then Priorities, then Method, then Read Me last, because the Read Me tab map must name
what was actually built. The sequence is never hard-coded; a section, region or national directive
can name thousands of stores and make Directed (capped at 500) one of the largest tabs. Smallest
first spends the run's budget on the small tabs and reaches the 500-row tab with nothing left, which
is how a workbook ships with a bare reference tab.

The three panels (Read Me, Priorities, Method) are built last but carry the same completion rule. A
tab is not complete until its data and its thirteen formatting elements are verified. Per-tab
completion is recorded in the stage checkpoint as the tab name, its row count and a true-or-false
formatting-pass flag; a tab whose flag is false is as incomplete as one missing rows, and the run
does not advance past it. Formatting is never deferred to a trailing pass.

Build Reliability Protocol - Checkpointing: Wait Before You Judge:

After each chunk the assistant:

1. Reads back the last three rows of the chunk, never the first; a partial write fills the head and
   drops the tail.
2. If the read is empty or short, does not retry yet: it waits 30 seconds and reads again. This step
   separates a real failure from replication lag.
3. If still empty after the wait, re-sends the same chunk (the write is idempotent) and re-verifies
   with the same wait-and-re-read.
4. After three failed retries, splits the chunk in half and writes 25 rows at a time.
5. Treats a 25-row chunk failing three times as a damaged sheet: it recreates that one sheet under a
   slightly different name and rewrites it from the top. This is the only deletion the skill ever
   performs: a single tab, inside the in-progress scratch workbook, before anything is published. It
   never deletes a published file, a user's planner or source data, or a tab holding verified rows,
   and never recreates a sheet to "start clean" when the real problem is a failed chunk.
6. Never starts the next chunk until the current one is confirmed; a skipped chunk becomes a hole in
   the middle of the list, harder to spot than a truncated end.

Build Reliability Protocol - Final Reconciliation, All of It, Before Publishing:

The assistant waits 30 seconds after the last write, then verifies every item; any failure sends the
run back to the retry loop, and none is optional.

- Row count exact per tab, against that tab's own expected count. First visit tab: 10 (Line A, the
  <territory role> line) or 20 (Line B, the manager tier). Second visit tab: 25 (Line A) or 50 (Line
  B). A user-requested size overrides the role default under contract C1, splitting one-third to the
  first tier, rounded up, and the remainder to the second ("Top 50" gives 17 plus 33; "just the top
  five" gives 2 plus 3), with the tabs named for the counts they hold and the split stated in the
  Read Me. All Other Stores: the smaller of 500 and the eligible count minus the last visit rank.
  The last merit rank is therefore 535 (Line A) or 570 (Line B) on a full run, or the requested size
  plus the reference-tab count on a sized run, never 35 or 70: those are where the visit tiers end,
  and All Other Stores continues the numbering. Asserting 35 or 70 fails a correct workbook and
  burns the budget the formatting pass needs. The only permitted shortfall is fewer eligible stores
  than a tab's size, stated on the tab as "showing n of N". Directed, All Other Stores, Assortment
  Targets and Coverage Gaps are each capped separately at 500 and reconciled against their own
  "showing n of N"; directed stores never count toward a merit tab.
- Rank continuity across the merit tabs: Top starts at 1, Next resumes after Top's last rank, All
  Other Stores resumes after that, forming one unbroken run with no gaps or duplicates. Numbering
  never restarts at 1 on a later tab. Directed stores are ranked 1 to N among themselves on their
  own tab.
- No cross-tab duplicates: no <store id> on more than one of Top, Next and All Other Stores, and no
  directed store on any of the three.
- Scope purity: every row belongs to the requested scope at its own level (territory, <market>,
  section, region, or the whole file for a national run). One stray row makes the list suspect.
- No blank required cells, with two exemptions stated on the row: a directed store named in a
  callout but not found in the planner carries a blank rank and blank identity fields with the note
  "named in the directive, not found in this planner", and a directed store with no volume carries a
  blank Volume and Scope rank. Every other row is fully populated in the identity block (<store id>,
  store, address, city, state, zip, chain, volume) and in the tab's narrative last column. The
  single explanatory row on an empty tab is exempt.
- Exclusions respected: no lifestyle store on any tab unless it qualified under one of the three
  escapes, and no store dropped for low, zero or null volume.
- Types preserved: <store id> and Zip still read as text, because Excel strips leading zeros
  silently and the <store id> is how every system finds the store.
- Source spot-check: three stores re-read at random from the planner, with volume, city and
  <store id> matching the source rows exactly. This is the only check that catches a mapping error.

Build Reliability Protocol - Uniformity Checks: Run These Before Publishing, Every Time:

Every item below was a real defect in a shipped build and is verified programmatically. These checks
are the Stage 10 gate: the run does not publish until every one passes, and each result is recorded
as a named pass or fail in Method.

- All nine tabs present in the contract C2 order (Read Me, Priorities, Directed, first visit tab,
  second visit tab, All Other Stores, Assortment Targets, Coverage Gaps, Method); a tab with nothing
  to report still exists with its explanatory row.
- The contract C4 formatting matrix: elements 1 to 12 on every one of the six store tabs, element 13
  on Coverage Gaps only and recorded as N/A on the other five, which carry no "Days since" column.
  An N/A cell is a pass; scoring those five cells as failures blocks every run. The matrix is
  printed in Method. This is the most important check in the list.
- Full gridlines confirmed by reading them back. On tabs of 100 rows or fewer every populated cell
  is walked and all four borders confirmed present, thin and the grid color 9BA7B5; on larger tabs
  the header row, the first and last twenty data rows and fifty rows sampled evenly through the
  middle are walked, with the sampling recorded. Never reduced to three cells.
- Every element gets a stated sampling method. Element 5, header band: row 1 of each store tab read,
  every header cell confirmed navy fill 1F3864, bold, white font, Arial 10, identical on all six
  tabs (a header rendering as plain bordered text has lost the band to the gridline override).
  Element 6, autofilter: the table definition read and the filter confirmed over every column of the
  block. Element 8, font: Arial 10, top-aligned, wrap on, walked in the same full-or-sampled mode as
  the gridlines, mode recorded. Element 9: volume, rank and any score or count column sampled and
  confirmed right-aligned.
- The identity block structurally identical across all six store tabs (Rank or Priority, <store id>,
  the span-of-control block, Store, Address, City, State, Zip, Chain, Volume), the scope block
  carrying the same columns on every tab, verified by header name and relative order, never by fixed
  column number, because the block's width changes with the requester's level.
- Header on row 1 on all six store tabs, with no banner or note row above it.
- Freeze panes at the first column after State on all six, identical, verified by name: the header
  row is read, State found, and the freeze confirmed one column to its right. Rank, <store id>,
  every scope column, Store, Address, City and State inside the frozen block; Zip, Chain and Volume
  outside it.
- <store id> in column 2 on all six, the span-of-control block immediately after it, and Store as
  the first column after that block.
- An Excel Table over every store block with the same style name on each, every table carrying the
  full identity block in the order above.
- Zero characters above ASCII 126 in any cell, sheet name or file name.
- Every heading in Title Case, in the workbook and in any message about it. Table column headers are
  field labels and are exempt ("Must close", "<business unit> focus", "Scope rank", "Days since",
  "Est. upside units per week" stay in sentence case); the exemption is part of the contract.
- Row height sufficient for wrapped text, the checkable form of "nothing is clipped". For every
  populated wrapped cell in a row: lines needed equals the text length divided by 0.88 times that
  cell's own column width, rounded up, times 15 points; the row height must be at least the row
  maximum. The constant is 15 (padding, never a measured 12.75-point line height) and the comparison
  is "at least". The check is never stated as "no cell clips", which depends on font metrics the
  engine does not expose and would make the gate unfalsifiable.
- No blank rows inside a table, and the table range matches the populated range exactly.
- The Read Me tab map lists every tab in workbook order, named exactly as the tabs are named.
- The three panels styled and verified too: a navy 1F3864 title band on row 1 at row height at least
  32; blue 4472C4 section headers at row height at least 22 to 24 in Title Case (minimums that grow
  to the computed height when text wraps); every title band and section header merged across the
  full panel width, so available width is the sum of the merged columns rather than column A alone;
  Arial 10 body text, top-aligned, wrap on; prose in column B rather than a narrow column A; an
  explicit row height on every wrapped cell. A failing panel is a defect exactly as a store tab
  would be.

Build Reliability Protocol - Recovery and Repeatability:

- The computed ranking is persisted to a scratch file before building; a failed build resumes from
  the saved ranking rather than recomputing, which risks a different result.
- A rerun on the same inputs must produce the same list; otherwise something is non-deterministic
  and is found before the report is trusted.
- The workbook is published only once, at the end. A partial workbook is never published to show
  progress, because a rep cannot tell a partial list from a complete one.
- If the build cannot be completed the assistant says so precisely, naming the tab and the exact
  ranks missing. It never publishes a known gap, quietly shortens a list, or calls an incomplete
  report finished.

The same discipline applies to any long table: it is written in blocks, then the artifact model is
read and the reported row count confirmed against what was sent. The assistant never stops
mid-workbook; it retries, waits, splits and recreates the sheet before giving up. A workbook 80
percent populated and silently published is worse than one that failed loudly.

Content and Order Within the Workbook:

The stores come first and everything explanatory goes after them; the opener is a few lines, not a
page. Tab order is fixed by Step 8. The content contract below states what is written and where it
lives: items 1 and 9 are the Read Me tab; item 2 is the chat reply, with its quoted priorities
carried in full onto the Priorities tab; items 3 and 4 are the merit tabs; items 5 to 8 are the
Method tab, in that order.

1. Title block: name, role, scope, cycle month, date.
2. Opener: one line stating that stores were ranked by a volume-driven algorithm weighted against
   the priorities below, over N stores in scope; a short quoted bullet list of <user's manager>'s
   priorities this cycle, attributed with name and date; a short bullet list of headquarters
   priorities this month from the <monthly POS kit>, with its initiative window; one line naming any
   directed stores with the full directed count.
3. The first visit tab (Top 20 for the manager tier, Top 10 for a <territory role>).
4. The second visit tab (ranks 21 to 70 for the manager tier, 11 to 35 for a <territory role>).
5. How These Stores Were Selected: volume metric, the lifestyle exclusion, <business unit> weights,
   the close-date rule, the user's steer if any.
6. Alignment: where <user's manager> and headquarters agree, where they differ, the most crucial
   focus.
7. Method: the <jurisdiction>'s <sign type 1> state guide checked, which state files were read, any
   priority dropped or conditioned by a state rule and the rule; the steer mode (TILT, FILTER,
   INITIATIVE or NONE), the words that triggered it and why that reading was chosen; the target
   cycle and the rule that resolved it; the volume column used, its unit and time basis, whether it
   was synthesized, every <business unit> column that fed a composite and a note that
   cross-<business unit> units are not additive; the planner file with sender and date; the
   <monthly POS kit> used; the sheet or sheets chosen and how many were rejected; the header row and
   why it won; the activity-block anchor; every column that resolved, resolved but empty, or did not
   resolve; the scope value and its derivation; the full row-count funnel; every gap or assumption.
8. Appendix: How This List Was Calculated, the full audit trail below.
9. Questions: a closing line inviting questions about how the skill works, naming <author> and
   <author email>.

Within any tab and the chat reply, selection criteria, alignment and methodology never sit above the
store lists; this governs order inside a tab, not the tab order.

The appendix is exhaustive by design and sits at the very end, so a skeptical manager can
reconstruct every number. In order:

- Sources checked: every mailbox search and its window, every SharePoint location queried, what was
  found and what was not, including searches that returned nothing.
- Identity and scope resolution: how user, role, supervisor and scope were derived, including the
  <market>-versus-territory expansion and the exact filter value used.
- File parsing: header row detection and why that row won, total columns, and the full
  concept-to-column mapping resolved, each named.
- Population funnel: rows in file, rows in scope, rows after each qualifier (named one at a time),
  rows after the closed-store exclusion, rows after the lifestyle exclusion, ranked population (N),
  minus directed stores, eligible count. Each step publishes the count remaining and the count
  dropped, measured inside the scope as it stood entering that step; a file-wide figure never
  appears (a territory run reports only closed stores that were in that territory, not those in the
  wider <market>). There is no floor step: zero or null volume ranks last in the zero-volume bucket.
  N and the eligible count are published as two figures: N is what every percentile and median was
  computed over and includes directed stores; the eligible count is the merit remainder that sized
  the tabs. Rows in scope is the count before the closed-store exclusion.
- Volume metric: the tier used, its exact column name and time basis, the median, minimum and
  maximum, and the count of zero-or-null volume stores ranked last.
- Activity inventory: total activities detected, split by originator tag and by <business unit>, how
  many were flagged at one or more in-scope stores, how many resolved but were empty, and every
  activity flagged at 5 percent or more of the population listed by name with its store count.
- Local activity detection: every activity carrying a scope number, the number found, whether it
  resolved as the user's own or another unit's, and the store count of each.
- Work-type weighting: every activity weighted zero, by name and store count, and whether the
  classification came from the kit's published type field, the price-subject rule or the
  confirm-verb fallback.
- Close-date derivation: the <monthly POS kit> window and the count of activities dated by each rule
  separately (rule 1, named by the kit or supervisor; rule 2, a period token in the header; rule 3,
  dropped). Zero activities dated by rule 1 on a cycle whose kit named several means the ladder was
  inverted.
- The scoring formula written out: every term, weight and the arithmetic, so a reader can recompute
  one store by hand.
- A worked example: one real store end to end, with volume, percentile, each scoring activity with
  its <business unit> and close weight, its gap terms and the total.
- Overrides applied: directed stores, what directed them and what they displaced.
- Known limitations: anything inferred, carried forward or unverifiable.

The non-table panels (Read Me, Priorities, Method) are styled to match the store tabs with one
visual system:

- Sheet title, row 1: white bold 16 point on navy 1F3864, row height at least 32.
- Section headers: white bold 12 point on blue 4472C4, row height at least 22 to 24, Title Case,
  merged across the full panel width (column A through the last populated column); an unmerged
  header is measured against column A alone and clips.
- Label column: bold navy on light blue EAF0F8 fill, thin 9BA7B5 borders.
- Body text: Arial 10, top-aligned, wrap on, thin 9BA7B5 borders where it forms a table.
- Spacer rows: height 10, between sections only.
- All row heights are minimums; any wrapped row grows to the per-cell computation in the uniformity
  checks, with 15 points as fixed padding, never 12.75.

Every Method section is numbered and titled (for example "1. Sources Checked") so a reviewer can be
pointed to one. Store tables carry thin 9BA7B5 borders on all four sides of every populated cell,
header row included, set explicitly per side and per cell rather than left to the table style, which
renders banding and partial rules only. Volume columns are right-aligned and the "why" column is
widest. The header row carries the navy band explicitly (fill 1F3864, Arial 10 bold, white text),
because cell-level gridlines outrank the table style's header rendering. The shaded label styling
applies to the three panels only. The assistant checks the organization's SharePoint template
library first, otherwise uses the navy and blue palette, and honors supplied brand colors or a
template. Verification runs through the artifact model, not the file on disk, which lags edits and
reports a correct workbook as broken.

Output Format:

File: an Excel workbook in the session's output folder, named "<scope> <month> <year> Store Visit
Plan.xlsx".

Per-store row: rank, <store id>, the span-of-control block (the scope levels below the requester),
store, address, city, state, zip, chain, volume and its rank within scope, must-close count, the
headquarters-question marker (Q), <business unit> focus, and one or two sentences of why, naming the
<business unit>, the gap and any directive.

Chat: 5 to 8 lines and no more: the scope and cycle used, the planner used, the steer mode in plain
words, the supervisor and headquarters priorities found (or plainly that none were), the directed
count, the top 3 stores with one line each, and the filename. The full list is never pasted.

Guardrails:

The assistant:

- Never fabricates a store, number, priority or quote; missing data is reported as missing.
- Never confuses levels in the organization code family. Levels are classified by trailing zeros
  (territory, then <market>, then section, then region), never by a bare test for a trailing "00".
  Trailing-zero classification is decisive only up to three trailing zeros; at four or more the code
  is ambiguous (a code ending in four zeros can be a section or a <market>, one ending in five zeros
  a region, section or <market>) and is resolved against the file by testing each candidate level's
  own column and taking the level that returns rows.
- Never analyzes a partial workbook.
- Never trusts a write's success response; it reads the last rows of every chunk back and reconciles
  the final row count before publishing.
- Never treats an empty read as a failed write; it waits 30 seconds and reads again first.
- Never mixes data writes with structural operations in one call.
- Never publishes an incomplete list; it retries, splits, recreates the sheet, then reports missing
  ranks by number.
- Never stops because a stage failed twice. It classifies the failure and works the matching path:
  wait on transient, narrow on scoped, repair on logical. Only the five named hard gates stop a run,
  and a stuck stage stops it without publishing anything.
- Never stops because the file is large; the output is capped, so a 250,000-store planner produces
  the same size workbook as a 2,000-store one.
- Puts correctness above speed, always.
- Never deletes anything outside the scratch workbook; the only permitted deletion is one damaged,
  unpublished tab during a rebuild.
- Never infers decline from one planner: if the user asked for a comparison it requests a second
  month; if not, it says nothing about trend. A comparison never changes the deliverable shape (10
  plus 25 for Line A, 20 plus 50 for Line B, plus the reference tab of up to 500 rows).
- Confirms a memory save succeeded before relying on a remembered territory.
- Never lets <business unit> weighting override volume; mandatory-store callouts override both.
- Treats undated activities as weightless and never lets raw activity count drive a ranking, but
  dates activities by the kit first: one named in this cycle's <monthly POS kit> is admitted as a
  scoring activity whatever its header says, and its close weight comes from its own published due
  date (1.00, 0.75, 0.50 or 0.20). Admission and close weight are separate questions; an activity
  named by this month's kit but due late next month still weights 0.50.
- Always reads the <monthly POS kit> for the cycle being planned; if it cannot be found, says so and
  names where it looked. Supervisor email is never a substitute for headquarters.
- Does not caveat a planner the user supplied when it matches the emailed file by name and size.
- Never asks the user to restate a request the file can already answer: it pads the code, maps the
  brand, reads the steer and delivers.
- Treats a resolved but empty column as an absent column, never a gap, a flag or a benchmark.
- Computes all arithmetic with a code tool, quotes priorities verbatim with sender and date, and
  never lets the two visit lists overlap.
- Declines any request to rank or evaluate people; stores only.
- Never emails or shares the document automatically.

Appendix A: Planner Column Mapping:

Planner formats drift. Two real exports of the same <market>, five months apart, differed in the
header row (row 4 beneath banners and counts, versus row 1), sheet count (one versus five, including
per-person extracts and an unrelated transfer list), the names of the territory, store id and store
name columns (short labels versus system field names), the volume basis (latest 13 weeks in cartons
versus year-to-date per <business unit> with per-week suffixes), scope codes stored as numbers
versus text, an activity block with a count row and a Grand Total column versus no activities at
all, a closed-store column present versus absent, contract columns populated versus present but
empty, and 156 columns versus 48. Both are valid inputs and both must produce a workbook; every
difference is handled by a named rule below, and the pair is the portability test for any change to
this appendix.

Appendix A - Sheet Selection:

The store data is not reliably on the first tab, and the first sheet is never taken on faith.

1. If a sheet name matches the user's <market id> stem or <territory id> exactly after stripping
   spaces and punctuation, it is taken, but only after confirming it resolves a store identifier, a
   volume measure and a scope column; a summary, pivot or transfer sheet can carry the <market>'s
   name. If the named sheet fails the column check, selection falls through to scoring every sheet.
   The whole name is matched, never a prefix, so a shorter section code that begins a <market> name
   does not pick it up.
2. Otherwise every sheet is scored by resolvable required columns (store identifier, volume, scope)
   and the highest scorer taken. A sheet resolving an account number and a scope column but no
   volume is not the data sheet, whatever its size.
3. Ties break on record count (rows with a non-blank account number), never the sheet's reported
   extent, which in the reference planner was nearly five times the real record count.
4. Sheets whose names read as documentation are ignored (Read Me, Notes, Legend, Glossary,
   Definitions, Cover, Instructions, Pivot, Summary, Dashboard).
5. A sheet named after a person is a working extract: per-rep tabs resolve the same columns and tie
   on score, so the record-count tie-break keeps them from winning, and their scope is never treated
   as the user's.

When no single sheet covers the requested scope the assistant concatenates rather than chooses:
every sheet whose resolved column set matches is taken, rows are concatenated, duplicates are
collapsed on account number keeping the highest-volume row, and Method states how many sheets were
combined and how many duplicates collapsed. Concatenation happens only when one sheet is genuinely
not enough. Method names the sheet or sheets chosen and how many were rejected; a silent wrong-sheet
pick is the worst failure this skill has.

Appendix A - Header Row Detection:

The first 15 rows are read, each scored by its count of non-empty string cells, and the highest
taken; data starts on the next row. At least 15 rows are scanned, because a planning tool carries
banners and per-activity counts above a header that can sit on row 4, while an extract puts it on
row 1. A tie or near-tie breaks in order: prefer the row whose values are all distinct; prefer the
row with no numeric cells; prefer the row with at least one non-empty row beneath it; then prefer
the topmost. The choice is confirmed by resolving the required concepts against it; if the required
three do not resolve, the next-best candidate row is tried before failing. The row chosen and why
are recorded in Method.

Appendix A - Matching Rules:

Both sides are normalized (lowercase; punctuation, spaces and underscores stripped), then matched in
three passes across the whole header list: exact, then prefix, then substring. Within a pass the
first hit wins and ties prefer the leftmost column. Each pass runs to completion over every stem
before the next starts, so an exact match anywhere beats a substring match. Every resolution is
logged.

Prefix and substring matching are bidirectional (a header matches a stem when either contains the
other after normalization, so a "<business unit 4> contract plan" header matches the
"<business unit 4> contract" stem and a short "<business unit 2> <allowance type A>" header matches
the longer election stem), with one guard: whenever either side is shorter than four normalized
characters, only an exact match counts. Two- and three-letter abbreviations (the store id prefix,
program acronyms, "lat") would otherwise fire inside unrelated words such as "merchandising" and
silently mis-map the lookup key the workbook is built on. Exact matching is never suppressed.

When header and values disagree, or neither settles it, the assistant asks only after trying to
settle it itself. For any column the dictionary cannot resolve confidently it:

1. Reads a sample of populated values and classifies by content: all digits of consistent length is
   an identifier or scope code; a decimal between 0 and 1 is a rate or satisfaction measure; a bare
   number above 1 is a count or volume; a date is a date; a short repeating value set is a status or
   election field; free text is a name or address.
2. Cross-checks the classification against the header words; a "volume" header over values between 0
   and 1 is a rate with a misleading header, and the values are trusted.
3. Compares against columns that did resolve; a candidate volume column whose values track a
   resolved volume column is the same concept at a different time basis.
4. If a required concept (store identifier, volume, scope) is still unresolved, asks the user once,
   in one message, showing the header text exactly, three or four example values and the two or
   three candidates, accepting a column name, a letter or a list position.
5. If an optional concept is unresolved, never asks: it skips the feature the concept feeds, names
   the column and what was skipped in Method, and continues.

Every question is batched into a single message; the assistant never asks, works, then asks again.
Every ambiguous resolution is recorded in Method with the header text, the values seen, the
resolution, and whether the user or the value check decided it, so the author can add the stem.

A resolved column is confirmed by its values: a scope column's populated values must be all digits
once separators are stripped, of consistent length; a volume column's must be numbers. A column that
resolves by name but fails its value check is the wrong column; the next candidate is tried and both
are recorded in Method.

Volume and velocity suffixes (units, cans or cartons, sticks per week, and similar) are
interchangeable and all accepted, since a <business unit> is spelled with a different one from cycle
to cycle; the trailing period token (latest 13, 26 or 52 weeks; year-, month- or quarter-to-date) is
stripped before comparing, and the suffix and token found are recorded as the unit and time basis
for Method.

Null-like values are normalized before any rule applies: an empty cell, whitespace only, and the
literal strings null, NULL, None, N/A, #N/A and a lone hyphen are missing. A chain-name cell reading
"null" would otherwise classify an independent as a chain and invert the independents- only contract
and <price support program> gates.

The activity block is resolved before concept matching. Substring matching is greedy: an activity
column titled as a <business unit 3> contracted-store task to add a <promotional price program> sign
would match the <business unit 3> contract stem and be consumed as a data concept, moving the
activity boundary; more than half the reference planner's activity columns match some data-concept
stem. The block (from the first activity column through the column before Grand Total) is fixed
first, then concepts are matched only among the columns outside it.

The Grand Total boundary is matched exactly, taking the rightmost match, never by substring. In the
reference planner a reminder header containing "brands" contained the substring "rand" and sat
forty-one columns before the real Grand Total; a substring search stopped at the decoy, cut the
activity block from 72 columns to 31, discarded 57 percent of the activity set including every
current-cycle <business unit 4> initiative, and failed silently. The rule: normalize case, compare
the whole normalized header to "grandtotal" for equality, take the rightmost of multiple matches,
and record the anchor column index in Method.

Appendix A - Concept Synonym Sets:

Each concept lists a few representative header stems; real planners use these and similar phrasings,
and the matcher works on normalized stems, never full literals. Only one of the territory, <market>,
section or region scope columns is required: a file resolving any single scope column is admissible,
with finer levels derived by left-anchored prefix and coarser ones by truncation.

- Territory (scope): territory, current territory, territory number or code, route, rep territory,
  and similar phrasings.
- <market>, section or region (scope): <market> code or number, section code, region code, division,
  area, zone, district, and similar phrasings.
- Account number (required): the <store id> label and its abbreviations, account number, store
  number, outlet id, and similar phrasings. Any candidate whose header carries "chain" or a chain
  level marker is rejected; an alternate identifier prefixed with the <CRM> name is a second key for
  another system, recorded but never ranked or joined on.
- Store name (required): store name, outlet name, account name, and similar phrasings.
- Location: address, city, state, zip or postal code, county, with or without a "store" prefix.
- Chain: first- and second-level chain account name, chain account number, chain, the chain flag,
  trade class, trade sub-class. When a file carries both a chain name and a chain flag they are
  reconciled once: a store is a chain when the first-level chain account name is populated after
  null-like normalization; the flag is a cross-check, because the name is what the Chain column
  prints and what the chain-based escape matches against. The independents-only gates and the
  "Independent" label use that one test. Disagreeing rows are counted in Method, and disagreement
  above 2 percent of rows is stated rather than resolved silently.
- Submitter or owner: submitter, submitted by, owner, rep, assigned to. Usually a wholesaler or
  system id, never a reliable rep or territory key; recorded, never scoped on.
- Volume, preferred: <cross-category equivalent volume>, headquarters' own cross-category
  calculation, under its full name, abbreviation and similar phrasings.
- Volume, fallback: <category 1> industry volume in cartons, including a recurring misspelling of
  "Volume" that real exports carry.
- Volume, last resort: <business unit 1>'s own volume.
- Volume, synthesized when no total exists, never by adding raw figures. Per-<business unit> stems
  (<business unit 1> through <business unit 5>, <our brand 4A> premium line, <volume index>,
  <competitor brand 4A>, <category 2>, <category 3>, <category 5>) are matched with any rate suffix
  and period token. The columns are never summed (<business unit 1> is cartons, <business unit 4>
  cans, <business unit 3> sticks, <business unit 5> units), never converted (pack-equivalence makes
  <category 1> dwarf every other signal), and no equivalence multiplier is invented; when
  <cross-category equivalent volume> is present nothing is synthesized. Otherwise a unitless
  composite volume percentile is built: (1) rank stores within each <business unit> column
  separately as a percentile from 0 to 1; (2) average those percentiles, unweighted, over the
  resolved columns in which the store carries a non-null value; (3) use the composite wherever
  volume is called for. The average is never weighted by the Step 5 <business unit> weights, which
  scale the priority term only; weighting here would count the same preference twice. Method states
  that no total existed, which columns were used, that a composite percentile was built rather than
  a sum, and that it is not a quantity to quote.
- A steer to one <business unit> ranks on that <business unit>'s own volume column, not any total,
  and Method names the column.
- Every <business unit> has an own column and a category column, never interchangeable. Own (rank on
  these): <business unit 1> volume; <business unit 4> units per week and <our brand 4A> premium line
  sell-through; <business unit 2> units per week; <business unit 5> units per week;
  <business unit 3> sticks per week. Category (size with these, never rank): <category 1> industry
  volume; <volume index> units per week, <volume index> decile and <competitor brand 4A>
  distribution; <category 2> and deep-discount <category 2> cans per week; <category 5> units per
  week; <category 3> sticks per week. In the reference planner the category column sits immediately
  left of the own column for all five, so the leftmost tie-break picks it every time. When a steer
  names a <business unit>, its own-column stems are tested to exhaustion before any category stem;
  if only a category column exists, the Read Me and Method say "ranked on category volume, which
  includes competitors, because this planner carries no <business unit>-only measure".
- By <business unit>, for weighting and steers, the signal set covers own volume, category volume
  (flagged competitor-inclusive), assortment satisfaction, contract and contract plan, allowance and
  loyalty elections, performance options, multi-pack and priority-store flags, and for
  <business unit 1> the <price support program> election, alignment and recommendation.
- The headquarters-question marker (Q) is derived from two text columns; a numeric comparison
  matches nothing. The <volume index> decile holds band labels ("Top 10%", "10-20%", through "Bottom
  10%", plus "No Reported Vol"); the top two quartiles are the five bands from "Top 10%" through
  "40-50%", matched as labels. The <our brand 4A> premium line performance identifier holds four
  published values plus blanks, quartiling stores within their own <market> on <volume index>
  opportunity (top half versus bottom half) and <our brand 4A> premium line performance (top
  quartile versus bottom three). Gap terms: high opportunity, low performance ("biggest upside")
  0.50; high opportunity, high performance ("winners") 0.25; low opportunity, high performance
  ("outperform") 0.15; low opportunity, low performance ("low priority") 0.00; blank 0.00. Buckets
  match by full value, never by a "low performance" substring, which merges the top bucket with the
  ignore bucket. A Q store has a top-two-quartile band and the biggest-upside identifier; "Q" is
  displayed when true, the cell left empty when false. It contributes the headquarters-question gap
  term of 0.5 and nothing more; the identifier scores its own 0.5 separately, only for the
  biggest-upside value, per Step 6. A territory yields a handful and a <market> a few hundred; zero
  means the bands were compared as numbers. If either column is absent or empty there is no Q
  marker: the column is blank, neither term is scored, and Method says so.
- Performance indicators: performance or opportunity identifier, assortment satisfaction or percent,
  distribution percent, <volume index> or volume decile, <our brand 2D> priority store, and similar
  phrasings.
- Coverage and recency: last call, visit or contact date, <photo platform> photo or visit date, last
  photo, image date, and similar phrasings. Dates arrive as real dates or text; ISO date, US slash
  date and ISO date-time forms are parsed before any arithmetic, and an unparseable value is
  missing, not old, because subtracting a date from a string produces nothing without an error and a
  coverage flag that never fires looks like a <market> with no gaps.
- Store status: temporarily closed flag, is closed, store status, closed flag, active flag.
- Store type: the chain flag, the <lifestyle store flag>, trade class, channel, store type, class of
  trade, outlet type. Not interchangeable: the lifestyle exclusion resolves by exact column name
  only (the full flag name, then its two short exact variants), per Step 5, never through this
  group, because first-hit-wins would land on the chain flag and remove roughly three times as many
  stores. If none resolves, no exclusion is applied and the assistant says so. No gate catches the
  error, since every lifestyle store in the reference file is also flagged as a chain.
- Contracts match on the stem, accepting any trailing month, year, quarter or cycle token and never
  registering a month-bearing literal: <business unit 1> contract (also <our brand 1A> and
  "agreement" phrasings); <business unit 4> contract (also <our brand 4A>); <business unit 2>
  contract (also <category 2>, <our brand 2A>, <our brand 2B>); <business unit 5> contract (also
  <category 5>); <business unit 3> contract (also <our brand 3A>, <category 3>);
  <digital trade program> contract, tier and allowance (digital contract, digital initiative,
  digital engagement including a recurring misspelling, <allowance type C>, and similar phrasings).
- Program elections (same stem rule): <allowance type B> election and loyalty funded program,
  <our brand 1C> election, multi-pack, <allowance type A> and its election per <business unit>,
  performance-option elections, loyalty, scan and non-scan allowance, and similar phrasings.
- Pricing and promotion: <price support program> alignment, recommendation and election; price,
  pricing or promotional alignment; <promotional price program>; pass-through; deep discount;
  discount cluster; <category 2> cluster.
- Distribution presence is a binary Y/N pair, not a percentage: <our brand 4A> premium line
  distribution is ours; <competitor brand 4A>, <competitor brand 4B> and <competitor brand 4C>
  distribution are the competitors'. Competitor Y with ours N is a distribution gap invisible to
  every assortment percentage, since a store carrying none of our range has no figure to fall below
  a median. The pair is resolved by <business unit>, scored under gap term (b), and both sides are
  shown on Coverage Gaps.
- Pricing pass-through: the <business unit 3> <promotional price program> opportunity column,
  pass-through, and similar phrasings. Values name the shortfall (no or partial pass-through) and
  anything short of full is the gap. These are pricing opportunity columns and are scored; the
  work-type zero for price-validation activities does not apply.
- Headquarters priority store flags (<our brand 2D> priority store, <our brand 4A> premium line
  target store, <business unit 4> high-volume high-velocity store): a flag carried by under 15
  percent of the scope is a headquarters-named priority on the same footing as a kit initiative, at
  source boost 1.5; one carried by more than 15 percent is too broad, recorded in Method and not
  scored (in the reference planner one flag marked about 1 percent of stores and qualified; another
  marked about two-thirds and did not).
- SKU and distribution counts: SKUs, SKU count, items carried, distinct SKUs, distribution, carried
  status, the brand distribution flags, and similar phrasings.
- Competitive: competitive assortment satisfaction, competitor distribution, competitive share.
- Distributor: primary distributor per <business unit>, wholesaler, direct or indirect, third-party
  provider.
- Contact and consent: key decision maker email, decision maker, consent, age verification
  validation.
- Geography: latitude, longitude, county, media market area.
- No trend columns exist; every volume field is a single-period snapshot, and the only change field
  (<business unit 5> contract change versus last cycle) is contract status. Decline comes only from
  comparing two planners.
- Activity flags: every column carrying a numeric value in the count row immediately above the
  header, up to but excluding Grand Total, regardless of originator prefix. A non-empty cell means
  the store is flagged; values are markers, not quantities. The prefix is never the admission test
  (in the reference planner over half the activity columns carried no prefix and held about 97
  percent of the flags). The block is never defined as "everything right of the last mapped concept"
  either, because most activity columns match some data-concept stem (promotional program names,
  pass-through, items carried, SKUs, a <business unit> contract, any two-letter stem). The block is
  anchored on the count row, which carries a number over every activity column and nothing else (its
  values are national and never scored). Fallbacks when the count row is absent: (1) from the first
  column with an originator prefix through the column before Grand Total; (2) with no Grand Total,
  from the first prefixed column to the last column; (3) with no prefixed column, the file has no
  activity block: zero activity columns are recorded, priority and must-close are zero for every
  store, the ranking rests on volume, performance gaps and the steer, and the Read Me and Method say
  so. Method names the anchor used. Activities are never invented, contract or election columns are
  never treated as activities, and the run is never refused.
- The prefix is optional metadata for weighting only: a "<market support role>, headquarters only"
  tag marks headquarters origin, a "<territory role> needed" tag marks required <territory role>
  action, a leading letter followed by a <market id> marks <market> origin, and "Activity", "Local
  Activity" or a local-activity-plan tag marks local. Tags match case-insensitively and tolerate
  whitespace, colon, hyphen or no separator.

Appendix A - Concept Synonym Sets - Extending This Dictionary:

A header that does not resolve is a skill defect, not a user error: the assistant logs the literal
header text, continues with the terms that did resolve, names the unresolved header in Method and
reports it to <author> so the stem joins the list. New entries register the stem, never the full
literal: trailing period tokens (month names, years, quarters, latest-n-week and to-date markers),
trailing rate markers and leading originator prefixes are stripped before storing, so a stem catches
every dated variant. Every stem is kept at four normalized characters or more where possible,
because shorter ones match only exactly.

Appendix A - Degenerate Scopes: Guard Before Dividing:

Every weighting term is a percentile, a median or a ratio with an input that can be empty or
uniform. The guards run before computing:

- Scope has 0 stores after filtering: the run stops and reports the distinct scope values present.
- A qualifier matched no rows: the run stops before ranking and reports the phrase, the column
  tested and the ten nearest values present in that column. It never returns an empty workbook or
  widens silently.
- A qualifier resolved to no column: the run proceeds without that term, labels the report
  unqualified in the Read Me and the opener, and names the dropped phrase.
- Blank, zero or negative volume: included in the ranking and the scope denominator as 0, receiving
  the lowest percentile and ranking last; nothing is dropped for lacking volume, since a store that
  opened this cycle must be visible at the bottom. The count is reported in the Method funnel; the
  usable-volume definition in the scoring formula governs.
- Phantom trailing rows: a record is a row with a non-blank account number, not the sheet's reported
  extent.
- Scope has 1 or 2 stores: nothing special; the average-rank percentile is well defined, and the
  Read Me says plainly that the scope holds one or two stores.
- Every volume 0, blank or identical: every store receives the same percentile, (N plus 1) divided
  by 2N, nothing divides by zero, and the ranking falls to the priority and workload terms; Method
  says so.
- Fewer eligible stores than the list size: every eligible store is delivered, the shortfall stated
  ("Top 10 (only 7 stores in the ranked population)"), and the list never padded.

A median over fewer than 5 stores is not a norm: on the assortment and coverage tabs the figure is
printed with the count beside it and the comparison skipped. A rank, score or benchmark that could
not be computed is left blank with a stated reason; a visibly missing number is recoverable and a
fabricated one is not.

Appendix A - Very Large Planners: What Changes and What Does Not:

A <section role>, a <region role> or an <HQ role> may hand the skill hundreds of thousands of rows.
Nothing about the method changes; only the mechanics of reading and writing do. The run goes to
completion; there is no size at which the answer becomes "this file is too large". Failures
regardless of run time: stopping partway and describing what would have been built; delivering an
empty or missing tab and noting it in the summary; shortening a list, lowering a cap or dropping a
tab; sampling rows, estimating a threshold or extrapolating a percentile from part of the file;
reporting progress instead of a finished file. If a step fails the recovery paths are worked
(re-read after the write lag, re-chunk smaller, rebuild a poisoned sheet from its checkpoint, resume
from the last good stage) and the run continues.

The only acceptable early stops are the five named hard gates: no store identifier; no volume
measure in any tier; an empty scope after the text re-test; a file that cannot be opened; a stage
stuck with no forward progress after the full ladder and one rebuild from checkpoint. Run length,
file size and retry count are never on that list. On a large file the opener says so up front in one
line (row count, scope, and that the build will take a while).

Scale tiers are picked from the record count and change nothing else; the method, nine tabs,
thirteen formatting elements, caps and gates are identical in all four. Values per tier are:
records; read; score; write; typical case.

- T1: up to 5,000; load resolved columns in one pass; in memory, one pass; 50-row chunks; the
  <territory role> and single-<market> case (a 200-store territory and a 2,200-store <market>).
- T2: 5,001 to 50,000; streaming, resolved columns only; in memory, one pass; 50-row chunks; section
  and small region files.
- T3: 50,001 to 250,000; streaming, resolved columns only, projection checkpointed to the session's
  working folder; two passes (pass 1 computes the volume distribution and every median, pass 2
  scores the whole ranked population against it); 50-row chunks; region and national files. Pass 2
  covers the same population as pass 1; the split computes the distribution once, it does not shrink
  the second pass.
- T4: above 250,000; streaming in row blocks, projecting to a columnar scratch file in the session's
  working folder; the same two passes over the scratch projection; 50-row chunks; the largest
  national case, with nothing about the output changed.

The output is bounded by the caps, not the input: 10 plus 25 or 20 plus 50 on the visit tabs and 500
each on Directed, All Other Stores, Assortment Targets and Coverage Gaps, so the largest workbook is
roughly 2,070 store rows in every tier and the build is a fixed amount of writing at any scale. Only
the read and scoring pass grow, both linear and streamable; the caps are never raised to "cover more
of a big file". Never permitted: sampling rows, estimating a threshold, extrapolating a percentile,
lowering a cap, dropping a tab, shortening a list. A percentile over a sample is a different number
wearing the same name.

The two-pass split must not change the percentile denominator. The percentile is defined over the
entire ranked population (every in-scope store after the closed-store and lifestyle exclusions, with
zero, blank and negative volumes included as 0), where "in-scope" means after scope, every qualifier
and any FILTER steer. Pass 1 retains the full sorted volume distribution, or an exact rank index
over it, and carries it into pass 2 in the stage checkpoint; pass 2 looks percentiles up against it
rather than ranking the survivors among themselves, which would shift every percentile upward and
make a T3 run rank the same stores differently from a T1 run. A store's percentile is identical
whichever tier processed it, verified by re-reading three stores at random after scoring and
confirming their percentiles against a single-pass computation over the full population. Two passes
over the whole file is not sampling; both read every row.

Checkpointing grows more frequent as the tier rises: T3 and T4 checkpoint after every 25,000 records
read and after every scoring block, so a failure at record 210,000 resumes at 200,000, and the
checkpoint carries the pass 1 distribution so a resumed run never rebuilds it differently. At T1 and
T2 the sheet is read in streaming mode in a single pass, keeping only the resolved columns; header
detection and sheet selection read the first rows only. Every threshold (volume percentile, scope
median, top-third assortment floor, every median and peer norm) is computed with a code tool over
the whole in-scope population in one pass. The real figures are reported: rows in file, records with
an account number, rows in scope, rows after the lifestyle exclusion, and how many fell below each
cap. A long run is not a failing run.

Appendix A - Resuming a Broken Run:

Checkpoints exist so a failed run never restarts from zero. On any re-entry the assistant reads the
stage checkpoint files in the session's working folder and resumes at the first stage whose
checkpoint is missing or whose invariants no longer hold against the current artifacts.

- Re-verify, do not assume: a checkpoint proves a stage once passed, not that the artifact still
  matches, so its invariants are re-checked against the file first.
- A stale checkpoint is worse than none: if the planner file changed (different name, size or row
  count than the recorded inputs), every downstream checkpoint is discarded and the run rebuilds
  from the parse stage onward. A workbook assembled half from last month's numbers looks complete
  and is the worst artifact the skill can produce.
- The run never resumes past a gate that has not passed.

Appendix A - When a Sheet Becomes Unwritable:

A sheet can enter a state where every write is acknowledged and lands nothing, most often after data
writes and structural operations were mixed in one call. It cannot be repaired by rewriting.

1. The assistant confirms it is real: it waits 30 seconds, re-reads, and confirms two consecutive
   writes landed nothing, never diagnosing off a single empty read.
2. It deletes the sheet, recreates it, and rewrites from the stage checkpoint, which is why the
   checkpoint holds the rows rather than only a count.
3. It writes data first, structure second, in separate calls, always.
4. If a recreated sheet fails the same way twice, it stops and reports; it never deletes a third
   time.

Appendix A - Failure Handling:

Each condition below names what the assistant does.

- Territory or <market> column unresolvable: tries the next level up (<market>, section, region)
  before stopping; if nothing resolves, reports the headers seen and asks.
- No volume column in any tier: stops and reports the available numeric columns; volume is the one
  term the method cannot run without.
- The target cycle's <monthly POS kit> not found: falls back to the most recently published kit,
  keeps building, and states in the opener and Method which month it covers. It never stops or asks
  the user to fetch it.
- The kit library unreachable: builds on supervisor priorities alone, labels the workbook as built
  without headquarters input, and names what could not be reached.
- A required column unresolvable from header or values: asks once, in one message, with header text,
  example values and candidates. Optional columns never earn a question: the feature is skipped,
  named in Method, and the run continues.
- A header that does not resolve: logs the literal text, continues with what resolved, names it in
  Method, and reports it so its stem can be added.
- Scope filter yields 0 rows: re-tests the comparison as text on both sides and re-tests the level
  reading; only then reports the distinct values present at every scope level in the file.
- A qualifier yields 0 rows: re-tests with normalized matching (case-folded, punctuation stripped)
  and against the alternate chain column and any parent-company spelling; only then reports the miss
  with the nearest present values. It never delivers an empty workbook or widens to the unqualified
  population.
- Two planners with incompatible schemas: reports the mismatch and compares only shared metrics.
- Duplicate account numbers: keeps the highest-volume row as the base and merges the survivors'
  signals into it: an activity flagged on any duplicate flags the merged row, a directed match on
  any row directs it, and a populated contract, chain name or address fills a blank on the base. The
  collapse and the merged-row count are noted in Method; keeping only the highest-volume row without
  merging silently drops flags and directives.
- Multiple candidate sheets: picks by resolvable columns and names the chosen sheet in Method.
- No total volume column, only per-<business unit>: builds the composite percentile, labels it in
  Method, and never sums raw figures.
- No supervisor in the directory: not an error; skips supervisor priorities, notes it, and ranks on
  headquarters alone.
- Role title matches no listed pattern: runs the geography-word test first (a title naming a region,
  division, section or the nation is Line B regardless of the rest); only then infers from the
  widest scope column the planner populates (one territory present means Line A sizing; one or many
  <markets>, sections or regions means Line B). It asks only when the file cannot settle it, and
  never demotes a senior title to a narrower scope than the file supports.

Author and Support - Tuning It to Your State or Market:

The skill is built to be calibrated, not just used. Its scoring reads the file it is given: the
assortment top-third floor, peer norms, call-cycle thresholds and penetration benchmarks are
computed from the user's own scope at run time, so it adapts to a territory of 200 stores or a
<market> of 2,200 without being told. What it cannot infer is local commercial reality: pricing
regimes, loyalty program availability, which banners the organization calls on a cycle versus as
needed, and how leadership ranks competing initiatives. Those are left as inputs, because a wrong
assumption stated confidently is worse than a stated gap. When the output does not match how the
business runs (a benchmark wrong for the <jurisdiction>, a chain treated as callable that the team
covers only on request, a category weighted against the user's priorities, a planner whose column
names were not recognized), the calibration belongs in the skill rather than in a monthly
workaround. Feedback and tuning requests go to <author> at <author email>. Worth raising, each a
small change: a column that did not resolve (the user sends the header text; one stem addition
usually covers every variant nationwide); a local rule the skill does not know (coverage
conventions, program availability, anything that makes a national default wrong locally); a ranking
that reads wrong to someone who knows the territory (the scoring is transparent, with the formula
and a worked example in every workbook, so a disagreement traces to a specific term); a planner
format it choked on, or a role or scope it did not resolve. Every workbook carries the same
invitation and address, so a rep who opens the file months later knows who to ask and what is
adjustable.
